# Falsified directions: MT and streaming

Every entry here is backed by A/B data. If the premises change, such as the table structure, window, corpus or CPU, re-evaluation is allowed, but new decomposition evidence must come first. The differential-testing discipline is in [benchmarking methodology](../bench/methodology.md), and the rules are in the [index](../negative.md).

## Job model

### Frame-per-job MT encoding

Frame-per-job MT encoding was rejected at design time. Multi-frame output burdens the decode side and is unfriendly to single-frame consumers. Stage-2 single-frame overlap jobs are the right answer.

### Overlap without strip prefill

Enlarging overlap alone, without strip prefill, is ineffective. The insertion paths only insert job-local positions, so the strips never get indexed, and enlarging overlap is mathematically ineffective.

### Fixed-grid prefill for periodic repeats

Fixed-grid prefill to find periodic repeats is mathematically unsolvable. Clustered 5-grams recursively bury twin slots with equal values and phase-alignment traps, and dfast and chain suffer the same burial. Seeding at the job start is mandatory.

### Pledge without a job-size cap

A huge pledge without a `job_size` cap causes a memory blowup. `MAX_JOB_SIZE` is 1 GiB, the same cap as zstdmt.

### Thread-local pool across scope bursts

A `thread_local` pool across `thread::scope` bursts always misses, because the scope threads are fresh each time. The pool must be owned by the encoder and carried across bursts.

### Hint-seeded growing grid

Seeding the Growing grid's first epoch at the bulk formula's value for a declared length hint was measured on 2026-11-14 through the `enc-feed` caller shape (128 KiB continues, w16, hint = payload length), sweeping seed sizes inside one binary through scratch env knobs to exclude layout noise. At the 100 MiB scale the cells split both ways (mixed fastest faster, dll100 fastest 18-25% slower at 0.5-0.7% denser bytes); at the 4x field scale (a 400 MiB ELF payload plus a scaled mixed payload) the bulk-formula seed regressed all four light cells against the unseeded floor — mixed fastest x1.389 to x1.986, mixed fast x1.236 to x1.556, ELF fastest x1.716 to x2.373, ELF fast x1.286 to x1.494 — so the direction as specified is refuted. Smaller seeds are non-monotonic: a Fixed/4 seed (3.125 MiB) beat the floor on the cells it completed while a 4 MiB cap sat at or above it, but the 400 MiB payload divides exactly into 128 Fixed/4 jobs, so the finish tail — the unposted remainder at `ZSTD_e_end` — is zero there and exposed at 4 MiB, and the apparent winner is partly an exact-tiling artifact; three of that arm's four cell runs were also killed mid-run by memory pressure and never completed. The surviving hypothesis is that the finish tail's fraction of the job size, not the job size itself, decides, so the open form is a finish-tail-aware split (split the unposted remainder across workers when the remaining span is a small multiple of the job size), never a blanket seed. Measured the same day and closed: the tail rule already re-slices the remainder across the workers and that slice is wall-optimal under the per-job strip cost (see the slice-floor entry under Finish tail).

### Light-row Growing floor at the field shape

Raising the light rows' Growing floor toward libzstd's fixed-job scale (a level-keyed floor of 4-8 MiB for the Fast and Dfast rows, leaving the deep rows' overlap-keyed geometry alone) was the candidate lever for the light rows' field gap after R109 excluded the far machinery. Measured 2026-11-15 and falsified without landing: the wall the floor would sit inside is caller-thread-bound, so the schedule change has nothing to buy. The evidence, on game-mid (308 MiB) and game-big (728 MiB) under the enc-feed shape at w16/w8 with `jobdecomp --hint` timelines: ours reads 1034/1082 MB/s at l1 and 918/965 at l3 across the two scales while the cap-size byte share rises from zero (no cap-size job ever runs at 308 MiB: l1's epochs cover [0,240) at 1/2/4/8 MiB and the tail re-slices [240,308) into 4.25 MiB jobs) to about a third, bounding the whole ramp-plus-boundary-plus-tail effect at 5.1 percent of wall; the worker pool is 90 percent idle (summed job time is a tenth of wall, and the pool width moves the wall by less than the measurement spread between w8 and w16 while libzstd's own cells move 40 percent); and the wall decomposes into three caller-thread byte spans — write loop 42 percent, core finish 24 percent, final sink drain 33 percent at l1 on game-big — none of which a job-grid floor touches. The per-job boundary tax itself is real but sits behind the same wall: 0.69 ms fixed of 6.30 ms job time at l1 and 3.70 of 8.28 at l3 on game-mid (the 2 MiB strip prefill), 1-5 percent of wall once summed job time is spread over the idle pool. A floor raise would also move bytes on every unpledged Growing light-row stream (the enc-stream open cells whose screen rejects), a cost with no wall to buy on the field class. The lever the attribution names instead is the caller-thread delivery budget (see [ffi-stream-gap](../bench/ffi-stream-gap.md)). Nothing landed.

### Entropy-table carry across job boundaries

Carrying each job's final entropy tables to the next job's first block, so its Repeat_Mode pricing matches a serial encoder's at the boundary, was measured on 2026-11-20 through a calibration latch on branch `r118-b` (commit 22438078, env `ZSTDX_ENTROPY_CARRY`): per-boundary publication cells receive the job-end decoder state (the three FSE previous slots plus the literals' Huffman table, exactly what the block loop rolls into `*_previous`), the next job installs them after its stock prepare, and the latch serializes a job's start on its predecessor's finish so armed runs price the byte axis at the stock job geometry. dll400 l9 w16 bulk (25 jobs of 16 MiB, 24 boundaries) read stock 74,283,460 B against armed 74,283,464 B, +4 B on the row's 0.5 percent bar. The mechanism engaged: 11 of 8152 blocks moved, concentrated in the two boundary clusters whose first block took Repeat and perturbed the following blocks' own table chains with mixed-sign deltas netting the +4 B, while the other 22 boundaries are byte-identical — `choose_tables_fast` priced the carried tables worse than a fresh description. Both frames decode byte-exact through the libzstd CLI, and the armed bytes are run-deterministic across repeats. The reading closes the row's premise: the boundary re-description is priced at about zero by the block coder's own per-block market, the same market that makes Repeat common mid-job, because at a multi-MiB job distance the predecessor's tables fit the new job's first blocks worse than their description costs. The all-boundaries-engaged ceiling (every boundary dropping its full huff and FSE descriptions, a few hundred bytes each) stays under 0.1 percent at the field's roughly 48 boundaries per 400 MiB, so the field's smaller job geometry cannot rescue the row. The pipelined entropy stage behind the in-order assembly was never built — the R118 span probe had cleared its serial-handoff kill condition by an order of magnitude, but the density payoff the row existed for is empty. The field density deficit re-attributes to the parse-side state the jobs restart: the repcode gate at job starts, the strip-bounded match reach, and the per-job split-savings restart.

## Job strip and LDM state

### Far-dead strip-cap screens

Two screens were tried alone in 2026-09-18: the head engagement screen `ldm_head_parses`, where a narrow alphabet means cap, and the far-lag repeat probe alone with a count bar. The head screen alone caps the corpus text and json fine with identical bytes, because their far leakage is shadowed by near-period repeats. It also kills any far-tiled stream whose repeats start past the head screens: text.raw x19 with a 32 MiB period and the head taken from text.raw's own data moved from 125,724 to 1,637,683 B, 13x larger, because the cap removed exactly LDM's beyond-reach twins. The far-repeat probe alone protects the tiled stream and refuses to cap the corpus text and json, because their collision leakage at a lag of at least 4 MiB clears any count bar at 199 and 362 strided hits, so the strip-bound cells keep their 470 MiB/s with no win.

The landed screen is the AND of both. The head screen runs first and kills the shadowed near-periodic classes, then far-repeat dominance runs with a bar of 1/12 of samples, above the 4.4% leakage ceiling and below any paying class. A head-prefix screen can never see repeats at lags beyond its own span, so the span must be the one the strips actually index. Repeats existing does not mean the far class pays, because shadowed repeats with a nearer twin within reach leak far-lag hits without ever winning a parse.

### Mixed-head far demotion (the paying-ness verdict)

The verdict arm of todo 33 was measured on 2026-11-15 on game-mid, a synthetic game-like solid block of 308 MiB: an 8 MiB head of four runtime-sharing executables (dll100 slices) so both presence screens accept, then ten 30 MiB tiles of 85 percent seeded urandom, 2 MiB unique text and 0.73 percent far-lag duplicated engine files each. The demotion was forced through a temporary env override on `resolve_far_class` and `resolve_fast_capture` inside one binary, interleaved against libzstd under the enc-feed caller shape at w16 and the no-hint Read stream at mt8.

The trade is real at the chain row. game-mid balanced feed-mt16 reads 330 MB/s stock against 469 forced, a +42 percent wall win, for +895,386 B of +0.306 percent size (292,419,571 to 293,314,957 B, libzstd at 293,257,606, so the armed frame's 0.29 percent density lead over libzstd becomes parity). jobdecomp w16 puts the stock prefill at 77.1 percent of summed job time, the LDM ingest alone at 36.7 percent, chain grid 20.6, gate 10.7 and seed scan 9.1, while the forced arm zeroes ldm and drops gate and seed to about 2 percent each, its remaining 55.8 percent prefill share being the 4 MiB reach strip's own grid. Demoted the frame still reads x2.47 behind libzstd (469 against 1155 MB/s in the same rounds): the far machinery is 139 of the stock cell's 841 MB/s gap on this class.

The light rows carry no far machinery at the field shape at all. A hint at or past the 64 MiB full band sits outside `fast_row_screen_pending`'s mid band, so the enc-feed cells run `FastCapture::Stock`, the source clamp holds the l1 and l3 windows at 1 to 2 MiB and the `Job` arming's mid-size bar disarms LDM outright: the forced runs are byte-identical and wall-flat at both levels (296,607,129 and 293,822,679 B on both arms). The light-row far tax lives only in the no-hint streaming shape, where the open screen accepts and the capture engages (jobdecomp shows the 23-job capture lattice at w8): game-mid fastest stream-mt8 reads 894 stock against 1854 to 1896 forced, +107 percent wall, for +1.409 percent size, and fast reads 851 against 1864, +119 percent, for +0.440 percent. At fastest the armed capture holds a 1.3 percent density lead over libzstd (292.7 against 296.6 MB): the far class pays there, and demotion loses more density than the whole field complaint.

No keyable verdict form exists in this evidence. A prefix-keyed paying bar is defeated by construction on exactly this corpus class, a twin-dense head over a far-barren body (twinspect: job 0 carries 95,309 twins, the body jobs 2,485), the same construction that killed the ramp's frame-head screen ("a pre-parse screen can never see content behind its own job's strip", [shapes](../bench/shapes.md)). The honest statistics leave only 2-3x bar margins: gear far-twin trigger mass over a 64 MiB prefix reads 1.03 percent on game-mid against 6.84 on dll100 (whole-frame twin mass 5.4 against 47.4 percent, and the strided 8-gram statistic of `far_repeat_dominant` itself reads 0.4 against 3.1 percent), a gap calibrated against one synthetic payload and one dll store snapshot with the field corpora's position unmeasured, while the size cost of a misfired demotion scales with the same statistic the bar reads. Job-zero keying serializes the first epoch (the growing grid posts a whole 192 MiB epoch at the first post, so jobs 1 through 15 start before job 0's parse exists) and per-job keying pays a strip-scale scan per job, the cost it removes. Reopening needs a non-prefix evidence source, far-win density data from the real field corpora, or an ingest-side quiet latch whose cross-job keying does not serialize the burst. Nothing landed.

### Chain head origin in the u32 domain

The chain head origin in the u32 entry domain was measured on 2026-09-23, with `dist = (pos + origin + 1) - entry` mod 2^32 and an explicit empty-sentinel reject, plus a never-zero-links companion where link slots store their own position pack when the threaded head is empty, making every link read check-free. gungraun Ir read json.balanced +3.94% and text +3.68%, with json.balanced bulk-ST wall at -5% and every paired round slower. The links form alone, on top of the checked head read, read json +12.24% and text +14.86%. The write-side select per insert dwarfs the saved per-link compare, because inserts run per position and the saved compares per walk step do not outnumber them until searches run multi-step per position, and the select also broke the insert block's scheduling.

The landed form resolves in the u64 domain, with `q = pos + origin + 1` and `dist = q - entry`, one subtraction per read and one fewer than the origin-free form's sub and add pair. The origin is capped at 2^30 by a real clear once per about 1 GiB, and since the 3.5-GiB negated-floor epoch roll it uses the same no-check property. Live entries stay below 2^32, and the empty sentinel dies by arithmetic, since `q >= pos+1 > reach`, with no check anywhere. json and text Ir read +0.87/+1.10% with walls at parity. The entry-domain choice is itself a hot-loop lever: a modulus that forces an explicit sentinel check, or a write-side branch to keep sentinels out of links, costs 4-12% Ir on walk-heavy shapes, while the u64 domain plus an amortized cap restores the origin-free per-step cost exactly.

### One-bit parity tag

The idea was a 1-bit parity tag replacing the opt tables' epoch, using bit 31 of a u32 entry flipped at every reset point, the u64 form's 16-bit epoch compressed to O(1) invalidation with no clears. All corpus gates passed, including the dump, the 120 cells and dll at st and mt8, but dll32 l16 mt4 grew +332 B deterministically on both builds, while st and mt8 of the same file were identical. The bulk-MT job path resets each pooled state twice, because `take_slice_state` and `run_job_with` each call `reset_slice_state`. The monotone epoch shrugs this off, but a 2-period tag neutralizes it: consecutive jobs on one thread_local state share parity, and the earlier job's entries revive wherever it ran within the later job's window band. The geometry was dll32 l16's 8 MiB jobs over a 4 MiB tree domain, while the corpus cells' same-state job pairs sit window-far apart and mt8 ran one job per state. The revived entries were strip-tail fill-skip interiors whose head slots the tail-half tree fill never rewrites.

A width-2 invalidation domain cannot back a pooled state's unbounded lifetime. The landed form advances a per-state monotone origin instead, with `opt_origin += last_pos + 1` per reset and `+= block_end + 1` at ultra's pass 1. Stale entries rebuild as `cand = q - advance < 0` and die below the window floor, double resets only add extra advance, and output stays independent of the origin value. Any tag narrower than the state's reset count needs the reset count bounded or the tag monotone.

Halving the opt rows' MT job strip for both the tree and LDM, with `strip_for_*` at reach/2 for parity with C zstdmt's btopt overlapLog 8, grew dll100 opt mt8 from 20,391,886 to 21,234,397, or +4.13%. dll32 opt mt8 read -0.13% and dll16 -0.27%, which is denser, and corpus json.opt.bulk-mt read -0.17% through job re-split interplay, while json.opt.stream-mt read +0.13%, text and skewed at most +0.021%, and st stayed byte-identical, measured on 2026-09-16. The [4,8) MiB band of job-start history is load-bearing exactly where the far class lives, which for dll100 is the mass twins and LDM is the server there, so shrinking the strip cut LDM's ingestion too. The decomposition's strip-fill cost of 40-73% of job time is attacked through the tree fill only, the tail-half bound in `prefill_job_strip`, leaving the LDM strip whole, as recorded in [mt-stream](../perf/mt-stream.md).

### Sorted strip fill for the btlazy rows

The R89 map's sorted strip fill was measured on 2026-11-02 and falsified. The btlazy strip prefill ran through the finder's own sorted insert, the `insert_bt1` form C's `ZSTD_updateTree` prefix loads pay: head write first, budgeted descent from the previous head with the commonLength carry, the >384 B interior skip and the `1 << searchLog` budget. It engaged on the strip grid alone, cleared the DUBT heads eagerly, skipped the first block's deferred clear, and kept uniform strips and dictionary loads on the lazy O(1) fill.

The size ledger moved the right way and the walls collapsed. dll100 best bulk-mt8 moved 20,515,629 to 20,506,118 B and dll32 best 4,242,823 to 4,242,440, the pledged stream cells stayed byte-identical to bulk on both builds, and the corpus best-tier mt cells stayed inside +146 B on json and -4 B on skewed. The interleaved clean-build A/B read dll100 best bulk-mt8 at 1895-1935 ms against 3898-5251 with the fill, its pledged stream cell at 1932-1963 against 3896-3949, and dll32 best bulk-mt8 at 367-465 against 717-729. The json clause the proposal named fired exactly: json best bulk-mt moved 1140.24 to 2279.73 ms mean under the trace build, the strip fill at 44-49% of job time at 2-3 MiB/s, for +146 B.

The compare volume moved into the fill as designed and cost more there. dll100's `dubt cmp` read 138.07M calls at 72.96 GiB on the base build against 234.11M at 96.62 GiB with the sorted fill, an added 24 GiB over 24 MiB of strips, a kilobyte compared per filled byte, while parse+emit collapsed from 10,669 to 449 ms of job sum and the six 4 MiB fills alone ran 9.2 s of traced span at 3 MiB/s. The structural difference is the amortization point: the batch sort pays one budgeted descent per walked node of the backlog a search actually reaches, while the eager insert pays the whole budget at every strip position, and the DUBT head chain hands each descent the nearest twin first, so the carry trims bytes only inside one descent. The >384 B skip never collapses a dll strip because the long twins run between the strip and the job's own bytes, which only the parse ever sees, while the bucket-local matches inside the strip stay short. The opt rows' eager fills carry the same per-position price at 40% of their job time, and against that yardstick the btlazy batch sort is the cheaper sorter on every measured class.

The fill also inherits the falsified fill-overlap shape: it sits on every job's critical path from t=0 ahead of its own parse, so the bulk timeline's per-job walls widened from 0.97-2.51 s to 2.59-5.55 s instead of the whale spread narrowing. Reopening needs a fill whose per-position cost undercuts the batch sort's per-search amortization, an insert budget of its own or the parse-coupled admission of strip positions, which is the R89 map's second proposal's family.

### Interleaved strip admission for the opt rows

The R89 map's parse-coupled strip fill was implemented in its literal form on 2026-11-03: `update_tree` admits the strip in 64 KiB blocks with the insert-side counts capped at the block end (so a twin's skip covers its whole interior in one insert), the searches' match-end skips stand down below the admission ceiling, and the parse interleaves with the admission. Two independent breaks falsify the interleaving.

The searches pay the fill's saving back. dll100 ultra bulk-mt8 read the summed strip-fill span down by about a quarter, 81.9-97.6 to 73.0 s traced, while the tree-search compare volume rose from 12.44 to 97.92 GiB at an equal 1.33 G calls: every admission block's first descents start from heads whose chains the prior blocks left un-carried, so each block re-pays the prefix-carry setup the one-sweep fill amortizes across the whole strip. This is the R88 windowed-LDM cold-tree class on the search side instead of the insert side.

The exactness edge is a tree contract. A search always inserts the position it searched, so during the admission the parse's own positions enter the tree while the frontier still sits below the ceiling; once the frontier crosses, the stock sweep re-inserts every parse-inserted position whose span it still owes, and a re-insert overwrites the node's link pair with fresh EMPTY terminators, orphaning the subtrees other nodes' links name. Measured as deterministic stream-mt corruption: dll100 ultra w8's first wrong byte at 95,942,242, a 4 KB span that decodes byte-exact through the libzstd CLI with the checksum off, absent at w4 and on the bulk path, and silent under the poison walls because the break is semantic. The parse's insert set is a function of its own emissions, which do not exist before the parse runs, so a fill that both precedes the parse and reproduces its set can only be the eager sweep or a density restriction of it.

The premise's density asymmetry also fails to transfer. ST's parse-coupled fill is sparse through the insert-side skip, whose arithmetic (`best_len - 384`, `match_end - pos - 8`) requires the winning candidate's length above its own offset. dll's strip twins are far-offset and LDM-served, where no insert can clear that bar, so the eager strip fill already sits at the parse's own density on exactly the whale content that sets the wall. The 35x fill-tax figure compares summed wall spans, and the honest density gap is the mt searches' 2.0x compare volume at equal per-call length, already recorded in the R88 decomposition.

The exactness-safe extraction of the map's premise, an admission density floor that finishes the sweep before the parse's own inserts begin, landed in [mt-stream](../perf/mt-stream.md)'s R91 section.

### Widened window adoption under the opt feed

The serial feed's first form, measured on 2026-11-03, adopted the row window as each opt job's match window, the falsified windowed form's geometry, with the feed publishing the candidates ahead of the jobs. The mechanism is the ultra seed parse's rewind inside `opt::run_block`. The feed's candidate class needs none of those bytes, because the opt parse prices the stored span that generation verified over the whole input. The landed form keeps the stock strip window and reads 17.82 MB on dll100 ultra at the stock wall with the traced fills and compares at stock levels; the 1.17 MB between the two forms is the far tree candidate class, which a shallow job tree buys only through these fills. Reopening the widened form needs the seed rewind bounded to the strip base and the far window's indexing paid somewhere cheaper than the insert descent.

R95 (2026-11-04) reopened the geometry with both levers and split the verdict: the long-compare kernel alone did not rescue it — the stride-4 form reborn on the SIMD compare read dll100 ultra bulk-mt8 at 6550 ms against 3715, because the crossing's cost is per-insert latency, with the traced fill down 179.5 to 71.0 s as the compare share alone — while a far-window admission tier at one position per 256 B carried the same class at a flat wall (the stride curve and the landed numbers are in [mt-stream](../perf/mt-stream.md)'s R95 section). The eager-insert descent at the strip density stays falsified; the far window indexes through the sparse tier.

### Job-start seed probes against a stale index
The job-start seed and LDM probes compared candidates against a stale scan index, which caused unpledged row-9 stream-MT frames to corrupt deterministically past 32 MiB on 2026-09-17. text.raw x1.5, x2 and x3 corrupted at w2, w4 and w8, and stayed clean at w1 and at l3, l13 and l17. The rep probe may advance `pos` one byte past the iteration's `idx`, and a seed or LDM offset of exactly that advance resolves the candidate onto `idx` itself. `read4` and the extension then trivially match the position against itself, and the store gate prices the zero offset with `ilog2(0)`, with a release backtrace straight into `pays_for_offset_lit`.

The panic rode two amplifier defects. The stream's `drain_all` returned with the poison unsurfaced, because aborted jobs assemble empty and drain like completions, so the frame shipped missing whole jobs. It decoded short by exactly the poisoned job's span, with the first wrong byte at a block start as later far matches read the shifted history. The bulk pool's workers all exit on poison, leaving unclaimed slots forever `None` and deadlocking the ordered-assembly wait, which produced a 14-minute futex hang with every worker thread gone.

Both layers are fixed. The probes take strictly older candidates only, with `ci < idx`, which is byte-neutral by construction because a self-compare either panicked or never updated best. The full-ladder dump and 5 shapes by 6 tiers of emitframe stream-mt8 plus mt2, mt4 and bulk-mt8 stayed byte-identical against the extension. `drain_all` surfaces the poison once the pending queue drains, and the bulk assembly fills unclaimed slots under poison so the panic resumes instead of hanging. The w1-versus-w2 asymmetry comes from the repcodes: a carried worker state's repcodes are job-tail values that do not trivially match a period-1 run, while a fresh state's format-default `rep[0]=1` does, so parallel jobs on fresh states are the corrupting geometry. The regression is `strip_tail_period_run_roundtrips`, with a 384 KiB word-run head, zeros and a repeat at w2 and w8, and the generator is fixed so the anchor alignment reproduces.

### CDC far-match index

A content-defined-chunk index was probed on 2026-11-01 as the alternative sharing structure for the fast rows' open capture lattice, the R83 answer to the prefix-chain falsification above. The probe reimplements the engine's LDM fill and generate standalone, with the engine's GEAR_TAB, constants, bucket geometry, batch staging and 16-ahead insert prefetch, because `LdmState` is private to `encoding`. The round's 0.5x fill bar fails.

Coverage fails with it. Over dll100 at the W26 window, with anchored feeds and backward extension on both sides, the LDM feed covers 0.3406 of the input in far matches of at least 1 KiB at offsets past 2 MiB, 0.3022 past 4 MiB, and sums 37.7 MiB of far length. ## Streaming buffer and pump

### Ring-shaped accumulate buffer (the write-loop input budget)

Direction 9's open arm held that the caller thread pays input-copy volume beyond the single chunk copy the borrowed-window falsification fixed as the floor: per-epoch `make_space` recycles of the live tail, growth reallocs, and page-fault churn on freshly exposed tails, which a ring-shaped accumulate buffer would delete by skipping the dead prefix. Measured 2026-11-18 with the `jobdecomp --hint` write-loop counters (whole `write` spans, chunk-copy spans and volumes, recycle and grow spans and volumes, quiescence waits, landed behind `job_trace` the same day) on textdom (160 MiB, output 33 percent) and game-mid (308 MiB, output 94 percent) at l1/l3 x w8/w16: the extra volume is empty in steady state. A decided open stream holds the 256 MiB cap buffer, so textdom never wraps at all — zero recycles, zero grows, zero quiescence waits, and the write span equals the chunk-copy span plus about 2 ms of posting per stream. game-mid crosses the cap once: exactly one recycle per stream (17 MiB of live tail at l1 w16, 34 MiB at l3, 9 MiB at w8) costing 0.5 to 3.2 ms against 170 to 205 ms walls, at most 1.6 percent, because the first wrap frees about 239 MiB of dead prefix and the remaining 52 MiB of stream fits inside it. A 728 MiB stream wraps about three times on the same arithmetic, still around 1 percent of its wall.

Growth belongs to the first stream on a thread alone, before `BUF_POOL` carries the cap buffer: the one visible grow moved 25.5 MiB in 0.49 ms, a 52 GB/s rate no memcpy reaches, and strace over a four-stream process reads 26 mremap calls totalling 1.24 ms including the decode side — glibc serves the accumulate buffer's doublings by moving PTEs, so the realloc arm copies nothing. Page faults stay flat at 235k per process across four streams plus roundtrips. The first stream also pays one growth-gated quiescence wait behind its in-flight jobs (35 ms at textdom l1 w8, zero at w16), which overlapped the jobs' own encode and moved that iteration's wall by about 10 ms of 96. The steady-state lever is therefore dead under the verdict bar by an order of magnitude. The write loop's whole input cost is the accepted chunk copy — 36 of 38 ms of write span at game-mid l1, 30 of 33 at textdom l1 — and the light cells' remaining caller budget sits in the delivery-to-sink byte volume and the finish span, the surfaces the segment queue already bounded. Reopening needs a shape that recycles per epoch: an epoch-sized buffer under a stream of many epoch multiples would pay the live-tail copy once per epoch, but the decided reserve is the cap by design and the pooled cap buffer already amortizes every first-touch cost, so the premise needs that reserve policy to change first, with the process-per-stream cold arm (the one growth-gated wait) priced alongside.

### Side-thread pump pipelining

### Output-capacity promises at post time

Mirroring the worker's own out sizing at the posting site, `span + 3 B per 1 KiB block` reserved into the caller's output Vec per post, was measured on 2026-11-04 and falsified. The mechanism diagnosis was right: the finish drain's delivery blockade stacks the blocked jobs' output concurrently, and the stack crossed the Vec's capacity at the last assembly (dll100 fastest's tail paid a 13.9 MB realloc copy after the straggler). The reserve fails on the promise arithmetic instead. A per-post `reserve` forgets earlier promises whenever delivery drains the length, so a watermark variant was measured too, and both regress the compressible corpus cells: json.fastest stream-mt8 lost 12% solo and the locked matrix read json -36% and text -63% over five invocations per side, because the raw worst-case bound over-promises 5 to 100 times the real emit ratio on these shapes and every growth step pays a copy of the live undelivered length. The stepped finish drain (see [mt-stream](../perf/mt-stream.md)) repairs the tail through scheduling alone: delivery during the straggler wait keeps the undelivered length near zero and the crossing never happens. A learned emit-ratio promise could tighten a reserve further, but the corpus cells' run-to-run bimodality, 7.4 to 12.2 GB/s on text.fastest solo, cannot resolve the single-percent effects it would trade, and it stays unbuilt.


### Split-first FFI delivery

The 7c4c86da write-through round kept the whole-span spill for a burst the caller's slice cannot take, after a split-first variant (always take the slice's worth direct, spill the remainder) measured x0.99 at w8. The mechanism, settled 2026-11-15 with the assembly delivery queue's landing: both variants move the same byte volume. The whole-span spill copies the whole pending span P into the sink and serves the caller off it; split-first copies the slice R direct and the remaining P-R into the sink. The sink pipeline receives P bytes either way and the slice serves off the sink follow identically, so the measured delta was granularity and placement noise on a worker-bound cell, where the delivery copies hide behind the encode. The spill also carried a byte obligation: the single-threaded core's closing shape reads the delivery cadence (the in-place last-block patch keys on `out_read`), so the FFI pins that core's output empty between calls, and a split that left bytes pending in it would move its closing bytes. The queue deletes the volume instead of re-slicing it — the burst's own buffer becomes the delivery buffer and one copy per byte vanishes outright — and it is MT-only, where the schedule is a pure function of absolute offsets and no frame byte reads delivery state, so the spill's cadence obligation never applied there. The landed numbers are in [ffi-stream-gap](../bench/ffi-stream-gap.md)'s delivery-budget section.

### Parallel pump copies

Parallel pump copies through the opt-in reader, the map's proposal 4, were probed on 2026-11-05 before any code landed. The premise was that the loaded single-stream copy rate is latency-bound and two or three copy threads reach the aggregate the memory system leaves under the workers. The probe falsifies the premise in both arms.

The real-cell arm drove the dll100 fastest stream-mt8 cell through the public Read-path encoder with probe-local reader impls, the helpers on scope threads off the encode pool (the shape the fast-row prefix-chain falsification's starvation clause demands), position-balanced over 9 and 21 interleaved rounds: the temporal libc reader read 989.5 MiB/s medians at one copy thread and 979.5 at two, behind the single thread; composed with the NT kernel, one thread read 1012.3-1018.4, two threads 1008.5-1014.9 and three 1010.8 — flat to slightly behind on every cell, whole-cell walls 98.2 against 98.5-99.0 ms. The synthetic arm agrees and names the mechanism: the NT kernel's single-stream rate is 20.2-20.9 GiB/s loaded and 20.5 unloaded, identical within noise, so the copy is not latency-bound and the workers' traffic does not contend with it — an NT store bypasses the cache hierarchy on the write side and the source streams once. Two NT copy threads read 16.3 loaded and 15.3 unloaded, worse than the single thread even with no encode load, because the 1 MiB spans split into halves too small to hide the handoff (a scope-spawn pair costs 32 us measured, about the half-copy span itself) and the streams interleave their WC flushes on the same destination pages. A `prefetchnta` source variant read 1015.9 against 1018.4, flat. Reopening needs a span shape whose single-stream rate sits below the machine's per-stream ceiling — the 1 MiB pump pull does not — or a handoff cheaper than the copy time it splits, and the whole-cell flatness at two and three threads bounds the recoverable share under one percent of the wall.

### Poison drain spin on queued jobs

Observed twice in debug runs on 2026-11-15 and not reproduced in six reruns: when a worker panics while another posted job sits unclaimed in the queue, `drain_all` spins `wait_progress` hot forever. The workers stop claiming under the poison flag (by design, `serve_queue` returns without a claim), the unclaimed job holds the incomplete count, and `surface_poison` — the only site that aborts queued jobs and clears the queue — runs at quiescence or empty pending, which that count prevents. The routing landed on 2026-11-18: the wait helpers (`wait_progress`, `wait_donation`) return a `WaitOutcome` whose `Poisoned` arm runs `surface_poison` at the `drain_all` and `finish_step` loops and at `resolve_probe`'s donation waits (a queued donation is the same hostage a drain's queued job is). `make_space` already polls the flag at its own loop head, and `surface_poison`'s own wait-out loop stays bounded if a second worker panics while the in-flight jobs run out, since every panicking worker still decrements its own count. A watchdog test builds the panicking worker's published state by hand with one job queued behind it, and fails the suite instead of hanging on a regression (todo 39).

### Reading into Read spare capacity

Reading directly into `Read`'s spare capacity, beyond the read-pump double copy, was dropped. Handing uninitialized buffers to arbitrary `Read` implementations violates the trait contract.

### Ping-pong buffer recycling

The idea was to regenerate the accumulate buffer into a spare when the dead prefix is not enough, retiring the old allocation behind an in-order assembled watermark, as the pump-never-waits-on-workers form. An interim interleaved A/B on 2026-09-16 measured it equal to the simpler quiesce-gated wrap and grow on every fastest and fast stream-mt8 cell. The audit found three lifecycle defects in the WIP form. The spare was reused without clearing the retired buffer's length, which gives stale-prefix corruption plus runaway capacity at the 256 MiB ceiling. The poison abort completed unclaimed jobs without setting `done` or decrementing the posted count, which gives an infinite spin in the quiesce wait. The in-buffer wrap is a move of the live tail whose destination overlaps the views of still-running jobs, so it cannot run ahead of quiescence without ring-buffer job views, zstdmt's model with a different job contract. The measured overlap value on the epoch-sized buffer is zero, because the pump laps the workers about once per epoch and the quiesce wait is bounded by the in-flight encode span. The quiesce-gated form landed.

### Larger pump pulls

Larger read-side pump pulls of 64 or 128 KiB instead of a 16 KiB staging chunk were measured in an interleaved A/B on the mt8 read path over text and json at fastest and fast on 2026-09-16. The 64 KiB median ratio against 16 KiB read 0.89-1.00, below 1 on 5 of 6 cells, and 128 KiB read 0.85-1.00, below 1 on 5 of 6. A pull larger than the grid's posting granularity overshoots the epoch boundary inside one append, so the buffer pressure shifts to mid-epoch recycle points, which are quiesce-gated, instead of boundary-aligned ones. The 16 KiB chunk stays, as a persistent per-encoder buffer, because the zeroing and not the size was the cost.

### Incremental last-epoch posting

Posting the growing grid's last epoch incrementally as its bytes arrive was rejected at design time. The read-pump round exposed json.fast's two-wave floor, where epoch N's jobs post only when fully buffered and epoch N+1's tail only at `finish`, so a 32 MiB stream runs ramp-read, wave and wave with about 1.5 ms of read unhidden. The stream-end tail re-slice is a function of where `job_start` sits at EOF. Incremental posts advance it, for example 4 MiB grid jobs posted at 20, 24 and 28 MiB against today's 8x2 MiB re-slice of [16,32), and the frame bytes change, which the byte-identity gate of dump and 120 cells vetoes. A pledge gets the pipelined fixed grid instead.

### Zero-copy slice source

A zero-copy `&[u8]` source for the read pump was rejected at design time. `read::Encoder<R>`'s pump is generic over `R: Read`, and detecting that the source is a slice inside the generic needs specialization, which is unstable, or an `Any` downcast. The bench's `&raw[..]` borrows a local, so it is not `'static` and the downcast cannot fire either. The single copy is the Read-trait floor for an opaque source.

### Deleting the input copy chain

The streaming-encode input copy chain's deletion was the borrowed-window, direct-fill and ring family, prioritized on 2026-09-30. The idea was to fill the owned window straight from the Read or write path so that raw to read-buffer to staged to win collapses to raw to win. The ceiling was probed by gathering the whole stream into a reused thread-local buffer and running the bulk direct-window core, env-flipped inside one binary with position-balanced alternating rounds.

Healthy cells were flat at every copy count that keeps at least one input copy. text.fast stock with 3 copies and compaction read 6644-6742 against 6712-6903 MiB/s for gather plus bulk with 1 copy. text.fastest read 7083-7114 against 7094, text.best 1022 against 1021, json.fast 432-446 against 448-464, and json.best 29-30 on both, while the zstd sides stayed flat at 5248-5355 throughout. The zero-copy bulk core under `prof enc 3` measures 10973 MiB/s on the same shape, +63% over the stock stream.

The marginal input copy is not linear. One serial copy per byte already saturates the resource the scan needs, so deleting two of the three copies buys nothing, and every owned-window streaming mechanism is bounded at the gather level, which is stock. Such a mechanism necessarily keeps at least one fill copy, plus the compaction schedule whose `win_base` timeline is byte-load-bearing, because every parse floors candidates at `reach = min(pos−win_base, max_window)`. The remaining 60% sits only behind a zero-copy borrowed window, which a true stream cannot have, since the Read-spare-capacity contract was falsified above and caller memory is not contiguous with the history. The collapse reproducer's degraded phase, with in-pass text.fast at 4960-5030 and the zstd side at -5%, ran 5/5 rounds in one about 4-minute window and then 0/36 across the interleaved series. That is a machine-wide phase and not an orderable per-process state, so no collapsed-phase ceiling sample exists, and the direction closes on the healthy-state bound.

### Reserve by length hint

Reserving the accumulate buffer to a declared hint's extent (`min(hint + 64 KiB, BUF_CAP_MAX)` in `reserve_decided`) instead of the cap measured as a no-op on peak RSS on 2026-11-14: dll100 `enc-feed` fastest at w16 read 782.4 MiB baseline against 777.1 with the change, within noise, at byte-identical output. `reserve_exact` commits address space only and the cap reserve's untouched tail never faults, so RSS is charged for the streamed bytes either way; the change moves only a commit-charge metric on Windows, and only for mid-size streams — the field's solid blocks (457 MB, 3.51 GB) declare hints at or above the cap, where the clamp is identity. The unpledged footprint worth attributing is touched per-worker state, not the reserve. Nothing landed.

## Allocation

### MADV_HUGEPAGE on the opt tables

`MADV_HUGEPAGE` over the opt and dubt tables' fresh `alloc_zeroed` allocations followed the accumulate-buffer precedent, aiming the fresh-state Fast to Opt build's first-touch faults at the stream cells' reset spans. The jobdecomp json stream-mt reset spans rose from about 45 to about 95 ms summed, and ultra rose from 133 to 294, on this 59-day-uptime fragmented box with the advice in. The same build without the advice sat below the old u64 baseline from the u32 halving alone, and the walls stayed dominated by the fill and moved -16 to -22% regardless. Huge-page allocation under fragmentation pays direct-compaction stalls that exceed the 4 KiB faults they replace. The advice helps dense steady-state buffers, as with the accumulate buffer, and hurts once-per-encoder cold mappings, so it was dropped.

### Pooled job-output buffers

Recycling the encode jobs' output Vecs through a pool instead of a fresh multi-megabyte allocation per job was the delivery budget's first lever (the caller-thread fault and munmap attribution, see [ffi-stream-gap](../bench/ffi-stream-gap.md)). Implemented to landing quality on 2026-11-15 — the buffer rides the job's existing `out`-slot mutex handoff to its worker and back with the bytes, the pool itself runs lock-free on the driving thread, best-fit take, and debug poison walls at the pool's give and the worker's claim (the claim-side assert requires arming the wall at the take too: a fresh Vec's spare is uninitialized and the first assert false-positives, the lifecycle rule the poison protocol already states) — and falsified: glibc 2.44 already runs the pool. Its dynamic mmap threshold adapts to the first freed multi-megabyte Vec, later job outputs come out of the per-thread arenas, and the arenas recycle the repeated cap class within a frame and the growing grid's ramp classes across frames; strace on the stock build shows the 15.9 MiB cap class munmap about sixteen times per process against about 500 cap jobs, so only the ramp classes churn, one 1/2/4/8 MiB set of sixteen munmaps per frame.

Three retention designs measured with divergent outcomes on the game-mid/game-big/textdom enc-feed grid at l1/l3 x w8/w16 plus a C driver through `ZSTD_compressStream2` (128 KiB continues, srcSizeHint, bounded sink). Full core-scoped retention cuts the workers' own cost visibly — jobdecomp summed job time -33 percent on textdom l3 w8, warm pages — and read +3 to +4 percent ours on the game-big bench cells, but loses 15 to 25 percent wall on textdom w16 (same-binary env-switch A/B, reproducible across pairs): the growing grid posts a whole epoch before any of it completes, so a ramp class's buffers can never serve their own frame again, and pinning them starves the arenas the frame's other Vecs feed on — the core's output Vec and the FFI sink staging lose the freed chunks their reallocs recycled and pay fresh-page copies (strace per two iters: mremap 61 against 26 calls, brk 99 against 74, munmap time 29 against 19 ms with the teardown concentrated into a few big-VMA zaps at encoder drop). Fit-only take plus schedule-keyed eviction (retain only what the next job size fits) restores parity everywhere but moves no counter, because the classes it still pools are exactly the ones the arenas already recycle. A thread-local cross-frame pool (`BUF_POOL`'s shape, 256 MiB per-thread budget, 64 MiB per-buffer cap) also reads wall parity on every cell with the process-wide counters flat — game-big l1 w16 bench cell 3.50M against 3.52M minor faults and 765 against 773 munmap syscalls, FFI driver 1.82M against 1.74M faults over four iters — and its one structural effect, the per-frame ramp munmap set becoming once per process, is about 2 percent of a frame's wall. Advising the pooled buffers for hugepages measured dead even with and without. Cross-binary comparisons on this box swing ±5 percent from code placement alone (the interleaved harness's ours column moved more between two base-binary runs than the pool moved it), so the verdicts above all come from same-binary env-switched pairs.

The premise the lever stood on — a multi-MiB Vec is an mmap, so each job pays fresh first-touch faults and an munmap — holds only until the allocator adapts, and the caller-thread fault and memmove mass the perf attribution saw sits in the delivery copies themselves (the assembly's staging through the core's output Vec, the sink drain) and the input side, not allocator churn. Nothing landed; the falsified map covers the core-scoped, evicting and thread-local retention forms whole. The debug-poison protocol note stands for any future pooling attempt: arm the wall at the take, not only at the give.

### Persistent table slab for the pooled matcher

The R108 open lever — table memory that survives `resize_tables` on a pooled core, so a level transition reuses already-backed pages instead of reallocating onto the freed DUBT frames' recycling — was built to landing quality on 2026-11-18 and falsified. The design was a retirement depot on the matcher (`hugepage::TableDepot`, a three-slot FIFO ring): `resize_tables` parks a replaced table mapping in the ring instead of unmapping it, a later allocation takes the smallest fitting park back best-fit and zeroes its live prefix, and the slab arms only at the single-threaded pooled cores' take boundaries, which are the slice pool, the dictionary pool and the ST streaming core. The MT worker pool's cross-thread states stay disarmed, so nothing retains per worker. Retention is bounded by three mappings, 48 MiB at the 32 MiB corpus shape (the DUBT heads-plus-ring pair), poison-scrubbed at retire in debug builds. Output stayed byte-identical on the pooled-sequence tests (a new deep-row transitions test included), and strace on the out-of-tree cell-sequence reproducer — text.raw pledged through the ST streaming read core, one Best frame then one Fast frame per round, plus a fast-after-fast control — confirmed the steady state runs with zero transition syscalls where the stock build cycles 48 MiB of mmap and munmap per round.

The counters the lever stood on stayed put, and so did the penalty. Pagemap read the surviving hint-range mappings at a 0.998 contiguous-PFN-pair ratio on both builds, span 97x with the slab against 233x without with the parked 48 MiB included, and `perf stat` over a one-best-then-24-fast process read identical instructions at 33.604G against 33.602G with dTLB-load-misses flat at 5.98M against 5.94M. The fast-after-fast control was flat at 7538 against 7556 MiB/s. The post-best fast cell kept its penalty: medians of 40 position-alternating rounds read 5020 against 5215 MiB/s, which is -33.4 percent on the stock build and -31.0 with the slab, and the settled regime (one best frame, then 24 timed fast frames, churn-free throughout) read -19.0 against -18.5 percent. The transition-proximate cell showed the slab's only movement, +3.9 percent median on the every-round alternation, inside that arm's own 4928 to 5468 MiB/s inter-round spread at flat instruction counts, which is the disturbance class rather than a counter-backed gain.

The falsification re-aims the residue line. The stock penalty survives with the churn gone, the pages contiguous and the TLB flat, and the fast tier's own tables are 768 KiB heap Vecs on this ladder anyway, so the 2026-09-29 record's 32 MiB L3 hash table no longer exists at this row. What differs between the arms is persistent buffer geometry: a pooled core that once served a Best frame runs every later fast frame against a 130 MiB hugepage-backed window kept by capacity plus a parked 8 MiB LDM table, while a fast-only core holds no hint-range mapping at all. That difference is where the remaining -19 percent lives, and it is a steady-state property of the pooled state rather than a transition event. Nothing landed. The re-aim was decided the same day: the window-capacity half of that difference is the mechanism and landed as the level-down shrink, and the parked-LDM half is falsified by a direct A/B (the next section).

### Persistent window geometry verdict (R113)

The slab section's re-aim was decided on 2026-11-18 with an env-gated probe build on the same out-of-tree reproducer shape (text.raw pledged 32 MiB, 64 KiB pulls, checksum off, pooled ST core; the reproduced base penalties read -32.5 percent transition-proximate and -17.5 settled against the record's -33.4 and -19.0). On this tree a pledged Best frame clamps to the W25 row window, so the kept mapping is 66 MiB with a parked 4 MiB heap LDM table, and the fast-only control holds a 4.2 MiB heap Vec window with no hint-range mapping. Releasing the window buffer's capacity down to the incoming row's declared need at the level-down transition moved the settled penalty from -18.3 to +1.5 percent (same-binary env-switched arms; the landed form reads +3.3 in the cross-binary A/B), while dropping the parked LDM table at the disarm moved nothing at all, -17.3 against -18.3 stock: the fast row never touches the parked pages and they cost RSS only, so the parking stays. The counters held where the slab round left them, with instructions at 1467M against 1505M per one-best-then-24-fast process (the shrunk arm slightly higher, the fresh buffer's first touch) and dTLB-load-misses flat at 276-298K on every arm, so the -19 percent sat in the per-access cost of the fast row's window stream through the kept hugepage mapping — a placement effect below every counter this box exposes, as before with IBS. The lever landed (see [encoding](../perf/encoding.md)); the falsified remainder of the geometry line is the parked-LDM half. The transition-proximate cell keeps a one-frame residue of about -25 percent after the lever (from -33), the fresh window's first touch plus the cold head tables after the DUBT teardown, paid once per down-transition.

## Checksum

### Streaming-MT checksum off the calling thread

Four placements for the streaming-MT checksum absorb were measured on 2026-09-15. XXH64 has no chunk-level merge, because the spec's `mergeAccumulator` is a finalize fold, so per-job digests cannot combine and one serial state must absorb in order, as the [decoding page's merge proof](decoding.md) shows. zstdmt's SerialState is an ordered per-job `XXH64_update` under a mutex and not a merge. The variants were (a) pool-job pickup like zstdmt, where our bursts freeze the whole range at post so no ordering is even needed, (b) a sidecar thread through the existing `AsyncChecksum`, (c) the hash fused into the pump memcpy through `write_appending`, and (d) the baseline of absorb on the calling thread at fire time.

In-process interleaved 3-way and 4-way A/B on 32 MiB at w8, on both the write path and the read path with 64 KiB chunks, read as follows. Caller absorb is free, with write text.fastest at x1.005-1.041 and every read-path cell at x0.958-1.013 on and off. The sidecar read x0.96-1.25, never beating the caller and reaching x1.09-1.25 at text.fastest. Pool pickup read x1.15-1.23 at text.fastest and x1.05-1.11 at text.fast. The fused pump read x1.08-1.21 on the text cells. The caller's absorb cost shows only in slow machine states, with the first session at on/off 1.19-1.20 at text.fastest and `Xxh64::absorb` at 16.65% of whole-process cycles.

The fire-time caller absorb already rides the fire-to-drain window that overlaps the encode span on a thread that would otherwise wait at the barrier. Every offload pays the same 1.6 ms of hash plus a new concurrent memory stream competing with 8 encoders on the bandwidth-saturated fastest-tier cell. The sidecar extends the burst, pool pickup starts after the first job finishes and pokes past the last, and the fused copy runs slower than memmove and pays on the pump directly. The residual on/off tax at text.fastest is a resource tax, with the serial hash sum about equal to the pump memcpy sum and the encode span, and no thread placement can remove it. Only a cheaper hash or de-saturating the cell through the pump and burst work can. Nothing landed, and the pre-change placement stands.

## Decode scheduling

### Caller validation jobs

The calling thread claiming validation jobs while it pooled staging during the fused phase was a sub-variant of the landed caller-staging, where the caller runs `claim_validation` between staging segments, measured on 2026-09-17. Three-round interleaved medians against the caller-staged-only form read json mt8 5252 to 5365-5392, which helps, but dll32 mt8 read 4298 to 4246, or -1.2%, and dll32 mt16 read 4192 to 4069, or -3% consistently. The staged-only form beat it on every cell, at json mt8 5411 and dll32 mt8 4411. A validating caller reads the staged set while workers still write it, which is the falsified hybrid execution's shared-resource asymmetry at smaller scale. The caller pools staging only, since it writes the same streams the workers write and adds no reader of the staged set.

### Hybrid overlap

The piece-decode hybrid overlap ran optimistic serial stage B on the caller under a cap, leaving a quarter plus a 2 MiB floor for the pieces, and switched mid-flight to piece execution at the validation verdict. It was the no-regression form sketched for the staging barrier, implemented and measured fully on 2026-09-17, with `place(0, fcs)` up front, `SerialExec` collect and execute interleaved into the staging and settle waits, straddle-piece re-execution, and serial-prefix checksum handoff. On 32 MiB ramp frames at D=2 MiB with `files` medians, json mt4/mt8/mt16 read 3259/5073/5417 against 2359/2369/2222, dll32 mt8 read 4211 against 2051, and skewed mt8 was at parity at 5595 against 5589, because staging is short and the verdict precedes any meaningful caller work.

Two losses compound. The caller's streaming execution evicts the workers' L3-resident staged set, so per-piece validation goes from 0.37 to 2.5 ms and the whole round from 0.63 to 7.25 ms, settling exactly when the caller hits its cap, and staging itself stretches nondeterministically, with worker completion at 2.19 ms once and 9-12 ms in other runs. Even a cap of 8 MiB only reaches 4233. The interleaved segments also delay the round posting and the verdict, so the switch lands at about 5.4 ms against the barrier's about 2.9 ms. The serially executed bytes run at about 1/5 of piece-parallel speed and postpone the parallel phase. The caller idling about 3 ms of the about 6 ms wall is true, but the memory system is the shared resource, and filling caller idle with stage-B bytes is not free. The staging barrier is not a defect to engineer around, because the barrier form is optimal among all tested variants on this box. Remaining worker-side levers only are to fuse validation into staging per piece as covering segments complete, and to shave the stage to validate to switch transition wakes. The debug run also produced a concurrency lesson: interleaving unlocked work between a predicate check and its `cv.wait` loses wakeups, so every wait must be preceded by a fresh predicate evaluation under the same lock hold. The corrupt-frame test hung exactly there, twice.

### Restart-position grid discovery

Discovering the encoder job grid from restart-block positions was measured on 2026-09-16. Job starts are restart blocks by construction, so their output positions should pin the job size, and the attempt seeded candidates from the first restarts and kept a seed whose every multiple is a restart. On json.lvl3 at 32 MiB, 92 of 128 blocks are restart points because this encoder re-sends entropy tables densely, so the restart lattice carries no job-grid signal. The seeds all fail the multiples check except a coincidental 1.75 MiB periodic alignment whose pieces carry near-boundary crossing reads at a lag of 0-8 B, and the true 4 MiB grid never enters the seed set.

Restart density destroys the signal. Grid candidates for the piece executor come from the job-size formula's lattice instead, with `ceil(fcs/2w)` over worker counts plus the overlap-raised sizes, cross-checked by measured per-piece lag. It landed with `mt_pieces`, recorded in [mt-stream](../perf/mt-stream.md).
