# Matchers and Compression Levels

This page answers how each matcher strategy finds matches, what each ladder row runs, and which costs the measurements have bounded. It is organized by strategy tier, and each experiment states the change, the mechanism and the measured effect. The x notation is our time divided by the reference time, so values below 1 mean we are faster.

## Fastest

### Scan structure

The tier began as a zstd-fast style rewrite: a contiguous window, a single-probe u64 hash table keyed on hash5, a u64-chunk compare with forward and backward extension, and an increasing miss step. The table originally held `epoch<<48|pos` slots, and a reset only bumped the epoch. Entry width moved to u32 and the epoch was retired. Matching throughput rose about 3x, and text ratio jumped 54x because the old matcher found almost nothing on long-repetition data. The original commit is `a2cc982`, and the u32 table is `327bc99`.

The matcher maintains three recent offsets with the same semantics as the decoder's history through `do_offset_history`. A position with no literals probes rep0 at pos+1, where the current byte acts as the one literal, and backward extension is guarded by `start > anchor+1`. Emission records rep0 as `of_value` 1 and a new offset as `offset+3`. This moved json's rep hit rate from 0.07% to 7.6%. Immediate rep1 chain probing, the zstd rep_offset2 loop with an ll==0 swap of rep0 and rep1, then raised json ratio from 4.93 to 5.23.

Sparse indexing inserts every position in matches of 16 bytes or less. Longer matches insert only the `start+2` and `end-2` anchors. Pure dual anchors cost json 2.1% and text 6.3% ratio, so the extra start anchor keeps json's long-distance matches. The form gained 6% on json.

The table is 2^15 slots, halved from 2^16, with strided insertion on the miss path. A small table under high contention with newest-wins replacement creates a strong proximity bias, and zstd level 1 runs 2^14 slots against a 512K window for a 32:1 coverage ratio.

The hash path accepts a candidate only at `ml >= 6`. A 5-byte match costs about the same as the literals it covers, so rejecting it lets the scan advance toward longer match starts. Thresholds of 7 and 8 were tried: json saturated and text collapsed. A threshold of 6 is the balance point, and it moved json from 5.44 to 5.72.

The window grew from 448K to 768K because the text corpus's tile period falls in the 512K to 768K band, which unlocks cross-tile matches. Text ratio moved from 6.46 to 239.

### Probe and emission code

Select-based probing, libzstd's selectAddr idea, selects invalid candidates into the scan position itself. A byte compare plus `cand != ip` replaces the epoch, window base and distance triple compare. The rep0 pre-probe folds into the single comparison `probe >= win_base + rep[0]`. Json moved from 455 to 466 MiB/s.

A `TableEmit` context struct replaced a free function with 12 to 14 parameters, which guards against parameter stack spilling. Scan-side table access uses raw pointers.

The emit path split the coverage-indexing loop out of `TableEmit::emit` into an `#[inline(never)] insert_covered`. While it was inline, emit exceeded the inliner budget and each of the scan loop's five call sites paid a full spill and reload of the emit context, covering literals, sequences and rep state, plus a hash-constant rematerialization around the call. The call prologue and epilogue alone sampled about 9% on json.fastest. The split leaves emit small enough to inline at every site. Output is byte-identical, and gungraun measured json.fastest at 2.7% fewer instructions and text.fastest at 1.1% fewer.

The scan loop has two phases in one macro. The job-start seed state (`seed_offset`, `seed_hits`, `seed_budget`, `rep_pending`) only winds down, so the macro is instantiated twice: a seeded phase until convergence, then a steady phase with all four values constant-folded out. A single loop kept them live across every iteration, which was pure dead weight in bulk single-thread and post-convergence MT jobs, and about 24% of the scan's cycles sat on stack spill and reload traffic. Output is byte-identical. Gungraun measured fastest json and text at 10.1% and 10.0% fewer instructions, random at 2.0% fewer, and wall json.fastest moved from 477 to 532 MiB/s (x1.78 to 1.60) while text.fastest moved from 11279 to 12855.

`rep1_chain` also moved to `inline(always)`. Duplicating the body halves the inliner's budget per copy, and the helper otherwise flips to an outlined call whose argument setup dominates dense-match emits. Json was at 3.1% fewer instructions before that fix, and forcing the inline doubled the win.

### Steady-body slimming

Three pure overhead cuts landed on 2026-09-11 and are byte-identical, wall-neutral on json because the loop is branch and latency bound at IPC about 3. The pair-prep u64 feeds hash5, the 4-byte prefilter's low half and the rep compares, which removes two loads per pair. Rep probes compare in window-index space (`pidx >= rep[0]`), which deletes the per-position `rep0_lim = win_base + rep[0]` u64 chain and its spill. The fast and dfast `resolve` rebuilds the candidate as a distance, `dist = pos - entry + 1`, with double wrapping and no `entry-1` underflow. The empty sentinel and previous 4-GiB-cycle entries wrap huge, so one compare `dist <= reach` replaces the entry, wrap and window checks and returns `idx - dist` directly. The reach is `min(pos - win_base, max_window)`. Gungraun measured fastest json and text at 5.9% and 5.7% fewer instructions. Random paid 1.35% more instructions for two extra ALU operations per empty probe, and its wall stayed at 2.3 GiB/s. The `pos1 == anchor` select in the second probe is provably dead because `anchor <= pos < pos+1`, and it was folded out. A steady and tail loop split that folded `pair_len` to a constant 2 was dropped: it cut 3.3% of instructions but cost text.fastest 7% wall because the third macro instantiation displaced the hot blocks, as recorded in `negative.md`.

The pair's two repcode compares collapse into one unpredictable branch. An XOR prefilters `a` and `b` over the same loads, and the branch is taken when either is zero. Adjacent-position rep hits are correlated, so the fold predicts no worse than either compare alone. A taken fold whose first position misses arms the second position's compare-free full probe, and probe and emission order are untouched, so output is byte-identical. This is a branch-bound regime trade: gungraun measured 1.5% and 1.4% more instructions for wall json.fastest moving from 530 to 545 MiB/s (x1.62 to 1.57), and text.fastest's 1.4% change sits inside that cell's layout noise, with untouched fast cells moving ±3% to 8% in the same binary. Halving the probe count instead, libzstd's one-compare-per-pair ip2 policy, costs json.fastest 4.75% size.

The pair's two hash-match compares share one branch. Both candidates are resolved and pre-compared up front as `cand != ip && read4(cand) == cur` at both positions, then folded as `m = (cand == ip) as u32 | (read4(cand) ^ cur)` and `(m0 == 0) | (m1 == 0)`. LBR attribution on json and text 256K blocks, from checksum-parity builds, put the two compare jumps at 5.3% plus 4.3% and 6.4% plus 6.3% of all process samples before the fold, and the scan's branch profile is flat afterwards with a maximum of 1.6%. The pre-reads are loads only because table writes happen in emit, and emission order stays rep0 at p0, hash at p0, rep at p1, hash at p1, so output is byte-identical. The fold was gated by a full-ladder dump over 30 files plus L1 and L2 emitframe gates in single-thread and MT modes. Fold-only A/Bs over three or four interleaved rounds with medians:

| Cell | Before | After | x before | x after |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| json 256K | 622 MiB/s | 677 MiB/s | 1.528 | 1.400 |
| json 1M | 573 MiB/s | 614 MiB/s | 1.507 | 1.406 |
| text 256K | 354 MiB/s | 370 MiB/s | 1.718 | 1.650 |
| text 1M | 814 MiB/s | 841 MiB/s | 1.604 | 1.554 |

Json 4K and 16K stayed flat, and the 32 MiB matrix read json.fastest x1.502 against the earlier 1.52 to 1.57 band. The one regression is json-64K at 5%, from 744 to 696 MiB/s, reproduced across three sessions with 3% fewer instructions, 37% more branch misses and 7% more cycles per MiB. In the fully L1 and L2 resident single-block regime, the merged branch's streak pattern loses to the two originals' separate biases. Folding `rep1_armed` into the same condition was worse. After the fold no branch lever remains in this loop, and the remaining mid-size gap is instruction volume at 1.5x libzstd per probe event plus `insert_covered`'s store floor.

### Where the cycles go

A branch and cycle attribution on json.raw 32 MiB solo used perf stat, branch-miss and cycle sampling, and a counter build for event rates. Each encode carries 2.22M branch misses, 2.0% of 108.8M branches and about 12% of 281M cycles at roughly 15 cycles per miss. Cycles split into scan 52.8%, `insert_covered` 18.2%, `encode_sequences` 13.3%, bit-writer 4.3% and table choose 2.2%. The scan's instruction profile is flat after the fold, with a maximum of 2.3% at a spill store on the fold's input. Mispredicts split into `insert_covered` 26% to 28%, with the pairs-loop exit at about 12%, the 16-byte-or-less versus anchor split at about 2.3% and the parity peel at about 1.3%, the hash-compare fold at about 12%, `extend_match` exits at 2% or less each, and the rest below 1.5% per branch across the rep prefilter fold, m0 disambiguation, `ml >= 6` gates, backward-extension loops, `rep1_chain`'s break and `push_seq_packed`'s branches.

Event counts per encode are 2.35M pair iterations, the fold taken 1.32M times at 56%, m0 validating 1.10M at 47%, m1-only hits 0.37M at 16%, the rep prefilter firing 455K at 19%, hash-probe extends 1.47M of which 1.09M pass `ml >= 6` and 1.08M emit, leaving 26% rejects in the 4-5 byte weak-validation band. The dense json scan is hit-path-dominated: over half of all pair iterations run the full extend, gate, backward-extension, bar and emit sequence, so it is not a miss loop. The one removable lump was the fill's branch structure, and its harvest is measured out: a fixed-count `DenseFlat` fill was falsified the same day at -7.9% misses for +7.9% instructions because the store floor protects the rolled loop, as recorded in the [matcher negative results](../negative/matchers.md).

### Table size

The table geometry sweep covered hash_log 13, 14 and 16 against the landed 2^15, using same-session solo perf stat, an interleaved matrix and deterministic sizes. Each build was identical except the `HASH_LOG` constant, which keys the row, the initial allocation and the dense-body const-log instantiation together. Json gets denser and faster as the table shrinks because near-field repeats ride the newest-wins proximity bias: 2^14 gave 5,250,281 to 5,241,622 bytes, down 0.16%, at 5.6% fewer cycles, wall 602 to 637 MiB/s (x1.424 to 1.337), mt8 3727 to 3936 and 1% to 2% on the small band. 2^13 gave -0.35% at 8.7% fewer cycles, wall 662 at x1.297 and mt8 +9.1%. Text's unique tiles and dll's far twins die to slot overwrites: text +2.16% and +5.99%, with the +0.09% zstd-1 parity flipping to -2.03% at 2^14, and dll100 moving 48,815,803 to 50,962,327 to 52,156,684, up 4.41% and 6.85%. 2^16 inverts the trade: text -1.06%, dll100 -2.40% and json +0.07%, at 6.5% more json cycles and 7.0% more dll cycles, wall -6.1% and mt8 -5.0%. All three alternatives lose on the size axis, and dll100's +4.41% is ten times the fill-thinning precedent's rejected +0.46%. The gain and loss split cannot be gated by parse statistics because json and dll sit in the same dense band and only text's alphabet separates. 2^15 is the ratio-chosen optimum, and the tier's residual json deficit is the per-event instruction volume of that choice, recorded in the [matcher negative results](../negative/matchers.md).

### Per-event instructions against libzstd

A json 4 MiB level 1 comparison used callgrind `--dump-instr` with per-basic-block attribution, comparing the scan function plus `insert_covered` against `ZSTD_compressBlock_fast`, with event rates from instrumented builds of both sides. Ours runs 298,544 pair iterations, the fold taken 163,935 times, m0 137,407 and m1-only 148,808, with 192,147 emits: rep0 at p0 56,249, hash0 105,749, rep1 at p1 54 and hash1 30,095, plus 18,129 rep1-chain steps. Libzstd runs 424,793 iterations, 62,010 rep hits, 129,037 hash hits and 12,866 repchain steps. Both sides emit about 191-192K sequences over the same bytes, and ours uses 30% fewer pair iterations from the dense-mode stepping.

The whole encode is 121.46M instructions against C's 76.27M, and the sides split oppositely. Matcher plus fill is ours 90.98M, split into dense scan 62.43M, `insert_covered` 24.29M and plain startup 4.25M, against C's 40.38M, a 2.25x difference. The entropy side is ours 29.4M against C's 35.9M at 0.82x, where `encode_sequences`, the bit-writer and table choose win. The dense scan's 62.43M splits into pair-fixed probe machinery about 14.1M at 23%, from two hash5 probes, two read8s, two pack and store pairs, the m compares and bounds, at about 51 instructions per pair against C's 34, the resolve closure twice plus the OR-fold 8.07M at 13% as the single largest block with 44 instructions executed 224,184 times, miss-ramp rejoin 3.7M, post-probe rejoins 3.5M, forward and backward extends 6.7M, the dense bar plus `pays_for_offset_lit` 3.5M, and emit sites plus `rep1_chain` 7.5M. Per pair, ours runs 209 instructions against C's 95, and C walks 1.42x the pairs for the same output.

The resolve block is large because the u64 distance, reach and `(pos - win_base) - dist` chain carries window-base, max-window and previous-pointer spill reloads on the candidate load's address chain. C pays about 6 instructions per position in u32 index space. Both structural levers off this table are falsified, the u32 index-space resolve measured the same day and the register-removal class of incremental index and position cursors measured on 2026-09-23. The index forms trade the base's free position low half at the stores and resolve, one `sub` for the index at addressing, for a spilled `win_base32` reloaded every pair plus LEA rebuilds, as recorded in the [matcher negative results](../negative/matchers.md). The per-event excess is the closed price of the 2^15 newest-wins geometry.

### Fast-row stream coordinates

`pack_pos` stores `abs as u32 + 1`, so once a stream passes 2^32 positions every in-window candidate rebuilds a distance of 2^32 or more and the u64 resolve rejects the whole table. Past 4 GiB the ratio collapses to repcodes only. A 6-GiB streamed token replay produced 3.46 GB broken against about 1.24 GB fixed, with the rate tripling past 4 GiB, and the output stayed decode-valid throughout, so the defect is ratio-only.

The fix uses the loops' coordinate purity. Every window index derives from `pos - win_base` and every table entry from scan positions, so a `fast_shift` field rebases the whole domain at the parse interface. Entries store `abs - shift`, and the loop text, its codegen-visible arithmetic and the u64 resolve are untouched. Libzstd's reference design sidesteps the class entirely with window-relative positions that are never truncated. Strip fills through `LdmStripFill::Ingest` set `shift = strip base`, and the rebased MT jobs inherit their entries against the job's strip base, while defer and windowed fills leave the shift and the scan-side trigger re-anchors. The trigger rolls the epoch in `start_matching_codes` at `pos - shift > FAST_EPOCH_SPAN` (2 GiB): it clears both tables and sets `shift = win_base.max(pos - SPAN)`. The cleared entries are beyond every strategy's reach, with reach at most `max_window` and far below the span, so no candidate is lost, and a job more than 2 GiB past the last anchor loses only its first block's prefilled candidates. Two regimes exist: `shift <= win_base` is a pure numeric rebase, and `shift > win_base` is the borrowed giant window for bulk one-shots past 2 GiB, where the window slices forward and `win_base_local = 0`.

Gates are a translational-invariance unit test that parses sequence streams at base `(1<<33)+12345` and at base 0 for fast and dfast, a 6-GiB streamed token-replay integration test for ST Fastest, Fast and MT Fastest under a 2 GiB bar, a full-ladder dump, 310 tests and 30 libzstd CLI roundtrips. Cost, measured with gungraun against the base build, re-rolls the scan functions' register allocation at byte-identical output: fastest json, skewed, random and zeros stay at ±0.05% and text moves +0.54%, while fast json +0.68%, text +0.73%, skewed +1.98% and random and zeros stay flat.

A follow-up that maintains `shift <= win_base` by re-anchoring the window at epoch advance, folding the entry's saturating rebase and the sliced-window base, was measured out the same day. The fold is byte-identical and re-rolls the allocation again: fastest json -1.57% and text +1.91% instructions, fast rows -0.1% to -0.24%, net -0.4% with one real loss cell. The folded arithmetic is per-block prologue at about 0.001% of executions, not the live cost the original paid, so the two-regime entry form is the closed price, recorded in the [matcher negative results](../negative/matchers.md). That entry also records that the giant-window regime is defensive only, because no reachable caller adopts a window above 2 GiB.

### Dense-block instantiation

The tier has a second `const DENSE: bool` instantiation of `start_matching_fast`, selected per block from the previous block's parse. Dispatch keys on a `ScanDensity` field alongside `RampGate`, so four function instantiations exist. A block enters dense mode when it has at least 512 sequences, fewer than 4 literal bytes per sequence, and a fed-back literal table covering fewer than 32 symbols. The alphabet arm is load-bearing because parse statistics do not separate the classes: text's four match-dense blocks carry 99.5% of its sequences and overlap json's sequence band at 2133-9420 against 6560-6799 and its average literal-length band at 1.37-2.64 against 2.60-2.83. A statistics-only gate either leaks text, changing output and damaging the stepping, or excludes json. The literal alphabet separates them, with json at 11-25 symbols and 3.47 bits per byte, text's dense blocks at 78-83 symbols and 5.4 or more, and dll's sparsest statistics-passing block at 43, so the bar sits mid-gap.

The dense body hosts two levers that stay falsified as unconditional forms. The fed-back-literal-price acceptance bar uses `dist >= 1024` as a pre-gate and `pays_for_offset_lit` after backward extension at both hash probes, declines fall through to the remaining probes and feed the miss ramp, and prices come from `note_literal_costs`, which already fires for the fast tier with a flat-4 default at frame and job starts. Miss-run stepping doubles the pair advance once `miss_count > 3`, one full missed pair. The plain instantiation's body is source-identical with the levers folded out.

Results: json.fastest size fell 3.91%, 3.83%, 3.67% and 3.34% across bulk single-thread, stream single-thread, bulk MT and stream MT, with bulk single-thread moving 5,466,381 to 5,252,606. The 32 MiB wall moved 559 to 590 MiB/s over three interleaved rounds, x1.528 to 1.443, and json-1M fell 2.72% at 608 to 630 MiB/s, x1.425 to 1.374. Json 4K and 64K are dormant at ±0.5% because single-block payloads never gate. Everything else is byte-identical: the 120-cell ratio sweep moved exactly the four json.fastest cells, the full-ladder corpus dump moved only json.l1, dll100 l1 and l3 emitframes are bit-equal, and dll100 fastest wall is flat at 358 MiB/s with the output xor-equal. The bar alone is -1.35%, reproducing the negative entry's number, stepping alone is -3.16%, and the combined form is -3.91%, so the levers stack. The step threshold's size response is non-monotone trajectory chaos, recorded in the [matcher negative results](../negative/matchers.md). Text.fastest pays -1.8% wall at byte-identical output from the plain instantiation's placement in the doubled-code binary, inside that cell's documented ±3% to 8% band, and the placement was not chased.

### Table allocation and hash-log instantiations

The fast, dfast and chain strategies originally held their head table and second table in two `Vec<u32>` allocations. The dfast steady loop paid both pointers' registers and their per-iteration spill reloads, callgrind-attributed as the `0x80` and `0x88(%rsp)` loads at the loop head and rep probe. One `tables` buffer now splits at `second`, with `[heads][second]`. Keep and refresh semantics across level switches are preserved exactly: kept heads with a resized second table use `truncate` and `resize` so only the new tail is zeroed, which is the old fresh-second-table behavior, and any heads-size change reallocates the whole buffer like the old two-allocation drop. In `start_matching_dfast` the split is the row's const log for instantiated rows, so the small table's accesses lower to displacements at `0x80000(%rsi,%rax,4)` and the loop's table state is one base register. Callgrind on dll4 measured the fast scan 145.22M to 136.55M instructions, down 6.0% and at 1.350x C's `ZSTD_compressBlock_doubleFast`, and the whole encode 205.9M to 197.2M at 1.099x C. Fastest moved 80.82M to 80.42M, down 0.5% as a layout side effect. Interleaved wall over four runs moved dll100 fast 383.5 to 387.2 MiB/s, fastest 370 to 373.5, text.fast +2.2% and skewed.fast +3.8%, with json and skewed fastest inside noise. The wall conversion is bounded by dll's 1.72 L1-miss per byte floor because the removed reloads were L1 hits. Output is byte-identical everywhere, gated by the full-ladder dump, dll100 l1 and l3 single-thread plus l1 and l9 bulk-MT emitframes, a 120-cell sweep diffed against a baseline-build sweep, and 199 plus 7 plus 16 tests. The steady loop still reloads the one base twice per miss iteration, so the register famine is reduced and not gone.

The fastest scan still carried its hash log as a runtime register with a per-position `shr %cl` and `(-log)&63` precomputed in the preheader after the dfast const-log landing treated only the fast tier. Instantiating `HASH_LOG` 15 as a const parameter removes the register and makes shifts immediate. The result is a per-body register-allocation re-roll. The dense body's roll landed well with json4 fastest scan 64.23M to 62.04M, down 3.4%, whole call -1.8%, wall parity, while the plain body measured dll4 80.82M to 81.94M, +1.4% at wall parity, because the freed register was consumed elsewhere. The dispatch therefore instantiates the const row only for `ScanDensity::Dense` blocks of full-row inputs where `params.hash_log == HASH_LOG`, and the plain body keeps the runtime-log form with dll4's scan instructions bit-identical at 80,817,944. Output is byte-identical under the full-ladder dump and dll100 l1 and l3 emitframes.

Dropping the dead mask in `hash5_log`, `hash_at_log` and `hash8_at_log` removed a provable no-op. `>> (64 - log)` already leaves exactly `log` bits, so `& ((1 << log) - 1)` is dead, but with a runtime `log` LLVM cannot prove it and rebuilds the mask as `mov -1; shl cl; not; and` per hash. The mask hoists out of the steady scan loops but is rebuilt per call inside an outlined body, which the chain walk paid every search. Dropping the AND is byte-identical by construction and gated by the full-ladder dump. Gungraun measured fastest json and text at 5.2% and 6.4% fewer instructions, fast at 4.2%, 7.5% and 6.1% on json, text and skewed, balanced at 6.1%, 5.9% and 0.6%, and opt and ultra at 0.1% or less.

The output bitstream uses u64-spelling bit writes, described on the [encoding page](encoding.md). Final ratio on json is 6.15 against zstd-1's 6.11.

## Fast

### Tier choice and skeleton

The Fast tier first used chain plus lazy, but libzstd level 3 is dfast. The hot 2.5 MB table with per-position insertion and lazy's doubled search left json 2.73x behind and text 3x slower than Fastest. After the dfast port, json gained 80% at 175 to 314 MiB/s with ratio 5.47 to 5.33, overtaking zstd-3's 5.31, and text gained 154%.

The skeleton uses two tables, hash8 2^17 with prime `0xCF1BBCDCB7A56463` and hash5 2^16 reusing the chain buffer. It inserts then probes, pipelines two positions, upgrades to a long probe at ip+1 after a short hit, catches up backward, and pre-probes the repcode at ip+1. It performs zero table insertion inside a match, backfills four anchors into both tables after a match, and runs an immediate repcode loop.

The emission path slimmed to a 16-byte `SeqWord` single stream. Codes, add-bits and add-bit widths move from three streams into one, so three pushes become one. Push and pack must be `inline(always)`, because plain `#[inline]` leaves them uninlined and still costs 6.8%. Sequences with no literals, which are 50% of json and all rep chains, skip the literal copy, and probes become conditional moves. Json moved from 314 to 366 MiB/s.

The miss step became stateless at `1 + (ip-anchor)>>8`, dropping the per-256-jump counter pair. The backfill predicate became exact, meaning it backfills only when the match covered the probe position, which replaced the step<4 proxy. Json gained 1.7% and text 2.6%.

### Code layout and constants

The two-phase scan loop and `inline(always)` on `rep_chain` mirror the Fastest split. This loop's live set is larger because it carries two tables and two hash logs, so the dead weight cost more. Gungraun measured fast json and text at 5.7% and 5.4% fewer instructions, skewed 8.1% and random 5.7%. Wall moved json.fast 371 to 387 MiB/s (x1.29 to 1.25), text.fast 10821 to 12033, and skewed.fast x1.37 to 1.31.

Distance resolution and an index-space rep probe on 2026-09-11, the same two rewrites as the Fastest steady body, land bigger here because the dfast body carries two tables and two resolves per position. Gungraun measured fast json and text at 9.7% and 8.4% fewer instructions. Wall sits at x1.22 to 1.25 against zstd-3, with the instruction cut the solid part because between-run drift is 5%.

`DfastEmit::emit` moved from `#[inline]` to `inline(always)`. The phase-split macro instantiates call sites twice, halving the inliner's budget per copy, so plain `#[inline]` flips to an outlined call in the steady phase. The calling convention was the tax: three stack-passed arguments, repcode round-tripping memory, `Vec` pointer, length and capacity loads through `self`, and prologue and epilogue, about 50 of the outlined body's 170 instructions per sequence over 1.21M calls and 26% of the whole fast encode on dll32. ### Zero-add-bit pack path

LL codes 0-15 encode the literal length itself and ML codes 0-31 encode ml-3, both with zero add bits. ### Layout deviations

Five layout deviations from libzstd are each measured. The tag packs into the entry's top byte above the position's low 24 bits as `(tag<<24)|((pos+1)&M24)`, with 0 as the sentinel, and the rebuild resolves uniquely in `[pos-2^24, pos)` against the scanning position with the 4 MiB-or-less window arbitrating. This gives one table and one line per row instead of libzstd's u32 `hashTable` plus u8 tag table, and the mask compare becomes lane-wide through `srli_epi32`, `cmpeq_epi32` and `movemask_ps` with one bit per entry, which the pitfalls entry explains is load-bearing.

The cycling insertion head, libzstd's `ZSTD_row_nextIndex` stored in its tag-row byte 0, lives in a parallel one-byte-per-row array of 32-64 KiB, L2-resident and off the entry line. No slot is burned on head storage, giving 16 usable slots against libzstd's 15, and the fill loop's head load never serializes behind its own cold-line stores. The array clears per job with the head table because slot choice is output-shaping state under the pooled-state determinism contract.

The search is two-phase like libzstd's `matchBuffer`. Phase 1 collects in-reach tag matches with each candidate's window line prefetched, because the beat-check reads are independent multi-MiB-window misses and an inline verify serialized them, and phase 2 verifies. The reach check and candidate rebuild work entirely in the 24-bit wrap domain, where `((pos+1)-entry) mod 2^24` is the true distance because the packing bias cancels, so phase 1 is one u32 subtraction per candidate with no 64-bit rebuild. The iteration is head-ordered newest-first with a rotated mask where bit i is slot `(head+i)&15` and no early break, because pooled-state residue and the search-time rep-advanced insert leave mixed ages in a row, and a break would cut the walk short of newer in-window candidates behind one stale entry. The ramp gate folds out of the per-candidate loop through a `RAMPED` const generic, the fast loop's discipline.

### Retention and fill

### Selection and storage

The chain tier prices lazy positions on `ml*4 - highbit(offset+1)`, with reps priced at zero, instead of raw length. The lazy walk prices displaced literals at the fed-back code lengths of the first four bytes, which every candidate has because it is at least MIN_MATCH long, clamped to 6 bits. The store gate prices the literals a match displaces. The block encoder feeds each block's Huffman code lengths back to the matcher through `Matcher::note_literal_costs`, with a per-frame and per-job reset that keeps MT output content-deterministic. The chain stores a match only if the displaced literals, priced at those lengths with uncovered bytes at the 11-bit cap, cost more than `highbit(offset) + 7`. The flat 4-bit constant traded json against text monotonically, with json 6.21 and 6.55 and text 368.1 and 366.3 at offsets +4 and +7. The whole swing is 5-byte matches at 8-32 KiB offsets, whose displaced bytes are nearly absent from text's residual literal stream, where average residual entropy is about 0.2 bits per byte against 3.3 on json and anti-predicts the desired direction. Text.balanced moved 366.3 to 368.1 and json.balanced 6.55 to 6.66 in one move. Skewed, with a flat 4-bit alphabet, and random, with no Huffman blocks, reduce to the static gate bit-for-bit. Only the chain levels consume this feedback, and the fast and dfast seed gates keep the static +7.

A single `ChainMatcher` reuses the emit, extend and repcode logic, with hash log, chain log, depth, lazy and window parameterized. Two pitfalls shaped it: repcode backward extension must keep at least one literal, and the position pos == anchor must probe the repcode, otherwise the row loses to its own Fastest by 8x, as recorded in the [encoding pitfalls](../pitfalls/encoding.md).

### Broken chains, entry width and window

The broken-chain fix addressed a major defect. Insert used window indices while the walk used absolute positions, so once the window base advanced, walks ran over truncated and garbled chains with an effective depth of 2 to 5. Balanced and Best's earlier speed lead was an artifact of burning fewer probes, and their ratio was off too. The 4x collapse of streaming Best at 18 against 74 MiB/s bulk shared the same root. The whole ladder was retuned on real chains with a beat check, a 4-byte compare on candidates that avoids full extension.

The Best core swap followed from depth cost: depth 16 to 64 bought only 0.9% ratio for a 2.7x speed difference. Best switched to a low-config optimal parser at 16 compares and targetLength 32, and json Best moved 5.77 to 6.88, overtaking zstd-12's 6.14, while text moved to 270.8, overtaking 256.1.

### Chain head coordinates

The chain heads' u32 entries are origin-shifted by `pack_pos(pos) + head_origin`. This is the opt tables' monotone-coordinate invalidation applied to the 8 MiB H21 table, so MT jobs stop clearing it. The chain heads' coordinate epoch rolls at 3.5 GiB, the chain twin of the fast-row fix. A rolled origin is a wrapped negative and every field arithmetic wraps. The hot loops are untouched because the scalar's value differs only past the bar, so there is no codegen re-roll. Pre-roll stale entries resolve with distances inflated past every reach. ## Tree rows

### DUBT structure

The tree tier uses a faithful port of libzstd's doubly-unsorted binary tree in `dubt.rs`, covering `ZSTD_updateDUBT`, `ZSTD_insertDUBT1` and `ZSTD_DUBT_findBestMatch`, replacing the retired shallow-insert architecture. The fill pays three stores per position, a hash-head update, a chain link to the previous head and an unsorted mark, with zero compares. Sorting amortizes into searches: a search walks its bucket's unsorted chain while reversing slot 1 into a backtrack link, nullifies the chain's far end as libzstd's deliberate ratio-for-speed cut, batch-sorts the walked nodes oldest to newest with one budgeted descent each, then descends the sorted tree collecting strictly improving candidates. C returns one best, while our literal-aware selection consumes the whole improving set, worth about 0.5% over zstd-13 at equal knobs. Match interiors and stepped-over positions never pay beyond the fill, which is the entire speed point over a fill-time-sort tree.

Slot discipline carries soundness. Slot 0 is the chain link while unsorted and the smaller child once sorted, and slot 1 is the mark and then the larger child. Children are always older than their parent, and every sorted node's slots were written by a completed descent, so a descent never crosses an unsorted node. The `UNSORTED` sentinel is 1, which is position 1's own stored form, the same ambiguity libzstd documents in `ZSTD_insertDUBT1`. A child pointer to it misreads as unsorted and costs at most one extra re-sort, which is bounded and byte-verified. Entries are raw u32 positions with no epoch tag, and a restart clears the heads only because the ring needs no clear at all, and the MT job-start clear is likewise deferred to the first searching block.

The collection cap must be block-scale. An early exit at `sufficient_len` 32 stops the descent's threading with the walk, and the tree starves on match-rich data: the tiny-vocab ladder synthetic collapsed to 28110 bytes against L9's 24142. Moving the cap to `OPT_NUM` 4096, like the optimal parser's own walk, gave 19390 bytes, past even the old Keep-retention rescue at 22761, and json moved 5472046 to 5364906 on the same edit.

The lazy2 selection loop is a port of `ZSTD_compressBlock_btlazy2`. Each position's repcode and tree candidates compete on value minus offset price. A two-deep lazy walk chases a better start under libzstd's alternating margins, rep times 3 plus 1 plus 4 and then rep times 4 plus 1 plus 7. Literal-offset matches backward-extend into the pending literals, and an offset-2 chain follows every emission. This replaced the tier's earlier low-config optimal-parser rows, which read json x4.22 and skewed x6.22, an algorithm-class mismatch. Displaced literals price at the previous block's fed-back Huffman code lengths through `lit_value`, clamped to 6 bits per byte, with flat 4 bits before the first feedback, which is libzstd's raw `ml*4` scale bit-for-bit.

The DUBT port retired the 2026-09-13 shallow-insert architecture: the opt tree with a split `insert_log` budget, `FillTerm::Keep` link retention and the STALE/taint recount machinery. The DUBT tree beats the rescue outright at 19390 against 22761 bytes on the synthetic. The one measured loss is json's retention edge, ratio 6.27 to 6.25, traded for 24% to 52% tier speed. Rows 13, 14 and 15 ship C's exact S4, S5 and S6 ladder at H22, H23 and H23. The old json edge of the Keep tree at +2.2% over zstd-13 was retention-only and is deliberately not ported: S5 deepening buys no json ratio at 5472469, flat, for 24% less speed, and keeping frontier links on budget exhaustion buys +0.05% for -12%.

### Frame fixed costs on RLE frames

The 2026-09-16 matrix showed zeros regressions of 2.9x and 5.1x, with encode single-thread throughput falling from 46,548 to 16,080 MiB/s and from 43,534 to 8,515. The suspected rep1 covered-fill linking was exonerated because `insert_covered` long-match fills insert two anchors regardless and a link-less revert moved nothing. An RLE-class frame's encode is O(1) per block, one `is_uniform` scan at about 40 GB/s plus an RLE block header, so any per-frame O(tables) cost dominates it while amortizing invisibly on real shapes.

Three costs were found and landed on 2026-09-15 and 2026-09-16. The DUBT rows' eager per-reset clear zeroed both tables, 48 MiB at l13, every frame because u32 entries carry no origin. `dubt_stale` now defers the clear to the frame's first searching btlazy block, so all-RLE and all-raw frames pay nothing while every frame that searches sees exactly the same clean tables. ## Optimal parser

The opt tier is a full port of libzstd's `zstd_opt.c`. Match collection uses a lazily filled binary tree through `insertBt1` and `insertBtAndGetAllMatches` with epoch-tagged absolute positions, and the parse runs a forward dynamic program over stretches with fixed-point fractional bit prices at a scale of 256, using highbit plus linear interpolation to approximate `-log2(p)`. Cross-block frequency adaptation uses `rescaleFreqs` decay plus `updateStats`, btultra rechecks each match plus one literal, and two-pass first-block statistics seed the frame, with the epoch bump invalidating the pass-1 tree.

Three correctness details are load-bearing. `ZSTD_count` returns a pointer difference, so continuing a count must replace the stored count. Rep history is updated once per series by the path traversal, and per-sequence updates on the emission side are the decoder-equivalent alternative, but one must be chosen. Tree-insertion counting is capped at 4096 inside the DP window, because regions skipped by the parser would otherwise be recounted from stale heads, which cost text 45 to 313 MiB/s.

Results at landing: json Opt ratio 7.57 overtakes zstd-16's 7.28, json Ultra 7.52 against zstd-19's 7.49 with a same-window 1 MiB comparison at 7.53, and text Opt 271.5 against 272.0. Speed was json Opt about zstd-16 and text Opt 313 against about 440 MiB/s.

### Fill-lag clamp

Instrumented event counters against instrumented C, with `zstd_opt.c` temporarily built with matching counters, decomposed the opt row exactly. On json 4 MiB the two sides' events are identical to the last digit: 2,183,659 fills, 33,867,570 fill walk steps, 1,930,043 searches, 4,191,082 DP positions, 9,939 series and 136,500 emissions. On text the search events are identical but the fill diverges 45 times. `next_update = match_end - 8` wrote the last improving candidate's end, which on block-tiled data sits exactly one 128 KiB block back, so every block's first search re-indexed the entire previous block through the tree. The clamp now runs in `opt::run_block` at the real parse's end, so it applies at the next block's start. It has three gates, each backed by a falsified ungated form. It runs only on the alphabet-gated population, because the unconditional form kept -0.65% of dll100's size when a far class is in play. It never runs on job or dictionary strip parses, because job-restart parses measurably use the lagged candidates and stream-MT text cells regressed by 15-16 bytes. It never runs on the ultra seeding parse, whose cursor is reset right after and which C's `initStats_ultra` also bypasses. Output is byte-identical everywhere across the 120-cell ratio sweep, full-ladder dump and dll100, dll32 and dll16 single-thread and MT emits. ### u32 tree entries

The u64 tables put a 96 MiB working set behind every opt job, 32 MiB of H22 heads plus 64 MiB of C22 ring, with ultra at 160 MiB, the same memory-latency-bound class the Best tier's u32 conversion halved. Opt entries are now `(position + origin + 1) mod 2^32` on per-state monotone coordinates. `opt_origin` advances past every position the state has written at each invalidation point, the per-frame and per-job reset adds `last_pos + 1`, and ultra's frame-start pass-1 adds `block_end + 1`. This replaces the u64 form's 16-bit epoch with O(1) invalidation and no clears. A stale entry rebuilds as `cand = q - advance < 0` and dies below the window floor, while live entries rebuild exactly because the true distance sits far below 2^32. Output is independent of the origin value, so worker scheduling cannot leak through.

A 1-bit parity tag was tried first and falsified. The MT job path resets each pooled state twice through `take_slice_state` and `run_job_with`, which is invisible to a monotone epoch but neutralizes a two-period tag, reviving the previous job's entries wherever adjacent jobs' windows overlap, costing dll32 l16 mt4 332 bytes, recorded in the [MT negative results](../negative/mt-stream.md). Past 4 GiB of cumulative positions on one pooled state, the wrapped advance can resurface an old entry as a byte-verify-arbitrated phantom, the same class the u32 chain and fast tables carry. Footprint halves to 48 MiB for Opt and 80.5 MiB for Ultra, times MT workers. Output is byte-identical across the full-ladder dump of 30 files, the 120-cell ratio sweep, and dll100, dll32 and dll16 emitframes at l16-19 in mt4, st and mt8 modes. Fill rates and walls are in [mt-stream](mt-stream.md).

### Far-class floor widening

The dll100 opt and ultra density gap to `zstd -19 --long=26` was mis-attributed. C's printed geometry at that setting is `wlog=26,clog=24,hlog=22,slog=7,mml=3`, and the tree ring is cLog minus one, 2^23 positions or a 64 MiB `chainTable`, exactly our bt_log 23. `adjustCParams` never grows chainLog to a forced windowLog, so the assumed 128 MiB tree does not exist. What C has and we lacked is a descent lower bound of `windowLow`, the windowLog at 64 MiB: candidates between the ring's btLow and the window are priced as the head-named terminal step of each descent branch, with link threading breaking at btLow after pricing exactly our `ca <= bt_low` break, and repcode probes are windowLow-bounded too. Our `chain_reach` floor rejected everything beyond the 4 or 8 MiB domain.

## Level ladder tuning

Several lessons hold across the ladder. Ratio is tightly coupled to table pressure at the Fastest tier: any change that raises insertion volume, such as full-density probing or a slow ramp, crashed json ratio down to the 5.5 tier, and only dual anchors cutting long-match insertion volume to one eighth created headroom.

Depth has poor cost-effectiveness. Json at depth 16 against depth 64 is only +0.9% ratio for a 2.7x speed difference, so the road to high-end ratio is the opt parser.

The hash table must match the data. On skewed's 16-symbol alphabet, H17 has about 256 chains per slot that can never be fully walked, H20 and H21 terminate at chain tails, and zstd-9 runs H21. H18 collapses on skewed because finer head buckets thin out chain-head hits.

Locking periodic corpora relies on direct rep0 probing. A fixed grid is mathematically hopeless on clustered 5-grams, where twin slots with equal values recursively bury each other and phase alignment traps appear, which is why the MT job-start seeding mechanism exists.

The gain gate acts as a replacement gate. C's +7 threshold assumes C's table density. Our tables are denser because stride-1 prefill and the walk take the longest, so the selection bias toward weak long-distance matches is larger and the gate is deliberately stricter. The flat `ml*4 >= ilog2(offset)+7` form false-killed short and medium-distance matches on text at -0.55% with a threshold of 7, which literal pricing at the previous block's Huffman lengths resolved.

W4M is a double loss on the 32 MiB corpus shape, though the W21 and W22-23 landings superseded it as a blanket rule. A window only finds more distant matches, not longer ones, so bare widening wins exactly when the corpus's redundancy sits inside the added reach, as with dll's 1-8 MiB and json's 1-2 MiB tiles. Farther distance classes need LDM as the carrier.

## Long distance matching

### Far-class model

Long-distance matches use libzstd's model: a retained candidate ends the current segment at its split, the encoder emits the match wholesale there, and the scan parses only the literal gap ahead of the candidate. Every bound retargets at the segment end, so forward extensions, repcode chains and loop guards stop there. The wholesale emission backward-extends into the pending gap literals and rides the row's own emit helper. After each emission the offset-2 chain is bounded at the next split, a chain that ran past a split kills that candidate, and the miss ramp restarts. An empty candidate set folds to the stock single-segment parse, and the `LDM = false` instantiation binds the empty slice constant.

Arming has three inputs. The declared window must reach the far domain, the frame's head content must pass an alphabet gate, and the arming context must be able to carry the history. `LdmArming` distinguishes frame-continuous paths, which arm at a clamped window of at least 32 MiB, from MT jobs, which keep a 64 MiB bar or ride the capture. The alphabet gate uses `LDM_SYMS_MIN` sampled distinct bytes at the first parsed or filled block. Low alphabets produce structural 64-byte repeats whose far twins never survive their offset price, so they arm and never emit. Skewed measured -35% solo before the gate and -1.5%, inside the layout band, after it. Binary content at 256 symbols clears the bar with margin.

Skipped blocks stop filling in the mid-size population because incompressibility-gated and RLE blocks never reach a scan, so the quiet latch cannot fire through them. Random, all gated with alphabet 256, paid -53% solo in gear fills whose windows can never match. Head blocks keep filling because the far class sources the frame start through them, and dll32's size is unchanged by the exemption.

The mid-size gate extension turned the single window bar into an arming context. Frame-continuous paths, both bulk and single-threaded streaming, arm at a clamped window of 32 MiB or more, while multithreaded jobs keep the 64 MiB bar on an unclamped row window except for the prefix-strip class. The reach probe's parses never arm. Dll32 bulk single-thread moved 5,462,510 to 4,384,966 (-19.7%) at 6% to 8% more speed, within 680 bytes of the unlatched 4,384,286, while dll100 single-thread and MT and dll32 MT stayed byte-identical and the 120-cell sweep stayed byte-identical.

Four rules keep the corpus at its chain-only speed. A shrunk frame abandons LDM because the reach probe decided near locality from parses LDM cannot influence, so the measurement and the executed parse stay the same object. Without that rule the probe's shrink parse carried LDM candidates at 4 KiB to 2 MiB offsets and taxed skewed and text single-thread 25% to 35%. The alphabet gate applies to the mid-size population. Skipped blocks stop filling there. The MT capture keeps far-class history across job strips. The W26 population skipped the alphabet check until 2026-09-24, and the blind spot was the unpledged stream, whose unknown size leaves the row window unclamped and whose low alphabet could arm the frozen population with no latch floor because quiet never counts inside the first window.

### Chain row 9 LDM source

### Mid-size arming screen

The fast rows' mid-size band, a declared length in `[LDM_MIDSIZE_WINDOW 32 MiB, LDM_FULL_WINDOW 64 MiB)`, arms on content. `fast_row_screen_pending` handles a fast or dfast row with LDM on, a stock window below the far domain and a declared length in band, and routes the two entries that hold their head bytes at header time through `far_screen::frame_arming`. The bulk slice path screens `src[..SCREEN_SPAN]` before the frame header serializes. The pledged stream core stages its first `SCREEN_SPAN` bytes as one unit on the reach probe's staging discipline, and a flush or finish inside the window cancels back to stock, then re-derives arming, matcher reset and header before the first block emits. A `FarRepeats` verdict sets `LdmArming::FrameScreened`, whose armed semantics are the full-window semantics exactly, with the declared window widened to W26 and the scan domain preserved through `chain_reach`, including the frozen full-window population's skipped-block fill. Only the qualification differs, at a declared length of 32 MiB or more instead of 64 MiB. At 64 MiB and above the declared length still arms on geometry with no screen, so dll100 bytes are untouched, and below 32 MiB nothing changes. The two entries sample the same head bytes, so their verdicts agree by construction: the armed bulk frame and the pledged stream frame share every block byte, with the compat stream layer's header form as the only sanctioned difference, measured on dll32 as a 6-byte header against a 9-byte header with identical bodies.

The screen lives in `encoding::far_screen`. A strided alphabet bar of at least `LDM_SYMS_MIN` sampled distinct bytes runs first at microsecond scale, and json at 40, text at 102 and skewed at 16 never reach the sampling pass while zeros never reach it either. Then comes a content-aligned twin count. A per-byte gear hash feeds a fingerprint, with bit n of the state depending on the last n bytes, so triggers land at content-relative positions and a misaligned twin still fires identically. A position-grid sample would miss every one. A trigger is the top six state bits zero, about one per 64 bytes, and the fingerprint is a splitmix-style multiply-xor fold of the 64-byte window, screen-local, so identical windows hash identically and distinct wide-alphabet windows collide at 2^-64. A twin is a fingerprint duplicate in an open-addressed table of about one slot per 48 bytes, L2-resident at 4 MiB. `TWIN_ACCEPT = 256` twins accepts with an early exit and dll32's 4 MiB head carries about 13.6K, a 50x bar. `REJECT_SPAN = 1 MiB` with `REJECT_TWINS = 8` is the cheap reject: a twinless opening rejects after one MiB of chain instead of four. Dll32's first MiB carries about 100 twins against the bar of 8.

### Chain-row MT capture

Dll32-class frames run their MT jobs on whole-frame-prefix strips. The class is a chain row with a Keep verdict, a clamped window in [32, 64) MiB, and a wide-alphabet head that parses, screened by `ldm_head_parses` as the gate-probe evidence class. `LdmArming::JobPrefix` with a shared prefix fill keeps a capture-class job size through the pinned lattice while each job's strip covers the clamped window, which is exactly the history the frame-continuous path holds at the same position, so the mid-size bar arms for the jobs and the far class survives the job split. Windows of 64 MiB and above run the windowed variant instead. The per-job whole-prefix fill tax is paid once: the calling thread builds the median prefix boundary's fill while the below-median jobs encode, segmented at LDM batch-freeze points through `build_prefix_snapshot` in `encoding/mt.rs`, and tail jobs wait on the build and adopt its snapshot through `adopt_strip_snapshot` plus a remainder fill on the r8-strip rules. The gate-probe strip seeding is load-bearing: without it a fresh worker's incompressibility gate kills the far class before LDM sees the block, which a synthetic far-repeat test caught as an mt4 and mt8 byte split.

The windowed capture band retired the 64 MiB bar. W26-population frames, which are the same gates as the mid-size capture minus the upper bound, run windowed jobs: the job's window and LDM history span the whole 64 MiB through a strip of `[max(0, start-W), start)` plus its own span, while the chain tables keep the reach tail. The beyond-reach strip has exactly one reader, LDM, so the chain grid fill stays at 4 MiB and the job's chain-side bytes match a stock job's. The LDM state comes from a snapshot ring. The calling thread runs one sequential LDM-only fill of the prefix through `build_ldm_prefix_chain`, segmented at batch-freeze points, and publishes a snapshot every cadence step, about 13 publishes spanning median to stop and never pruned. Adopting jobs take the newest publish at or below their start and fill only the remainder.

Byte invariance is the design's core. A freeze-point continuation is exact, entries older than the job's window die on the distance filter, and the round-robin bucket holds the newest inserts either way, so a fill of `[0, start)` from any adopted boundary, or the stock from-zero fill below the first publish, leaves the same usable candidates and the snapshot a job gets is pure scheduling. Mid-stream jobs adopt the same way, and a snapshot older than the window base re-arms there with the byte-derived rolling state, which gives equivalent candidates. The job-start screens must see the window span: the gate-probe replay and the seed scan re-run over the LDM window through `windowed_ldm_prefill`. Without that, far-only content dies at the incompressibility gate and periods between the reach and the window lose their seed. A 5 MiB-period synthetic measured MT at +62% over single-thread before the fix and MT below single-thread after.

The grid is pinned at `clamp(len/8, reach floor, 16 MiB)` through `mt::capture_grid`, and it is worker-independent, so mt4, mt8 and mt16 are byte-identical. The size response to job size is steep below about three times the reach: each small job's exhaustive LDM refill of its recent history floods the 16-deep buckets and evicts older unique twins that a long job's parse-indexed history keeps. Dll100 at 4 MiB jobs lands +0.97 MB over single-thread and at 13.1 MiB jobs +34 KB. Chain-strip width is provably not the lever, because 4, 8, 12 and 64 MiB strips all land within 110 bytes, and neither is the adoption, where stock window fills equal snapshot fills within 292 bytes, nor the latch, where dead-start jobs are identical on the losing regions.
