# Performance Optimization · Multithreading and Streaming

## MT encoding (bulk slice path) `fd931a1`

**job model**: single-frame overlap jobs (stage-2; frame-per-job was rejected — multi-frame output is a burden on the decode side). Input is split into jobs (shared formula `job_size_for`: floor 1MiB, split evenly by workers, cap 1GiB — the same cap as libzstd zstdmt); `std::thread::scope` + atomic-counter task pickup + condvar join; worker state goes through the thread_local pool for reuse across calls; the main thread writes the frame header (FCS=total length) + assembles the job block streams in order + absorbs checksums in parallel; worker panics are recovered by the calling thread after assembly drains.

**three conditions for job independence** (at each job start, cross-checked against zstdmt):

1. entropy tables reset (last_huff/the three previous = None → expressed explicitly in the block header, legal);
2. repcode sentinel `rep=[u32::MAX;3]` (the probe is guaranteed to fail out-of-bounds; after 3 literal-offset sequences it converges naturally with the decoder's rep state; ST output stays byte-identical once the hot loop got the `rep_pending` gate);
3. overlap prefix prefill (see below).

**ratio preservation** (once the biggest defect: text periodic corpus mt16 ratio 328.8→19.7, zstd-mt 189):

- overlap=window and strips **actually prefilled into the tables** — previously strips were only borrowed as the window while the insertion path inserted only in-job positions, making cross-job matches mathematically impossible. `a37ebaa`
- fast/dfast/chain strip table-filling on a **stride-3 grid** (`4bba04a`; chain deviating from libzstd's dense dictionary filling is intentional: our strips = the full window, dense filling ≈ half a job's scan duration); chain stores got the gain gate to keep strips from flooding in 5-byte garbage matches.
- **periodic seed at job start**: during prefill, a backward scan over the strip tail finds the nearest long-repeat distance O and the job start directly probes `pos-O` (after 3 hits rep[0]=O and the repcode chain takes over; dead seeds retire after an 8192-probe budget). Periodic corpora across three levels mt/st 2.7-2.8→1.00. `a37ebaa`
- **Best-tier prefill under the DUBT finder (2026-09-14)**: the strip fill is the finder's own O(1) fill (3 stores/position) after a head-table clear — the tree restarts from the bucket chains and the first searches' batch sort rebuilds it (strip = full window = full ring, so every ring slot is rewritten and nothing stale stays reachable). No sort-at-prefill needed; best mt/stream cells stay within +0.08% of ST (ratio sweep), and json.best stream-mt8 came out ahead of zstd (x0.93).
- **determinism**: head tables are cleared per job (pooled state residue made output depend on worker scheduling, ±0.06% jitter; a landmine buried by the u32 no-clear design, previously masked by dense filling); chain lists need no clearing — the induction (candidates come only from cleared heads or this job's linked values) holds once every head insert writes its link too: the rep1 covered fill's link-less form broke it and made the stream pool's persistent worker threads produce claim-order-dependent bytes (fixed 2026-09-16, `insert_linked_at`; see [pitfalls](../pitfalls/encoding.md)) → clearing halved; clears ≥4MiB use NT stores. `a6cf8a6`
- **AVX-512 seed scan**: occupancy bitmap (64B chunks × 8 overlapping loads assemble one bit per position, taking bits top-down = exact equivalent of the scalar nearest-first order, output unchanged); random-class 0.45→0.06-0.13 ms/job (4-7×). `e1c6d67`
- **opt/ultra per-job cost decomposition + tree-fill bound (2026-09-16, `prefill_job_strip`)**: the deep tiers' per-job fixed costs are measured by `zstdx-bench jobdecomp` (`job_trace` dev feature — spans around each job's strip tree-fill, block-boundary refills, hash3 ingestion, ultra's seed parse, state reset, LDM prefill). Shares of summed job time (32 MiB corpus / dll100, mt8): the **strip tree-fill dominates everywhere — 40-73%** (text.opt stream-mt 72.7%, json.opt bulk 44.8% at a 3-5 MiB/s insert rate, dll100 44-50%); ultra's **seed parse is #2 on block-tiled text only** (32-38%, ~43 ms/job — the 256 KiB isolated seed span sits below the ~441 K tile period, so the seed parse runs literal-dense), minor on json/dll (0.8-3.8%); the stats/table reset is noise (the stream cells' ~5 ms/job reset spans are the fresh-state Fast→Opt table builds, once per pool worker per encoder — a state-pool follow-up, not a per-job cost); hash3 ≤ 1.9%, LDM prefill ≤ 0.3%. Two cuts landed on that decomposition:
  - **the opt rows' tree fill covers only the tail half of the job strip** (`opt_tree_strip`: C zstdmt's btopt/btultra overlap is window/2 — `ZSTDMT_overlapLog_default` 8; our ultra keeps the full strip, btultra2's overlapLog 9). The LDM table still ingests the whole strip: shrinking both cost dll100 opt mt8 +4.13% (the [4,8) MiB history band is LDM-served there — falsified in [negative](../negative/mt-stream.md)), while the tree-only bound is gate-clean: corpus worst json.opt +0.011%, text.opt −0.017%, dll100 −0.002%, dll32 −0.04%, dll16 −0.11% (all mt cells, st byte-identical).
  - **ultra's job-boundary seed span halved to one block** (the frame-start pass's own depth): corpus ultra cells +0.007-0.010%, dll100 ultra +0.003%; halves the seed's per-job cost on the shapes where it pays.
  Effects, interleaved A/B vs the pre-change build (mt8, medians): json.opt stream-mt8 9→13 MiB/s (**+44%**; the json strip fill is dense-insert-bound at 3-5 MiB/s, so halving its volume dominates the cell), json.opt bulk mt8 4.31→3.17 s (+36%), dll100.opt bulk 4.36→3.63 s (+20%), text.ultra stream-mt8 134→147 MiB/s (+10%, the seed halving — text's seed was 37.5% of job time), text.opt stream-mt8 +3% (text's fill is skip-assisted, so the volume cut buys little; its stream wall is pump-bound), json.ultra flat (its seed is json-cheap and ultra keeps the full tree strip). The follow-up levers recorded then are landed (2026-09-16, u32 tree entries — see [matchers](matchers.md)): the u64 tables' 96/160 MiB working sets halved (per-state monotone-coordinate origin replacing the epoch tag), and the walls on the same jobdecomp cells moved json.opt bulk-mt 3351→2814 ms / stream-mt 2422→1881, json.ultra 8189→6409 / 6386→5111, text.opt 125→57 / 170→66, text.ultra 185→101 / 229→119, dll100.opt 3690→2800 / 5034→3868 (3-iter means, mt8, `job_trace` build; the parse+emit share rides the halved ring traffic too). The fresh-state table-build span halves with the footprint; pooling it via MADV_HUGEPAGE advice on the fresh allocations is falsified below.
- **Ultra job-boundary statistics seeding**: the per-job `opt_state` reset was the entire remaining boundary ratio cost — a sequential in-order-carry decomposition (json.ultra, 4×8MiB jobs, every variant roundtrip-gated) put ~104KiB of the 85KiB mt-vs-st loss on the stats alone vs 1.3KiB entropy restart + 2.6KiB rep gate + 3.1KiB tree refill. Mechanism is a price-feedback basin, not miscalibrated histograms: a BASE-priced parse with the 8MiB strip reachable chooses near-offset/ll0 chain shapes, `updateStats` reinforces them, and the job never leaves that basin (carried stats show 37% rep0 / ml=12 / ll∈{1,2}; reset jobs parse 10% rep0 / ml=7 / ll0). Fix (2026-09-12): the job's first parsed block runs a throwaway parse of the strip tail (two blocks) with the window re-based to the span — candidates and fills clamp inside it, reproducing the empty-window frame start — purely to seed the stats; the real parse then re-fills the strip from `win_base` (seed-span re-insertion is benign: the head rejects the duplicate position). json.ultra bulk-mt/stream-mt −1.85%→−0.13%; ST dump byte-identical; mt wall unchanged (json 4.1 MiB/s best-of-5). Seeding the same span with the strip reachable recovers nothing at any span size — see [negative.md](../negative.md).

Results: 64MiB text 2/4/8 workers = 2.05×/3.95×/7.55× (ratio loss <0.1%); 32MiB matrix mt8/16 speed 2-4.5× ahead of zstd-mt, and after the ratio fix every mt16 cell returned to ST ±0.5%.

## MT decoding (exclusive dimension: libzstd has no MT decode) `3219947`

- **restart-point segmentation**: block headers are pre-scanned and segments cut where entropy state is self-describing (literals not Treeless, no Repeat FSE streams); job-style encoders (zstd `-T` and our MT encoder) produce exactly these points at every job boundary; multi-frame input = a special case of clean-start units, unified in one unit dispatcher.
- stage A (worker pool) entropy-decodes in parallel, producing packed sequence streams + exact output sizes; stage B (calling thread, in input order) executes into the output and carries the rep history — the repcode is the only cross-sequence state and never touches stage A.
- dictionary frames, malformed, single restart, single core → serial fallback (error reporting belongs to the serial path).
- pitfall: zero-sequence compressed blocks mid-segment (our MT encoder emits them on small-alphabet data at balanced levels) used to abort stage A with `MissingCompressionMode` — the serial path never builds FSE tables for them; stage A must skip the sequence decode too (2026-09-15 fix, regression test in `mt.rs`).
- **current status** (2026-09-15, post wildcopy execution + pooled staging): 64 MiB solo scaling json.zst3 1.5-1.7×(4T)/1.74×(8T), skewed.zst9 1.57×, our-encoder skewed 2.10×(4T)/2.60×(8T); 32 MiB matrix json.zst3 mt4 1.56×/mt16 1.67× ours-ST (0.71-1.03× before), skewed.zst9 1.21-1.28× (was 0.71×) — skewed now at parity with zstd's ST stream reference, json above it from mt4.
- **stage-B parallelization is falsified for typical frames** (see [negative.md](../negative.md)): piece watermarks with exact per-sequence source gating were implemented and byte-correct, but the shallowest near-boundary read (offset ~100-400 B: nearest matches, repcodes) chains pieces into a serial pipeline — the min-offset at any cut, not the window, sets the stagger. Stage A/B overlap already exists for free in the serial-B design (the calling thread executes segment i while workers stage i+1..), which is why the piece machinery was a net regression and was reverted. Remaining real levers landed instead: wildcopy staged execution (`65e4b98`), pooled staging buffers (`3c850d3`).
- **encoder-side deep-offset ramp (2026-09-15, `match_generator::RampGate`, env `ZSTDX_MT_RAMP_BYTES`)**: gated MT jobs reject every match whose source lies within D bytes below the job start (DEPTH semantics — the fixed-offset-floor variant is falsified in [negative.md](../negative.md)), covering all 9 probe paths plus the backward-extension floor (the covert leak, also in negative.md). Zero guarantee violations under the executor-truth audit (`piecepipe --depth`, bulk and growing-grid stream frames), and the ratio cost is ~zero where D clears the shape's match period: json D=2 MiB −0.72% (suppressed crossing matches were weak), skewed D=2 MiB −0.02%, text D=512 KiB +1.27% — but text at D=2 MiB +403% (its ~800 KiB period falls inside the band; D must stay under the dominant period). Piece-pipeline critical-path ceilings on ramp frames: json 8.0× (no crossing reads at all — D equals the level-3 window, so no legal crossing exists; piece-count bound), skewed 1.99×, text 1.94×. Ramp frames decode byte-identically and at speed parity through the serial-B decoder (json mt4 ~2000 MiB/s ≈ 1.46× ours-ST). ~~Still open: the piece-parallel stage B~~ **landed 2026-09-16 — see the next bullet**; per-job content-adaptive D stays open.
- **piece-parallel stage B landed (2026-09-16, `decoding/mt_pieces.rs`, env-paired with the ramp)**: the decoder spends the ramp guarantee, and engagement is **measured, never trusted**. Routing (both bulk MT entry points, gated on `ZSTDX_MT_RAMP_BYTES` plus a single pledged frame of 2-64 MiB — the pledge partitions the output up front and bounds staging memory) runs stage A to completion, then plans candidate job grids from the job-size formula's lattice (`ceil(fcs/2w)` over w=2..32 plus the overlap-raised 2/3/4/6/8 MiB, ascending; every `k·J` must land on a block start). Each candidate's pieces are validated standalone **in parallel**: a walk from a sentinel repcode history (`[u32::MAX; 3]` — never-written slots resolve far beyond any legal offset, so any dependence on pre-piece state rejects the grid; true job starts pass by the encoder's rep gate) measures every piece's deepest external read top (+16 wildcopy read slack), and engagement demands every piece ≥1 keep that top ≥ 3/8 of the job size below its start (pipeline ceiling ≥ 1.6×). Wrong candidates die on the first violating piece (the validation round aborts early), so the lattice's reject cost stays near one piece walk. Validated pieces execute on the worker pool + caller: in-order claiming (the watermark dependency chain cannot deadlock — the minimal claimed piece never waits), one start-gate per piece on the contiguous-executed watermark, wildcopy bounded by the piece end (parallel pieces never write into a neighbor), and the frame checksum absorbed in piece order under the coordination mutex (own-range absorbs stay hot; a foreign range is absorbed by whichever thread unblocks the order). Validation failure runs the serial stage B over the same staged segments, byte-identical to `decode_parallel`.
  - effects (32 MiB corpus, D=2 MiB, checksummed frames, interleaved medians): **json.lvl3 mt8 5122 vs 1599 MiB/s serial-B (3.2×; 3.5× own ST 1466)**, mt4 2.4×; **dll32.lvl3 mt8 2.6-2.9×, mt4 1.8×** (3.5× own ST); skewed.lvl9 1.0-1.1× (entropy-bound — stage B was never its bottleneck); ratio cost +0.00% on all three shapes at D=2 MiB (plain-mt size identical). Tests: `tests/mt_piece_decode.rs` (own binary — the override knobs are process-global and would poison parallel unit tests).
  - **falsified: restart-position grid discovery** — job boundaries are restart blocks in theory, but this encoder re-sends entropy tables densely (json.lvl3: 92 of 128 blocks are restart points), so the restart lattice carries no job-grid signal and false grids (a 1.75 MiB periodic alignment) pass the multiples check with near-boundary crossing reads; grid candidates come from the formula lattice instead, and the restart scan stays for segment cutting only.
  - open: the staging **barrier** — stage A completes before any execution, losing the serial path's A/B overlap (another reason the router stays env-gated; the no-regression form is optimistic serial execution with a mid-flight switch to pieces at a segment boundary); mt16 plateaus at mt8's level on json (5173 vs 5122 MiB/s — the staging+validation serial fraction); high-ratio shapes never reach any MT decode through bulk (text 32 MiB → ~100 KB compressed, under the 512 KB `MIN_MT_INPUT` floor — a floor rethink is its own lever); unpledged streams stay excluded (the growing grid's tail split depends on the encoder's worker count).
- pitfall (fixed 2026-09-15): `decode_to_vec_mt`'s place callback assumed a fresh vector — base = `as_mut_ptr()` (execution coordinates are append-relative), `reserve(end - start_len)` (short by `start_len`), limit `capacity + start_len`. Appending behind a non-empty prefix overwrote it and wrote past the allocation; invisible to the suite because every caller passed a fresh Vec. Regression test appends behind a 1 MiB prefix (heap corruption only surfaced under MALLOC_CHECK_/valgrind — the overflowing writes usually landed in allocator slack).
- frame checksums are verified (2026-09-15) **inline in stage B** (2026-09-15 rework): the pre-scan collects each frame's trailer word and the executor absorbs each executed segment's range into the frame's xxh64 stream the moment it is final (`decoding/frame_checksum.rs` — bytes are hot in the executor's caches, so the hash costs its ~1.6 ms/32 MiB ALU work without a second memory pass; stage A workers keep staging ahead during the absorb), finishing and comparing at frame closes, erroring `ChecksumMismatch` like the ST trailer-read compare (previously *no* path compared; the ST paths fold it into the trailer read). Two placements are falsified with data ([negative.md](../negative.md)): a sequential post-pass over the assembled output (reads cold — up to +7.6 ms on skewed whose later decode traffic evicted the output), and a dedicated concurrent hasher thread (on L3-capacity-bound json its 32 MiB read stream runs at DRAM speed *and* slows the decoder's own misses: instructions equal, cycles +14%, wall +33% — while skewed/text hide it fully; concurrent placement is shape-dependent and loses where the post-pass was cheapest). Locked 32 MiB matrix, serial post-pass → inline: json mt4 1871→2029 / mt16 1996→2200 MiB/s (1.33×→1.44× / 1.41×→1.56× ours-ST — the pre-checksum scaling is back), skewed mt4 695→756 (1.07×→1.16×), text unchanged. Frames without the checksum flag skip verification entirely.

## Streaming encode/decode (ST)

- streaming encoders (write::Encoder / read::Encoder) share the incremental core and the same matcher/block-encoder building blocks as bulk; with no intermediate flush the output is byte-identical to `encoding::compress` (asserted across write chunkings). `51ec2fc`
- for the streaming-decode flat outBuff model and `StreamingDecoder` read-granularity optimizations, see the decoding page.
- read/write Decoders are transparent to multi-frame and skippable frames (`single_frame()` restores single-frame semantics); the write side stages a complete block header+body+trailer before handing to FrameDecoder (starved reads would poison the state).

## Streaming encoding MT (burst model) `44e11e5` `27b91cf`

- `FrameEncoderCore` becomes enum `Single(Box<FrameEncoderCoreSt>) | Mt(MtEncoderCore)` (std-gated), zero changes to the read/write paths; no_std workers>1 stays Unsupported; routing (single core / raw level / Uncompressed → Single) matches bulk.
- one contiguous buffer `[window strip retained from the previous burst][unencoded data]` (no memcpy sliding window) + **absolute job boundaries derived from the stream offset alone** (see the JobGrid bullet below) → without flush, output is independent of how writes are chunked (deterministic; flush is a documented exception that rebases the grid).
- a burst fires once max(workers,2) complete jobs have accumulated: scope parallelism + main thread absorbs checksums in parallel + in-order assembly + poison propagation; a single-job tail runs inline without spawning.
- **pledged**: jobs cut with the shared bulk job-size formula + holding back the last job so finish can mark it last → byte-identical to bulk MT (32MiB measured, sizes fully equal). Cost: that job runs inline single-threaded inside finish — fast tier 0.55-0.78× slower, best tier actually 1.24-1.28× faster (big jobs amortize per-job fixed costs). hold-back must carry the `pos <= n` guard (on pledge overshoot, pending grows unboundedly).
- **unpledged JobGrid::Growing = quantized epochs**: jobs quantize into epochs of `burst_jobs` equal jobs, each epoch doubling the job size from the bulk floor to the cap; bursts fire exactly on epoch completion, so a burst is one epoch of equal-size jobs and the barrier never idles behind a straggler. The finish/flush tail (the only range that can land mid-epoch, `hi = stream end`, itself a pure function of the input) is re-sliced into ≤workers equal jobs sized ≥MIN_JOB_SIZE, so the tail burst fills the workers instead of running 2-4 schedule-sized jobs on 8 threads. History: grid v1 (fixed floor, pre-`946dd2f`) paid a boundary restart at the floor forever; grid v2 (per-job geometric growth `job_size_for(4o)`, `946dd2f`) bought json.opt −0.30%/ultra −0.28%/text.balanced −0.086% but put ~1.25×-apart jobs into one 8-job burst — utilization sum/(8·max) ≈ 0.52, halving every stream-MT cell (the 2026-09-12 regression). The epoch grid (v3, 2026-09-12) holds ratio within ±0.1% of v2 (mt8: json.ultra −0.32% denser, json.opt −0.02%; mt4: json/skewed balanced/best +0.05-0.09% sparser) while restoring the speed: matrix mt8 json.fastest 961→1423 MiB/s (44% of bulk ceiling, was 30%), json.fast 724→1248 (53%), json.balanced 175→247 (94%), text.balanced 982→1546 (70%), text.fast 1991→2279 (15%). text.fastest (2049→2363, 12% of ceiling, 1.8× behind zstd) and json.fastest (1.26×) remain bounded by the burst model itself, not the schedule: accumulate (one serial 16 KiB-pump memcpy pass), per-burst thread spawn and the barrier cannot overlap encoding — the overlapped producer-consumer pool is the persistent-pool redesign (todo 12).
- **epoch-sized buffer reservation + MADV_HUGEPAGE** (grid v3, same day): the accumulate buffer reserves the whole next epoch at each burst (one exact realloc/mremap instead of a doubling ladder) and is `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)`-advised over its capacity — on this THP=**madvise** machine the dense first touch otherwise pays one 4 KiB fault per page (~3 ms/iter at 32 MiB; solo text.fastest 2464→3706 MiB/s, json.fastest 1342→1567). THP=always machines map huge regardless (the original falsification's premise, see negative.md); the advice targets exactly the one buffer, not a global policy.
- pitfall (grid v3, fixed same day): a flush re-grids `job_start` to a mid-epoch offset, so the job holding it legitimately crosses the epoch floor — `encode_jobs` clamps it to `min(job_end, aligned)` and the short job re-aligns the walk (a burst must never straddle an epoch). The v3 assert `next == job_end(...)` assumed an untouched grid and fired deterministically on flush→burst (caught by flush_interleaved_roundtrip in debug only; release took the clamp all along). Grid invariants must be stated relative to a possibly-off-grid start: the kept invariant is "whenever the epoch floor lies ahead of job_start the walk lands on it exactly".
- **encoder-owned cross-burst worker-state pool** (`Mutex<Vec<Box<CompressState>>>`): thread::scope spawns fresh threads every burst so the thread_local pool always misses (Balanced 8MiB tables + first-touch page faults); the deep tier gains +22-30% from this. A panicked state may be mid-compress garbage — discard, do not return it.
- results (2026-09-12, post epoch-grid + tail re-slice + buffer advice): json.fast stream-mt8 3.1× vs zstd (1248 vs 400 MiB/s); json-class vs its own streaming ST and full data in the [matrix page](../bench/matrix.md).
- **persistent burst worker pool** (2026-09-14): burst threads used to be a fresh `thread::scope` set per burst (~0.5-2ms spawn+join each); the pool now spawns once at the first multi-job burst and parks on a generation condvar between bursts. Per-burst job loop unchanged (atomic pickup, poison check, pooled states), plus an `exited` countdown that gives the posting thread a quiesce point — the freeze the scope used to guarantee — before it mutates/moves/drops the accumulate buffer. Output byte-identical; wall neutral alone on the 32MiB bench (spawn was not the bound at 3 bursts/stream — the serial accumulate pump and the strip-prefill volume are, see below), but the pool is the substrate for overlapping accumulation with an in-flight burst.
- **overlapped accumulation (producer-consumer on the pool, 2026-09-14)**: `encode_jobs` splits into `fire_jobs` (post the burst, hash, return) and `drain_inflight` (ordered assembly + quiesce + trim + panic surfacing); the steady `write` path fires and keeps pumping while the workers encode, so epoch N+1's accumulate overlaps burst N instead of serializing behind its barrier. Two invariants make the overlap memory-safe: appends during a burst are confined to the reserved window (the next epoch is `reserve_exact`-ed *before* posting, so the frozen buffer never reallocs under the workers), and the prefix drain (`try_compact`) runs as soon as the exit countdown hits zero — an acquire load of the same quiesce the drain waits for. Lesson: the first draft deferred the trim to the next fire, which made the prefix drain move a whole epoch of pending bytes per burst (~1 extra full-data copy); compacting at the first quiesced write keeps it at strip scale. Output byte-identical (jobs and boundaries unchanged — the split is pure scheduling). Effects: json.fastest stream-mt8 1475→1958 MiB/s (x1.19→x0.90, now ahead of zstd); text.fastest's fast mode ~2322→~4900 solo, but the interleaved A/B bench stays bimodal — the slow mode's mechanism is diagnosed in the next bullet.
- **pooled accumulate buffers (2026-09-14)**: the interleaved A/B bench's bimodality (the text fast/balanced stream-mt cells swinging 2-3× between passes, a months-old open question) is diagnosed: each fresh encoder maps a ~34MiB accumulate buffer, and under the bench's alternating allocations the THP=madvise allocator (57d uptime, fragmented physical memory) falls back to 4KiB pages — measured per round with getrusage: solo 809 minflt vs interleaved 2622, and the +1800 faults ≈ +3ms ≈ exactly the fast/slow gap; `perf stat` shows whole-process faults doubling (29k→59k). THP=never collapses solo to the slow mode too, confirming the fault path is the bound (a machine without THP pressure would not reproduce). Fix: a thread-local pool of accumulate buffers (depth 2, keep ≤64MiB) hands each new encoder the previous stream's already-faulted pages (interleaved minflt 2622→126); buffer contents never affect output (bytes arrive via `write`), so output stays byte-identical — the full ratio sweep diffs clean cell-for-cell against the pre-change build. Effects (matrix enc-stream mt8, interleaved A/B): json.fastest 1475→2600 MiB/s (x1.19→x0.72 vs zstd), text.fastest 2322→5598 (x1.87→x0.75), json.fast x0.31→x0.22, text.fast x0.78→x0.39 — all four cells ahead of zstd, and the between-pass swing is gone.
- **fastest-tier strip fill capped to the retention tail + confirmed seed** (2026-09-14, `3ba7335`+`27e8d52`): the Fast strategy's strip grid fill covered the whole window (768 KiB at Fastest = 256K inserts into 32K single-candidate slots, ~8 overwrites per slot), although entries beyond the retention horizon are overwritten before the scan probes — the fill now covers only `slots*stride*2` = 192 KiB of the strip tail (prefill micro 124-218→30-88 µs/strip). **The cap is safe only because the seed got a confirmation rule in the same round**: on a period≈window corpus the cross-job twins (text tiles, period 441226 < window 786432) hash their slot once per period and *survive* newest-wins — they are not churn — while the old nearest-first seed rule could lose them to any ≥56-byte local repeat near the strip tail (measured: a 194-byte duplicated source statement at offset 2244 beat the tile period, burned the seed budget, and collapsed its job from ~200 B to 55183 B; the full fill had masked this since `a37ebaa`). Seeds must now agree at spread anchors `[64, 640, 6144]` behind the 56-byte window (one u64 compare each). Effects: ratio sweep json.fastest mt cells 0.07-0.11% **denser**, skewed ±0.0005%, every other cell byte-identical; speed json.fastest.mt8 3375→3477-3499, text.fastest stream-mt8 5377→5647-5944 (x0.79→0.70), json.fastest stream-mt8 x0.71→0.69. Lesson: the stream-mt8 fastest cells are **pump/burst-bound, not prefill-bound** — the 56%-of-input prefill volume was a cycle-share number, not the wall bound; the job-side strip saving lands at its serial fraction only (text +5-10%, json +3%). The fastest tier's remaining per-job strip cost is the seed scan itself (full-strip walk, 60-130 µs — inherent for period≈window shapes whose twin sits deep in the strip).
- **job-queue pipelining — the burst barrier's replacement (2026-09-16)**: the burst model's per-epoch barrier (firing epoch N+1 waited for N's assembly) serialized the pump against the encode span. The pool is now a posted-job queue: an epoch's jobs post the moment the epoch is fully buffered (the growing grid's gate is re-checked per posted job — a large write or an oversized pooled buffer can carry `pos` past several epoch ends in one append, and greedily posting an incomplete epoch's jobs changes the stream-end tail re-slice and the bytes; caught by `growing_grid_roundtrip`), workers claim from the front and keep a worker-local `CompressState` across jobs, assembly drains whatever completed at each post, and the fixed (pledged) grid posts each job at its own completion (its `build_bounds` never re-slices, so cadence cannot affect bytes; the last-job hold-back stays). Buffer recycling is quiesce-gated: the dead-prefix wrap (an in-buffer move whose destination overlaps live job views) and growth (which may relocate the allocation under resolved `FrozenSrc` pointers) run only once every posted job has completed, so the pump stalls on the workers at most once per epoch, bounded by the in-flight encode span; growth doubles up to 256 MiB, past which the wrap alone recycles. The read-side pump kept a persistent 16 KiB staging chunk (64/128 KiB pulls measured slower — see negative.md); superseded the next day by the single-copy direct pump below. A completion burst (the finish tail's 6-8 jobs) that lands exactly between the caller's `n_incomplete` read and the waiter's `completed` snapshot fires its `notify_all` before the waiter parks; the snapshot is then already final and the wait's only exit (`completed != before`) never fires — the 100 ms timeout re-checks the same stale condition, sleeping forever at 0% CPU with every worker parked (the exact captured signature; reproduces on an idle machine within a few matrix runs, dodges under load — which is why 10k stress streams stayed clean while single benchmark runs hung). Fix: the wait re-evaluates `n_incomplete == 0` on every wake and re-snapshots per iteration. The ST core keeps the chunked pump (its copies hide behind the encode span; the block-staged buffer is small). `std::io::Read::read_buf` would be the cleaner tool but is still unstable.

- **cross-encoder worker-thread pool (2026-09-17, r8)**: the per-encoder thread lifecycle was the fixed cost the read-pump round left standing — every encoder spawned its workers at the first post and joined them at drop, serially on the pump thread. Attribution first: a spawn+park+notify+join round of 8 default-stack threads measures ~130 us (clone plus stack guard-page setup dominates; the queue is a condvar either way), the slot handoff that replaces it ~10 us, so the cost is recoverable. The pool follows the r7 process-level precedents (worker `STATE_POOL`, the donation kit) at the thread level: a worker whose lease ends parks into a global slot pool (`THREAD_POOL`, depth 32 — one parked thread per slot, `Parked`/`Serving`/`Retiring` under the slot's own mutex+condvar), and the next encoder's `ensure_workers` pops parked slots and hands each a lease on its queue, spawning fresh only for the remainder. Semantics kept: drop sets shutdown and waits every lease out of its queue (`wait_leave` with a pointer check — a recycled slot may already serve a later encoder; this is the join equivalent, holding until no thread can touch the encoder's buffer), a worker whose own job panicked retires (never reuse a thread that has seen an unwind; its state is dropped, not pooled), the thread body wraps the lease machine in a catch_unwind that withdraws and retires the slot if the machinery itself panics (no dropper can wait on a dead thread, no future lease lands on one), and a parked thread retires after 5 s idle, withdrawing itself from the pool first so the pop/retire race resolves under the slot lock. The pool mutex and a slot mutex are never held together, so none of the park/assign/retire races can deadlock. The worker-local `CompressState` now rides the thread across leases instead of round-tripping the state pool (provenance cannot reach the bytes — every job clears what it reads; the deep tiers' table builds stay pooled for cold starts and the inline path).
