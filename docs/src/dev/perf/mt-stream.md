# Performance Optimization · Multithreading and Streaming

## MT encoding (bulk slice path) `fd931a1`

**job model**: single-frame overlap jobs (stage-2; frame-per-job was rejected — multi-frame output is a burden on the decode side). Input is split into jobs (shared formula `job_size_for`: floor 1MiB, split evenly by workers, cap 1GiB — the same cap as libzstd zstdmt); `std::thread::scope` + atomic-counter task pickup + condvar join; worker state goes through the thread_local pool for reuse across calls; the main thread writes the frame header (FCS=total length) + assembles the job block streams in order + absorbs checksums in parallel; worker panics are recovered by the calling thread after assembly drains.

**three conditions for job independence** (at each job start, cross-checked against zstdmt):

1. entropy tables reset (last_huff/the three previous = None → expressed explicitly in the block header, legal);
2. repcode sentinel `rep=[u32::MAX;3]` (the probe is guaranteed to fail out-of-bounds; after 3 literal-offset sequences it converges naturally with the decoder's rep state; ST output stays byte-identical once the hot loop got the `rep_pending` gate);
3. overlap prefix prefill (see below).

**ratio preservation** (once the biggest defect: text periodic corpus mt16 ratio 328.8→19.7, zstd-mt 189):

- overlap=window and strips **actually prefilled into the tables** — previously strips were only borrowed as the window while the insertion path inserted only in-job positions, making cross-job matches mathematically impossible. `a37ebaa`
- fast/dfast/chain strip table-filling on a **stride-3 grid** (`4bba04a`; chain deviating from libzstd's dense dictionary filling is intentional: our strips = the full window, dense filling ≈ half a job's scan duration); chain stores got the gain gate to keep strips from flooding in 5-byte garbage matches.
- **periodic seed at job start**: during prefill, a backward scan over the strip tail finds the nearest long-repeat distance O and the job start directly probes `pos-O` (after 3 hits rep[0]=O and the repcode chain takes over; dead seeds retire after an 8192-probe budget). Periodic corpora across three levels mt/st 2.7-2.8→1.00. `a37ebaa`
- **determinism**: head tables are cleared per job (pooled state residue made output depend on worker scheduling, ±0.06% jitter; a landmine buried by the u32 no-clear design, previously masked by dense filling); chain lists need no clearing (provable by induction: candidates come only from cleared heads or this job's linked values) → clearing halved; clears ≥4MiB use NT stores. `a6cf8a6`
- **AVX-512 seed scan**: occupancy bitmap (64B chunks × 8 overlapping loads assemble one bit per position, taking bits top-down = exact equivalent of the scalar nearest-first order, output unchanged); random-class 0.45→0.06-0.13 ms/job (4-7×). `e1c6d67`
- **Ultra job-boundary statistics seeding**: the per-job `opt_state` reset was the entire remaining boundary ratio cost — a sequential in-order-carry decomposition (json.ultra, 4×8MiB jobs, every variant roundtrip-gated) put ~104KiB of the 85KiB mt-vs-st loss on the stats alone vs 1.3KiB entropy restart + 2.6KiB rep gate + 3.1KiB tree refill. Mechanism is a price-feedback basin, not miscalibrated histograms: a BASE-priced parse with the 8MiB strip reachable chooses near-offset/ll0 chain shapes, `updateStats` reinforces them, and the job never leaves that basin (carried stats show 37% rep0 / ml=12 / ll∈{1,2}; reset jobs parse 10% rep0 / ml=7 / ll0). Fix (2026-09-12): the job's first parsed block runs a throwaway parse of the strip tail (two blocks) with the window re-based to the span — candidates and fills clamp inside it, reproducing the empty-window frame start — purely to seed the stats; the real parse then re-fills the strip from `win_base` (seed-span re-insertion is benign: the head rejects the duplicate position). json.ultra bulk-mt/stream-mt −1.85%→−0.13%; ST dump byte-identical; mt wall unchanged (json 4.1 MiB/s best-of-5). Seeding the same span with the strip reachable recovers nothing at any span size — see [negative.md](../negative.md).

Results: 64MiB text 2/4/8 workers = 2.05×/3.95×/7.55× (ratio loss <0.1%); 32MiB matrix mt8/16 speed 2-4.5× ahead of zstd-mt, and after the ratio fix every mt16 cell returned to ST ±0.5%.

## MT decoding (exclusive dimension: libzstd has no MT decode) `3219947`

- **restart-point segmentation**: block headers are pre-scanned and segments cut where entropy state is self-describing (literals not Treeless, no Repeat FSE streams); job-style encoders (zstd `-T` and our MT encoder) produce exactly these points at every job boundary; multi-frame input = a special case of clean-start units, unified in one unit dispatcher.
- stage A (worker pool) entropy-decodes in parallel, producing packed sequence streams + exact output sizes; stage B (calling thread, in input order) executes into the output and carries the rep history — the repcode is the only cross-sequence state and never touches stage A.
- dictionary frames, malformed, single restart, single core → serial fallback (error reporting belongs to the serial path).
- **current status**: 64MiB 2.06×(4T)/2.10×(8T) with no scaling beyond; stage B serial occupies 45-80% (skewed.zst9 match copies 80%), and stage A parallelism only adds noise. Parallelizing stage B (reachback analysis + rep history prefix scan) is the biggest TODO — done well it jumps from a 0.66-1.0× liability to exclusive leadership.

## Streaming encode/decode (ST)

- streaming encoders (write::Encoder / read::Encoder) share the incremental core and the same matcher/block-encoder building blocks as bulk; with no intermediate flush the output is byte-identical to `encoding::compress` (asserted across write chunkings). `51ec2fc`
- for the streaming-decode flat outBuff model and `StreamingDecoder` read-granularity optimizations, see the decoding page.
- read/write Decoders are transparent to multi-frame and skippable frames (`single_frame()` restores single-frame semantics); the write side stages a complete block header+body+trailer before handing to FrameDecoder (starved reads would poison the state).

## Streaming encoding MT (burst model) `44e11e5` `27b91cf`

- `FrameEncoderCore` becomes enum `Single(Box<FrameEncoderCoreSt>) | Mt(MtEncoderCore)` (std-gated), zero changes to the read/write paths; no_std workers>1 stays Unsupported; routing (single core / raw level / Uncompressed → Single) matches bulk.
- one contiguous buffer `[window strip retained from the previous burst][unencoded data]` (no memcpy sliding window) + **absolute job boundaries derived from the stream offset alone** (see the JobGrid bullet below) → without flush, output is independent of how writes are chunked (deterministic; flush is a documented exception that rebases the grid).
- a burst fires once max(workers,2) complete jobs have accumulated: scope parallelism + main thread absorbs checksums in parallel + in-order assembly + poison propagation; a single-job tail runs inline without spawning.
- **pledged**: jobs cut with the shared bulk job-size formula + holding back the last job so finish can mark it last → byte-identical to bulk MT (32MiB measured, sizes fully equal). Cost: that job runs inline single-threaded inside finish — fast tier 0.55-0.78× slower, best tier actually 1.24-1.28× faster (big jobs amortize per-job fixed costs). hold-back must carry the `pos <= n` guard (on pledge overshoot, pending grows unboundedly).
- **unpledged JobGrid::Growing = quantized epochs**: jobs quantize into epochs of `burst_jobs` equal jobs, each epoch doubling the job size from the bulk floor to the cap; bursts fire exactly on epoch completion, so a burst is one epoch of equal-size jobs and the barrier never idles behind a straggler. The finish/flush tail (the only range that can land mid-epoch, `hi = stream end`, itself a pure function of the input) is re-sliced into ≤workers equal jobs sized ≥MIN_JOB_SIZE, so the tail burst fills the workers instead of running 2-4 schedule-sized jobs on 8 threads. History: grid v1 (fixed floor, pre-`946dd2f`) paid a boundary restart at the floor forever; grid v2 (per-job geometric growth `job_size_for(4o)`, `946dd2f`) bought json.opt −0.30%/ultra −0.28%/text.balanced −0.086% but put ~1.25×-apart jobs into one 8-job burst — utilization sum/(8·max) ≈ 0.52, halving every stream-MT cell (the 2026-09-12 regression). The epoch grid (v3, 2026-09-12) holds ratio within ±0.1% of v2 (mt8: json.ultra −0.32% denser, json.opt −0.02%; mt4: json/skewed balanced/best +0.05-0.09% sparser) while restoring the speed: matrix mt8 json.fastest 961→1423 MiB/s (44% of bulk ceiling, was 30%), json.fast 724→1248 (53%), json.balanced 175→247 (94%), text.balanced 982→1546 (70%), text.fast 1991→2279 (15%). text.fastest (2049→2363, 12% of ceiling, 1.8× behind zstd) and json.fastest (1.26×) remain bounded by the burst model itself, not the schedule: accumulate (one serial 16 KiB-pump memcpy pass), per-burst thread spawn and the barrier cannot overlap encoding — the overlapped producer-consumer pool is the persistent-pool redesign (todo 13).
- **epoch-sized buffer reservation + MADV_HUGEPAGE** (grid v3, same day): the accumulate buffer reserves the whole next epoch at each burst (one exact realloc/mremap instead of a doubling ladder) and is `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)`-advised over its capacity — on this THP=**madvise** machine the dense first touch otherwise pays one 4 KiB fault per page (~3 ms/iter at 32 MiB; solo text.fastest 2464→3706 MiB/s, json.fastest 1342→1567). THP=always machines map huge regardless (the original falsification's premise, see negative.md); the advice targets exactly the one buffer, not a global policy.
- pitfall (grid v3, fixed same day): a flush re-grids `job_start` to a mid-epoch offset, so the job holding it legitimately crosses the epoch floor — `encode_jobs` clamps it to `min(job_end, aligned)` and the short job re-aligns the walk (a burst must never straddle an epoch). The v3 assert `next == job_end(...)` assumed an untouched grid and fired deterministically on flush→burst (caught by flush_interleaved_roundtrip in debug only; release took the clamp all along). Grid invariants must be stated relative to a possibly-off-grid start: the kept invariant is "whenever the epoch floor lies ahead of job_start the walk lands on it exactly".
- **encoder-owned cross-burst worker-state pool** (`Mutex<Vec<Box<CompressState>>>`): thread::scope spawns fresh threads every burst so the thread_local pool always misses (Balanced 8MiB tables + first-touch page faults); the deep tier gains +22-30% from this. A panicked state may be mid-compress garbage — discard, do not return it.
- results (2026-09-12, post epoch-grid + tail re-slice + buffer advice): json.fast stream-mt8 3.1× vs zstd (1248 vs 400 MiB/s); json-class vs its own streaming ST and full data in the [matrix page](../bench/matrix.md).
- **persistent burst worker pool** (2026-09-14): burst threads used to be a fresh `thread::scope` set per burst (~0.5-2ms spawn+join each); the pool now spawns once at the first multi-job burst and parks on a generation condvar between bursts. Per-burst job loop unchanged (atomic pickup, poison check, pooled states), plus an `exited` countdown that gives the posting thread a quiesce point — the freeze the scope used to guarantee — before it mutates/moves/drops the accumulate buffer. Output byte-identical; wall neutral alone on the 32MiB bench (spawn was not the bound at 3 bursts/stream — the serial accumulate pump and the strip-prefill volume are, see below), but the pool is the substrate for overlapping accumulation with an in-flight burst.
- **overlapped accumulation (producer-consumer on the pool, 2026-09-14)**: `encode_jobs` splits into `fire_jobs` (post the burst, hash, return) and `drain_inflight` (ordered assembly + quiesce + trim + panic surfacing); the steady `write` path fires and keeps pumping while the workers encode, so epoch N+1's accumulate overlaps burst N instead of serializing behind its barrier. Two invariants make the overlap memory-safe: appends during a burst are confined to the reserved window (the next epoch is `reserve_exact`-ed *before* posting, so the frozen buffer never reallocs under the workers), and the prefix drain (`try_compact`) runs as soon as the exit countdown hits zero — an acquire load of the same quiesce the drain waits for. Lesson: the first draft deferred the trim to the next fire, which made the prefix drain move a whole epoch of pending bytes per burst (~1 extra full-data copy); compacting at the first quiesced write keeps it at strip scale. Output byte-identical (jobs and boundaries unchanged — the split is pure scheduling). Effects: json.fastest stream-mt8 1475→1958 MiB/s (x1.19→x0.90, now ahead of zstd); text.fastest's fast mode ~2322→~4900 solo, but the interleaved A/B bench stays bimodal — the slow mode's mechanism is diagnosed in the next bullet.
- **pooled accumulate buffers (2026-09-14)**: the interleaved A/B bench's bimodality (the text fast/balanced stream-mt cells swinging 2-3× between passes, a months-old open question) is diagnosed: each fresh encoder maps a ~34MiB accumulate buffer, and under the bench's alternating allocations the THP=madvise allocator (57d uptime, fragmented physical memory) falls back to 4KiB pages — measured per round with getrusage: solo 809 minflt vs interleaved 2622, and the +1800 faults ≈ +3ms ≈ exactly the fast/slow gap; `perf stat` shows whole-process faults doubling (29k→59k). THP=never collapses solo to the slow mode too, confirming the fault path is the bound (a machine without THP pressure would not reproduce). Fix: a thread-local pool of accumulate buffers (depth 2, keep ≤64MiB) hands each new encoder the previous stream's already-faulted pages (interleaved minflt 2622→126); buffer contents never affect output (bytes arrive via `write`), so output stays byte-identical — the full ratio sweep diffs clean cell-for-cell against the pre-change build. Effects (matrix enc-stream mt8, interleaved A/B): json.fastest 1475→2600 MiB/s (x1.19→x0.72 vs zstd), text.fastest 2322→5598 (x1.87→x0.75), json.fast x0.31→x0.22, text.fast x0.78→x0.39 — all four cells ahead of zstd, and the between-pass swing is gone.
- **fastest-tier strip fill capped to the retention tail + confirmed seed** (2026-09-14, `3ba7335`+`27e8d52`): the Fast strategy's strip grid fill covered the whole window (768 KiB at Fastest = 256K inserts into 32K single-candidate slots, ~8 overwrites per slot), although entries beyond the retention horizon are overwritten before the scan probes — the fill now covers only `slots*stride*2` = 192 KiB of the strip tail (prefill micro 124-218→30-88 µs/strip). **The cap is safe only because the seed got a confirmation rule in the same round**: on a period≈window corpus the cross-job twins (text tiles, period 441226 < window 786432) hash their slot once per period and *survive* newest-wins — they are not churn — while the old nearest-first seed rule could lose them to any ≥56-byte local repeat near the strip tail (measured: a 194-byte duplicated source statement at offset 2244 beat the tile period, burned the seed budget, and collapsed its job from ~200 B to 55183 B; the full fill had masked this since `a37ebaa`). Seeds must now agree at spread anchors `[64, 640, 6144]` behind the 56-byte window (one u64 compare each). Effects: ratio sweep json.fastest mt cells 0.07-0.11% **denser**, skewed ±0.0005%, every other cell byte-identical; speed json.fastest.mt8 3375→3477-3499, text.fastest stream-mt8 5377→5647-5944 (x0.79→0.70), json.fastest stream-mt8 x0.71→0.69. Lesson: the stream-mt8 fastest cells are **pump/burst-bound, not prefill-bound** — the 56%-of-input prefill volume was a cycle-share number, not the wall bound; the job-side strip saving lands at its serial fraction only (text +5-10%, json +3%). The fastest tier's remaining per-job strip cost is the seed scan itself (full-strip walk, 60-130 µs — inherent for period≈window shapes whose twin sits deep in the strip).
- **remaining structural gaps** (all verified as non-bugs, see the TODO): read::Encoder pumping double-copies 16KiB (reading directly into an uninitialized buffer violates the Read trait contract; evaluated and dropped) — this pump plus the burst barrier is the measured bound of the fastest/fast stream cells above; the checksum absorb stays on the calling thread (serial ~10% of cycles with checksums on — movable into a pool job later); the per-job NT table-clear fixed cost behind zeros.Balanced 0.8× (low priority). Epoch-growth/job-size rescheduling (e.g. ×4 epochs) was deliberately left untested this round — the pump bound caps its payoff.
