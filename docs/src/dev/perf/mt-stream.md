# Multithreading and streaming

This page records the multithreading and streaming work: the encode job model, the streaming buffer model, worker scheduling, the decode parallelism, and how dictionaries interact with each. It answers where the time went, which structures survived, and which measurements are final. Every change below was gated on byte-identical output unless a size change is stated.

## Encode job model

The bulk MT path splits one frame into overlap jobs, and the frame-per-job layout was rejected because multi-frame output burdens the decode side. `job_size_for` splits the input with a 1 MiB floor, an even split by worker count and a 1 GiB cap that matches libzstd's zstdmt. A job runs under `std::thread::scope` with atomic-counter pickup and a condvar join, and worker state comes from a thread-local pool. The calling thread writes the frame header with the full content size, assembles the job block streams in order and absorbs checksums in parallel, and a worker panic is recovered after assembly drains. The commit is `fd931a1`.

A job start must satisfy three conditions, all cross-checked against zstdmt. Entropy tables reset, so the last Huffman table and the three previous tables are None and the block header states this explicitly. The repcode sentinel is `rep=[u32::MAX;3]`, which fails the probe out of bounds and converges with the decoder's rep state after three literal-offset sequences, and the single-thread output stays byte-identical once the hot loop has the `rep_pending` gate. The overlap prefix is prefilled into the tables, as described below.

### Ratio preservation

The original job model lost ratio badly: text with a periodic corpus read mt16 328.8 against 19.7 bytes per input unit and zstd-mt read 189. Overlap and strips were borrowed as the window while the insertion path inserted only in-job positions, so cross-job matches were mathematically impossible. Strips are now actually prefilled, in `a37ebaa`. The fast, dfast and chain rows fill their strip tables on a stride-3 grid in `4bba04a`, and chain deviating from libzstd's dense dictionary filling is intentional because our strips are the full window and dense filling costs about half a job's scan. Chain stores also got the gain gate so strips do not flood the tables with 5-byte garbage matches.

A periodic seed now runs at each job start. During prefill, a backward scan over the strip tail finds the nearest long-repeat distance O, and the job start probes `pos-O`. After three hits `rep[0]` becomes O and the repcode chain takes over, and dead seeds retire after an 8192-probe budget. Periodic corpora across three levels moved from 2.7-2.8 to 1.00 for both mt and st. The commit is `a37ebaa`.

The Best tier prefills under the DUBT finder through the finder's own O(1) fill at 3 stores per position after a head-table clear, measured on 2026-09-14. The tree restarts from the bucket chains and the first searches' batch sort rebuilds it, and because the strip is the full window and the full ring, every ring slot is rewritten and no stale entry stays reachable. No sort at prefill is needed, best mt and stream cells stay within +0.08% of ST over the ratio sweep, and json.best stream-mt8 came out ahead of zstd at x0.93.

The fastest-tier strip fill was capped to the retention tail with a confirmed seed on 2026-09-14, in `3ba7335` and `27e8d52`. The Fast strategy's strip grid fill covered the whole window, which at Fastest meant 256K inserts into 32K single-candidate slots at 768 KiB, or about 8 overwrites per slot, although entries beyond the retention horizon are overwritten before the scan probes them. The fill now covers only `slots*stride*2`, or 192 KiB of the strip tail, and prefill micro time moved from 124-218 to 30-88 us per strip.

The cap is safe only because the seed got a confirmation rule in the same round. On a corpus with a period close to the window, the cross-job twins hash their slot once per period and survive newest-wins, and text's tiles at period 441226 under window 786432 are such twins. The old nearest-first seed rule could lose them to any local repeat of 56 bytes or more near the strip tail. A measured 194-byte duplicated source statement at offset 2244 beat the tile period, burned the seed budget and collapsed its job from about 200 B to 55183 B, and the full fill had masked this since `a37ebaa`. Seeds must now agree at spread anchors `[64, 640, 6144]` behind the 56-byte window, one u64 compare each. The ratio sweep moved json.fastest mt cells 0.07-0.11% denser, skewed within 0.0005%, and every other cell byte-identical. Speed moved json.fastest.mt8 from 3375 to 3477-3499, text.fastest stream-mt8 from 5377 to 5647-5944 at x0.79 to 0.70, and json.fastest stream-mt8 from x0.71 to 0.69.

The fastest stream-mt8 cells are pump and burst bound, not prefill bound. The 56%-of-input prefill volume was a cycle-share number and not the wall bound, so the job-side strip saving lands at its serial fraction only, at text +5-10% and json +3%. The remaining per-job strip cost is the seed scan itself, a full-strip walk of 60-130 us, which is inherent for period-close-to-window shapes whose twin sits deep in the strip.

Head tables are invalidated per job for determinism. The chain row advances its coordinate origin, described below, and every other row clears, because pooled state residue made output depend on worker scheduling with about 0.06% jitter. Chain lists need no clearing once every head insert writes its link too. The rep1 covered fill's link-less form broke that induction and made the stream pool's persistent workers produce claim-order-dependent bytes, fixed on 2026-09-16 through `insert_linked_at` and recorded in [pitfalls](../pitfalls/encoding.md). The remaining clears for fast, dfast and row are non-temporal stores of at least 4 MiB. The commit is `a6cf8a6`.

The AVX-512 seed scan assembles an occupancy bitmap from 64 B chunks and 8 overlapping loads, taking bits top-down to match the scalar nearest-first order exactly, so output is unchanged. Random-class jobs moved from 0.45 to 0.06-0.13 ms per job, a 4-7x gain. The commit is `e1c6d67`.

### Deep-tier job cost

The deep tiers' per-job fixed costs were decomposed with `zstdx-bench jobdecomp` and its `job_trace` dev feature, which spans the strip tree fill, block-boundary refills, hash3 ingestion, ultra's seed parse, state reset and LDM prefill, all under the `prefill` span. Shares of summed job time on a 32 MiB corpus and dll100 at mt8 put the strip tree fill first everywhere at 40-73%: text.opt stream-mt 72.7%, json.opt bulk 44.8% at a 3-5 MiB/s insert rate, and dll100 44-50%. Ultra's seed parse is second only on block-tiled text at 32-38%, about 43 ms per job, because the 256 KiB isolated seed span sits below the about 441 K tile period and the seed parse runs literal-dense, while it is minor on json and dll at 0.8-3.8%. The stats and table reset is noise, since the stream cells' about 5 ms per job reset spans are fresh-state Fast to Opt table builds, once per pool worker per encoder. Hash3 is at most 1.9% and LDM prefill at most 0.3%. The decomposition and the bound below live in `prefill_job_strip`.

The opt rows' tree fill now covers only the tail half of the job strip through `opt_tree_strip`, because C zstdmt's btopt and btultra overlap is window/2 through `ZSTDMT_overlapLog_default` 8, while our ultra keeps the full strip and btultra2's overlapLog is 9. The LDM table still ingests the whole strip. Shrinking both cost dll100 opt mt8 +4.13%, because the [4,8) MiB history band is LDM-served there, a result falsified and recorded in [negative](../negative/mt-stream.md). The tree-only bound is clean: the corpus worst is json.opt +0.011%, text.opt -0.017%, dll100 -0.002%, dll32 -0.04% and dll16 -0.11% across all mt cells, with st byte-identical.

Ultra's job-boundary seed span is now one block, the depth of the frame-start pass. Corpus ultra cells move +0.007-0.010% and dll100 ultra +0.003%, and the seed's per-job cost halves where it pays. The effects against the pre-change build, interleaved at mt8 with medians, read json.opt stream-mt8 9 to 13 MiB/s, +44%, because the json strip fill is dense-insert-bound at 3-5 MiB/s and halving its volume dominates the cell. json.opt bulk mt8 moved from 4.31 to 3.17 s, +36%, dll100.opt bulk from 4.36 to 3.63 s, +20%, and text.ultra stream-mt8 from 134 to 147 MiB/s, +10%, from the seed halving because text's seed was 37.5% of job time. text.opt stream-mt8 gained 3% because its fill is skip-assisted and its stream wall is pump-bound, and json.ultra was flat because its seed is cheap on json and ultra keeps the full tree strip.

The u32 tree entries landed on 2026-09-16 and halved the 96 and 160 MiB working sets of the u64 tables, through a per-state monotone-coordinate origin that replaced the epoch tag, as recorded in [matchers](matchers.md). The same jobdecomp cells moved json.opt bulk-mt from 3351 to 2814 ms and stream-mt from 2422 to 1881 ms, json.ultra from 8189 to 6409 and from 6386 to 5111. They moved text.opt from 125 to 57 and 170 to 66, text.ultra from 185 to 101 and 229 to 119, and dll100.opt from 3690 to 2800 and 5034 to 3868, as 3-iteration means at mt8. The parse and emit share rides the halved ring traffic too.

### Ultra boundary statistics

The per-job `opt_state` reset was the entire remaining boundary ratio cost for Ultra. A sequential in-order-carry decomposition on json.ultra with 4 jobs of 8 MiB each and every variant roundtrip-gated put about 104 KiB of the 85 KiB mt-versus-st loss on the stats alone, against 1.3 KiB for the entropy restart, 2.6 KiB for the rep gate and 3.1 KiB for the tree refill. The mechanism is a price-feedback basin and not miscalibrated histograms. A BASE-priced parse with the 8 MiB strip reachable chooses near-offset and ll0 chain shapes, `updateStats` reinforces them, and the job never leaves that basin, so carried stats show 37% rep0 with ml=12 and ll in {1,2} while reset jobs parse 10% rep0 with ml=7 and ll0.

The fix landed on 2026-09-12. The job's first parsed block runs a throwaway parse of the strip tail, two blocks, with the window re-based to that span. Candidates and fills clamp inside the span, reproducing the empty-window frame start, purely to seed the stats, and the real parse then re-fills the strip from `win_base`. Seed-span re-insertion is benign because the head rejects the duplicate position. json.ultra bulk-mt and stream-mt moved from -1.85% to -0.13%, ST dump stayed byte-identical, and mt wall was unchanged at json 4.1 MiB/s best-of-5. Seeding the same span with the strip reachable recovers nothing at any span size, as recorded in [negative](../negative.md).

### Bulk state pool

The text.balanced bulk-mt8 fill family was re-measured on 2026-09-26 against both a uop and spill bound and a DRAM bound, and neither held. The chain grid fill loop compiles to 11 instructions with zero spills: an unaligned u64 load, a mask, imul, a variable shr, a head load, a chain store, and a head store with the pack value carried as a rolling `add $3` register. A warm solo 4 MiB strip fill runs 0.82 ns per position, about 4.4 cycles, at its uop and memory-overlap floor. The uop diet, whole-loop asm and position-pure split scan are falsified for it, because the scan is 3 of 11 uops and the per-position memory work of one random head RMW and one chain stream store is fixed by semantics.

The cold-pool attribution was real, but the mechanism was first-touch faults and not bandwidth. A fresh state's fill pays 2513 minor faults and runs 68% system time at 2.60 against 0.82 ns per position. The bulk jobs ran on `thread::scope` workers whose thread-local state slots die with the call, so every call re-faulted about 12 MiB of tables per job on the balanced row's H21 and C20 tables. The stream side's cross-thread `STATE_POOL` at depth 32 now lives in `encoding::frame_compressor` and serves the bulk jobs' take and return through `run_job` and the donation path, while the ST path keeps its thread-local slot. `pooled_bulk_mt_matches_fresh` pins repeated in-process calls, mixed worker counts and pool-level mixing against fresh-call bytes.

The effects under a locked interleaved A/B of 6 rounds per side with medians of iterations 2 and 3 read dll32 balanced bulk-mt8 264.3 to 241.7 ms, -8.6%, and dll100 balanced 733.5 to 676.0 ms, -7.8%. The dll cells are job-throughput-bound, so summed job time reaches the wall there, and text.balanced bulk-mt8's own wall stayed flat because its steady wall is serial caller-side work. That work is the reach-probe donation of two 2 MiB L9 parses plus the verdict at 24-27 ms, now visible as jobdecomp's `probe` span, plus the windowed spf build at about 12.5 ms when the plan arms. The probe was invisible to every job span, repeating the earlier caller-side whale lesson. Its shrink parse now runs on a scope thread under the keep donation through `reach_probe::measure_shrink_concurrent` in [matchers](matchers.md), closing the serial residue on 2026-10-17, moving text.balanced bulk-mt8 from x1.14 to x1.03 and leaving the keep parse as the probe's floor.

### Long-compare regime

The dll100 best-tier collapse was closed on 2026-10-02 with `count_from_long`. Jobdecomp on dll100 l13 at w8 put 94.9% of summed job time inside `DubtFinder::find_best`, with the search count at about 1.15x the ST parse's but the compared byte volume at 28x, 4084 against 144 B per `count_from` call, or about 624 GB of compares per 100 MB. dll100 is concatenated ELF binaries with multi-KiB twin regions. The ST parse's deep sorted DUBT tree starts compares at high `common_smaller/larger` prefix offsets and skips them, while each mt job's shallow freshly filled strip tree, with a 4 MiB strip and an unsorted fill plus budgeted sort with orphaning, re-pays them full-length per candidate.

Strips, lattice and adoption are byte-pinned, so the fix is at the compare. `count_from_long` is a pure-function SIMD twin of `count_from` with AVX-512BW and AVX2 kernels behind the `common::simd` tier clamp. It is monomorphized into the job-side `DubtFinder` and btlazy instantiations only through `const LONG`, keyed on the driver's `LdmArming` job context, so the ST rows keep the stock loop with zero dispatch cost. It is byte-exact by construction and gated by the 120-cell sweep, forced tiers and gungraun ST Ir within layout noise. dll100 stream-mt8 best moved from 6 to 15-16 MiB/s, x14.27 to x5.4, and bulk-mt8 best from 13 to 29, with opt and ultra flat. The remaining dll100 stream-mt8 gaps are scheduling-shaped: fastest at x3.56, balanced at x1.15 and ultra at x1.22. Fastest's capture lattice leaves one 16 MiB job exposed at finish, and 10 jobs on 8 workers run two waves, plus per-job whole-window LDM cold fills. zstd's 2051 MiB/s headline uses 48 MB out against our 24 MB, so the comparison is not iso-ratio.

### LDM mode dispatch

`compress_slice_mt` takes the frame's `LdmMode` and threads it into every state the frame runs, on 2026-10-21. That covers the plain jobs through `prepare_job_state`, the donated probe span through `donate_keep_span`, the dictionary seed job through `run_dict_job_zero` and `reset_with_dictionary_mode`, the shared-prefix builds re-reset with the mode after their pooled take, and `st_fallback`. Under `Auto` the stock ST entries run verbatim, and otherwise the mode-carrying `compress_slice_shaped_ldm` and `compress_slice_with_dictionary_id` run, so workers 0, 1 and 4 agree per mode below the MT gate. The pre-fix fallback dropped the switch and `Enable` produced different bytes at workers 0 and 4 under 2 MiB.

`Auto` is byte-identical everywhere, and the switch reads as the old hardcoded Auto. `Disable` stands the whole capture lattice down: `far_screen::mt_capture_window` never runs, `capture_grid` and `plan_prefix_ldm` return `None`, so there is no pinned grid, no SelfFill, Whole or Windowed schedule and no header max-raise, including on dictionary frames, and every job parses with `params.ldm = false`. `Enable` keeps the window half through the 2^27 raise in `ldm_window_log` and forces the far source on per job through the size gate's bar bypass and the fast and dfast scan-domain split. The fast-row screen capture stands down under `Enable` because the raised window widens the fast rows' strip, which equals the window on rows without a `chain_reach`, past the capture grid's 16 MiB ceiling in the [32, 64) MiB band. The SelfFill lattice cannot grid that schedule, and the per-job forcing already carries the reach the capture would have bought.

The sanctioned divergences under every mode are part of the job model: repcode gates and per-job entropy resets at job boundaries, the MT header's default content-size declaration, and non-capture job grids that scale with the worker count. `capture_job_size`'s reach floor is now capped at the ceiling, because a forced `with_window_log(25)` on a fast row over a far-class mid-size source hit `clamp(min > max)` and panicked, a pre-existing case reachable with no mode involved. The gates were corpus dumps at st and mt4 against main, the ratio sweep, the `ldm_mode_mt_*` tests, the FFI `ZSTD_c_enableLongDistanceMatching` with nbWorkers smoke, and the suites at debug, release and no-default.

### Test pins

`capture_job_size_clamps_a_reach_past_the_cap` locks the clamp-panic class: a reach past the 16 MiB cap floors the grid at the cap for any length, and the in-band arms are pinned. `spf_finish_tail_adoption_is_pinned` drives a real finish-tail build and pins the strictly ascending freeze points, the prefix-domain bound, every job's adopted freeze as the newest publish at or below its start, and run-to-run determinism of freeze points and frames. ## Streaming buffer model

The streaming MT burst model landed in `44e11e5` and `27b91cf`. `FrameEncoderCore` becomes an enum `Single(Box<FrameEncoderCoreSt>) | Mt(MtEncoderCore)` under std, with no change to the read and write paths, and no_std with more than one worker stays Unsupported. Routing to the single core, the raw level or uncompressed matches bulk.

The buffer is one contiguous region `[window strip retained from the previous burst][unencoded data]`, with no memcpy sliding window. Absolute job boundaries derive from the stream offset alone, so without a flush the output is independent of how writes are chunked, and flush is a documented exception that rebases the grid. A burst fires once `max(workers,2)` complete jobs have accumulated, with scope parallelism, the main thread absorbing checksums in parallel, in-order assembly and poison propagation. A single-job tail runs inline without spawning.

Pledged jobs are cut with the shared bulk job-size formula and the last job is held back so finish can mark it last, which makes the stream byte-identical to bulk MT, measured at 32 MiB with fully equal sizes. The held-back job runs inline and single-threaded inside finish, costing the fast tier 0.55-0.78x but making the best tier 1.24-1.28x faster because big jobs amortize per-job fixed costs. The hold-back needs the `pos <= n` guard, because a pledge overshoot otherwise grows pending without bound. The mid-size keep class with a clamped window in [32, 64) MiB and an `ldm_head_parses` head re-grids at the reach verdict onto the bulk capture's schedule, with reach-based job size, whole-prefix strips and `JobPrefix` arming, landed 2026-09-18. dll32 moved from 5,460,884 to 4,390,255 bytes, byte-identical to bulk-mt as recorded in [matchers](matchers.md).

The unpledged `JobGrid::Growing` quantizes jobs into epochs of `burst_jobs` equal jobs, and each epoch doubles the job size from the bulk floor to the cap. Bursts fire exactly on epoch completion, so a burst is one epoch of equal-size jobs and the barrier never idles behind a straggler. The finish and flush tail is the only range that can land mid-epoch, with `hi = stream end` and itself a pure function of the input, and it is re-sliced into at most workers equal jobs of at least `MIN_JOB_SIZE`, so the tail burst fills the workers. A fixed floor paid a boundary restart at the floor forever, and per-job geometric growth through `job_size_for(4o)` in `946dd2f` bought json.opt -0.30%, ultra -0.28% and text.balanced -0.086% ratio but put jobs about 1.25x apart into one 8-job burst. Utilization was sum over 8 times max at about 0.52, halving every stream-MT cell in the 2026-09-12 regression. The epoch grid holds ratio within 0.1% of the geometric grid at mt8, with json.ultra 0.32% denser and json.opt 0.02%, and at mt4, json and skewed balanced and best 0.05-0.09% sparser, while restoring speed. Matrix mt8 moved json.fastest from 961 to 1423 MiB/s at 44% of the bulk ceiling against 30%, json.fast from 724 to 1248 at 53%, json.balanced from 175 to 247 at 94%, text.balanced from 982 to 1546 at 70%, and text.fast from 1991 to 2279 at 15%, as recorded in the [matrix page](../bench/matrix.md). text.fastest moved from 2049 to 2363 at 12% of ceiling and 1.8x behind zstd, and json.fastest sat at 1.26x, both bounded by the burst model itself and not by the schedule. Accumulate through one serial 16 KiB pump memcpy pass, per-burst thread spawn and the barrier cannot overlap encoding, which the persistent pool, the job-queue pipelining and the single-copy pump address below.

The accumulate buffer reserves the whole next epoch at each burst, one exact realloc or mremap instead of a doubling ladder, and is advised with `madvise(MADV_HUGEPAGE)` over its capacity. On this THP=madvise machine the dense first touch otherwise pays one 4 KiB fault per page, about 3 ms per iteration at 32 MiB, and solo text.fastest moved from 2464 to 3706 MiB/s while json.fastest moved from 1342 to 1567. THP=always machines map huge pages regardless, which was the premise of the original falsification, and the advice targets exactly that one buffer.

A flush re-grids `job_start` to a mid-epoch offset, so the job holding it legitimately crosses the epoch floor. `encode_jobs` clamps it to `min(job_end, aligned)` and the short job re-aligns the walk, because a burst must never straddle an epoch. The assert `next == job_end(...)` assumed an untouched grid and fired deterministically on flush to burst, caught by `flush_interleaved_roundtrip` in debug only while release took the clamp all along. Grid invariants must be stated relative to a possibly off-grid start, and the kept invariant is that whenever the epoch floor lies ahead of job_start the walk lands on it exactly.

An encoder-owned cross-burst worker-state pool holds `Mutex<Vec<Box<CompressState>>>`, because `thread::scope` spawns fresh threads every burst and the thread-local pool always misses on the Balanced 8 MiB tables and their first-touch faults. The deep tier gains 22-30% from it. A panicked state may be mid-compress garbage, so it is discarded and not returned.

The persistent burst worker pool landed on 2026-09-14. Burst threads used to be a fresh `thread::scope` set per burst at about 0.5-2 ms of spawn and join each, and the pool now spawns once at the first multi-job burst and parks on a generation condvar between bursts. The per-burst job loop is unchanged, with atomic pickup, poison check and pooled states, and an `exited` countdown gives the posting thread a quiesce point before it mutates, moves or drops the accumulate buffer. The pool is wall-neutral alone at 32 MiB, because spawn sits below the bound at 3 bursts per stream. It is the substrate for overlapping accumulation with an in-flight burst.

Overlapped accumulation landed the same day. `encode_jobs` splits into `fire_jobs`, which posts the burst, hashes and returns, and `drain_inflight`, which does ordered assembly, quiesce, trim and panic surfacing. The steady write path fires and keeps pumping while the workers encode, so epoch N+1's accumulation overlaps burst N instead of serializing behind its barrier. Appends during a burst are confined to the reserved window because the next epoch is `reserve_exact`-ed before posting, so the frozen buffer never reallocs under the workers, and the prefix drain `try_compact` runs as soon as the exit countdown hits zero, an acquire load of the same quiesce. The first draft deferred the trim to the next fire, which moved a whole epoch of pending bytes per burst, about one extra full-data copy, while compacting at the first quiesced write keeps it at strip scale. json.fastest stream-mt8 moved from 1475 to 1958 MiB/s, x1.19 to x0.90 and ahead of zstd, and text.fastest's fast mode moved from about 2322 to about 4900 solo, though the interleaved bench stayed bimodal.

Pooled accumulate buffers landed on 2026-09-14 and diagnosed the bimodality. Each fresh encoder maps an about 34 MiB accumulate buffer, and under the bench's alternating allocations the THP=madvise allocator on a 57-day-uptime fragmented machine falls back to 4 KiB pages. `getrusage` per round read solo 809 minor faults against 2622 interleaved, and the extra 1800 faults are about 3 ms, exactly the fast and slow gap, while perf stat showed whole-process faults doubling from 29k to 59k. THP=never collapses solo to the slow mode too, which confirms the fault path is the bound. A thread-local pool of accumulate buffers at depth 2 keeps at most 64 MiB and hands each new encoder the previous stream's faulted pages, cutting interleaved minor faults from 2622 to 126. Buffer contents never affect output, so the full ratio sweep diffs clean. Matrix enc-stream mt8 under interleaved A/B moved json.fastest from 1475 to 2600 MiB/s at x1.19 to x0.72 against zstd, text.fastest from 2322 to 5598 at x1.87 to x0.75, json.fast from x0.31 to x0.22 and text.fast from x0.78 to x0.39, all ahead of zstd, and the between-pass swing disappeared.

The finish-tail shared prefix fill landed on 2026-09-17. The unpledged row-9 keep class posts nothing during write, because epoch 0 at 512 MiB spans the whole stream, so at finish every tail job prefilled its own whole-prefix strip `[0, start)`. Those nested prefixes were redundant and jobdecomp put them at about 84% of the tail's summed job time, 243 ms summed and about 50 ms critical at about 0.5 GiB/s per worker, and the session's jobdecomp context wall moved from 83.6 to 59.2 ms. `build_spf` now fills the median tail boundary's prefix once on the calling thread before `settle_probe`, inside the donation wait the finish would spend anyway, and a shrink verdict retires the build. The build is segmented at LDM batch-freeze points, the one call-boundary kind that is exactly invisible to a stock one-call fill, because the batch count is zero by construction and the gear group grid restarts there inside the stock fill too. Mid-batch boundaries are not exact, and the gear4-c4 divergence is recorded in [negative](../negative/mt-stream.md). Under std the mt core hands `Read::read` the accumulate buffer's spare capacity directly, and because the Read contract forbids uninitialized buffers, an `init_len` extent tracks the ever-initialized bytes. The extent is monotone under wraps, clamped to the live length by growth because a realloc copies only the live bytes of `Vec::reserve`'s abstract machine, extended by a bounded zero-fill one region at a time, and carried across encoders by the buffer pool as the returned Vec's length, so steady-state streams zero-fill nothing. Each read is capped at 1 MiB so posting stays close behind the buffered bytes, and this is not the falsified 64 or 128 KiB chunk path because recycle points are buffer-fullness events. The ST core keeps the chunked pump because its copies hide behind the encode span and its block-staged buffer is small. `std::io::Read::read_buf` would be the cleaner tool but is still unstable. Growing at every recycle point up to the 256 MiB cap was the second cut: the grow-only-when-full policy kept the buffer at epoch scale forever because the growing grid's posting gate holds a whole epoch live, so every recycle paid a dead-prefix wrap of 6-12 MiB per iteration at 32 MiB plus a quiesce wait. A doubled buffer fits a whole stream, so the steady state wraps, waits and zero-fills zero times, verified by drop-time counters under the matrix loop.

The global worker-state pool landed in the same round, because pool threads are fresh per encoder, spawning at first post and joining at drop, so the per-encoder state pool always missed and every stream rebuilt eight matcher table sets. Workers now loan from a depth-capped global pool and return at shutdown, and the inline-job path uses it too. `finish` posts the tail re-slice before draining, because the pre-drain idled workers behind the previous epoch's straggler while tail jobs were already postable, and a stream ending exactly on an epoch boundary still drains pending jobs before the empty last block through `epoch_end_finish_assembles_pending`.

The effects against the job-queue build under interleaved solo A/B at mt8 on the read path at 32 MiB with locked medians moved text.fastest from 6736 to 9580 MiB/s, +42%, text.fast from 5515 to 7618, +38%, json.fastest from 2742 to 3551, +30% and 95% of its own bulk ceiling, and json.fast from 2026 to 2263, +12%. Matrix enc-stream mt8 moved json.fastest from 2667 to 3405 at 91% of ceiling, json.fast from 1968 to 2260, text.fastest from 6217 to 7084-7318 and text.fast from 4910 to 6464. The matrix interleaving costs the pump-bound cells about 20% against the controlled solo A/B on this box, with per-round spread 5.3k to 9.8k and bimodality, and the counters show it is not pool, growth, wrap or zero-fill because they are flat from round 2 on.

The job-queue pipelining replaced the burst barrier on 2026-09-16. The burst model's per-epoch barrier, where firing epoch N+1 waited for N's assembly, serialized the pump against the encode span. The pool is now a posted-job queue. An epoch's jobs post when the epoch is fully buffered, and the growing grid's gate is re-checked per posted job because a large write or an oversized pooled buffer can carry `pos` past several epoch ends in one append, while greedily posting an incomplete epoch changes the stream-end tail re-slice and the bytes, caught by `growing_grid_roundtrip`. Workers claim from the front and keep a worker-local `CompressState` across jobs, assembly drains whatever completed at each post, and the fixed grid posts each job at its own completion because `build_bounds` never re-slices, with the last-job hold-back kept. Buffer recycling is quiesce-gated: the dead-prefix wrap, an in-buffer move whose destination overlaps live job views, and growth, which may relocate the allocation under resolved `FrozenSrc` pointers, run only once every posted job has completed, so the pump stalls on the workers at most once per epoch and growth doubles up to 256 MiB before the wrap alone recycles. The read-side pump kept a persistent 16 KiB staging chunk, since 64 and 128 KiB pulls measured slower.

The effects against the burst build at mt8 on the read path at 32 MiB with medians read text.fastest +11.5%, text.fast +11%, json.fast +4.7% and json.fastest flat. The json.fastest cell is encode-span-bound, with its 8 workers as the floor and not the schedule, and the old pump and barrier decomposition put barrier wait at 4.8 of 11.1 ms, so removing the barrier only exposes the encode span. The historical sporadic hang was root-caused in this work. A TOCTOU between `drain_all`'s quiescent check and the completion-count snapshot inside the wait let a completion burst of the finish tail's 6-8 jobs land exactly between the caller's `n_incomplete` read and the waiter's `completed` snapshot. Its `notify_all` fired before the waiter parked, the snapshot was already final, and the wait's only exit of `completed != before` never fired, so the 100 ms timeout re-checked the same stale condition and slept forever at 0% CPU with every worker parked. The wait now re-evaluates `n_incomplete == 0` on every wake and re-snapshots per iteration. A bounded-timeout wait whose loop exit is a snapshot delta and not a re-checked predicate is not a safe fallback. Stress with 8 parallel encoders over json and text at fastest and fast covered 9600 streams with a gdb watchdog and zero stalls, and after the fix 30 consecutive narrow matrix runs were clean.

## Decode parallelism

The decode side is the one exclusive dimension where libzstd has no MT decode, landed in `3219947`. Block headers are pre-scanned and segments are cut where entropy state is self-describing, meaning literals are not Treeless and there are no Repeat FSE streams. Job-style encoders such as zstd `-T` and our MT encoder produce exactly these points at every job boundary, and multi-frame input is a special case of clean-start units unified in one dispatcher. Stage A entropy-decodes in parallel on the worker pool and produces packed sequence streams plus exact output sizes, while stage B runs on the calling thread in input order, executes into the output and carries the rep history, which is the only cross-sequence state and never touches stage A. Dictionary frames, malformed input, a single restart point and a single core all fall back to the serial path, because error reporting belongs to the serial path.

Zero-sequence compressed blocks mid-segment used to abort stage A with `MissingCompressionMode`. Our MT encoder emits them on small-alphabet data at balanced levels, and the serial path never builds FSE tables for them, so stage A must skip the sequence decode too. The fix landed on 2026-09-15 with a regression test in `mt.rs`.

The status after wildcopy execution and pooled staging on 2026-09-15 read 64 MiB solo scaling at json.zst3 1.5-1.7x on 4 threads and 1.74x on 8, skewed.zst9 1.57x, and our-encoder skewed 2.10x on 4 threads and 2.60x on 8. The 32 MiB matrix read json.zst3 mt4 1.56x and mt16 1.67x of ours-ST, against 0.71-1.03x before, and skewed.zst9 1.21-1.28x against 0.71x, so skewed is at parity with zstd's ST stream reference and json is above it from mt4.

Stage-B parallelization by piece watermarks is falsified for typical frames and recorded in [negative](../negative/mt-stream.md). Piece watermarks with exact per-sequence source gating were implemented and byte-correct, but the shallowest near-boundary read at offset about 100-400 B chains pieces into a serial pipeline, so the minimum offset at any cut and not the window sets the stagger. Stage A and B already overlap for free in the serial-B design, because the calling thread executes segment i while workers stage i+1 onward, which is why the piece machinery was a net regression and was reverted. Wildcopy staged execution landed in `65e4b98` and pooled staging buffers in `3c850d3`.

### Piece-parallel stage B

Piece-parallel stage B landed on 2026-09-16 in `decoding/mt_pieces.rs`, env-paired with the ramp, and engagement is measured and never trusted. Routing at both bulk MT entry points is gated on `ZSTDX_MT_RAMP_BYTES` plus a single pledged frame of 2-64 MiB, because the pledge partitions the output up front and bounds staging memory. It runs stage A to completion and then plans candidate job grids from the job-size formula's lattice, `ceil(fcs/2w)` over w=2 to 32 plus the overlap-raised 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 MiB, ascending, with every `k·J` landing on a block start. Each candidate's pieces are validated standalone in parallel. A walk from a sentinel repcode history of `[u32::MAX; 3]` measures every piece's deepest external read top plus 16 bytes of wildcopy read slack, and never-written slots resolve far beyond any legal offset so any dependence on pre-piece state rejects the grid, while true job starts pass by the encoder's rep gate. Engagement demands that every piece keep a top at least 3/8 of the job size below its start, for a pipeline ceiling of at least 1.6x. Wrong candidates die on the first violating piece, so the lattice's reject cost stays near one piece walk.

Validated pieces execute on the worker pool and the caller. Claiming is in order, and the watermark dependency chain cannot deadlock because the minimal claimed piece never waits. Each piece has one start gate on the contiguous-executed watermark, wildcopy is bounded by the piece end so parallel pieces never write into a neighbor, and the frame checksum is absorbed in piece order under the coordination mutex, where own-range absorbs stay hot and a foreign range is absorbed by whichever thread unblocks the order. Validation failure runs the serial stage B over the same staged segments, byte-identical to `decode_parallel`. The verdict is consumable only after staging completes: a winner settled against a partial prefix whose staged output equals the pledge while trailing segments keep producing would otherwise execute `[0, fcs)` and silently drop the divergence. The complete-refresh kill now withdraws a settled winner, and an over-pledged frame fails with the Frame_Content_Size mismatch on every path, with `overlong_frame_after_early_winner_errors` as the regression.

The effects on a 32 MiB corpus at D=2 MiB with checksummed frames and interleaved medians read json.lvl3 mt8 5122 against 1599 MiB/s serial-B, 3.2x and 3.5x of own ST at 1466, and mt4 2.4x. dll32.lvl3 read 2.6-2.9x at mt8 and 1.8x at mt4, or 3.5x of own ST, and skewed.lvl9 read 1.0-1.1x because it is entropy-bound and stage B was never its bottleneck. ## Streaming single-thread

Streaming encoders in `write::Encoder` and `read::Encoder` share the incremental core and the same matcher and block-encoder building blocks as bulk, and with no intermediate flush the output is byte-identical to `encoding::compress`, asserted across write chunkings in `51ec2fc`. The streaming-decode flat outBuff model and `StreamingDecoder` read-granularity work are on the [decoding page](decoding.md).

Read and write decoders are transparent to multi-frame and skippable frames, and `single_frame()` restores single-frame semantics. The write side stages a complete block header, body and trailer before handing it to FrameDecoder, because starved reads would poison the state. `write::Decoder` staging moved from a per-block drain to a cursor with bounded compaction on 2026-09-19. The staged input used `Vec::drain(..consumed)` after every block, an O(remaining-tail) memmove per block at about 1:1 with input bytes at the recommended 128 KiB batch, since block size equals batch size, and about 3.5 bytes moved per input byte at 1 MiB batches. The replacement advances a `start` cursor and compacts only when the buffer empties or the cursor crosses `COMPACT_LIMIT` at 512 KiB, four times the block maximum. The intermediate compact-only-when-empty shape measured slower than the drain, at raw 2300 to 1408 MiB/s, because with the 3-byte header phase offset the cursor never catches the length inside a frame, so the live region slides through the allocation with fresh-page faults and a whole-stream-capacity Vec. A slide-anywhere cursor must be paired with a bound. Measured with 96 MiB frames through `write::Decoder` on an in-memory source and best-of-7, raw and LC blocks moved from 2280 to 2360 MiB/s at 128 KiB batches, +3.5%, and from 1942 to 2155 at 1 MiB, +11%, while compressed text moved from 144 to 146 and was decode-bound and small 7 KiB writes were flat. The memory profile stays at staged-tail scale plus a 512 KiB bound.

The no_std `io::Read::read_to_end` doubles its probe buffer while reads fill it completely, starting at 16 KiB on the stack and capping at 1 MiB on the heap, following std's heuristic instead of re-looping 16 KiB at a time over whole streams, and small readers never touch the heap buffer. This landed on 2026-09-19.

The ST encoder core pool landed on 2026-09-21. A fresh `read` or `write` Encoder allocated and first-touch-faulted the matcher's whole table set per stream, about 50 MiB at the best tier, measured at about 16K page faults per stream against a 40 ms bulk encode of the same bytes. Profiling enc-stream-read 13 read 117.5K faults and 148 ms system time per 6 iterations against bulk's 20.9K and 34 ms, so kernel page-fault work was about 29% of the stream wall. The MT core had already pooled this buffer class, covering accumulate buffers, worker states and the kit, while the ST core pooled nothing. `FrameEncoderCoreSt` now sits behind an owning handle whose drop returns it to a one-deep thread-local pool `ST_CORE_POOL` in `stream/encoder_core.rs`, so every stream end, whether finish, error or abandonment, recycles. `reinit` recycles the entropy tables like `reset_slice_state` and reconfigures under the same residue contract as the bulk slice pool. ## Later stream scheduling

The stream input copy-tax map was measured on 2026-09-30. Every ST stream encode cell moves its input through three libc copies, plus a window-ring slide on the fast rows. It was measured per cell with a scratch LD_PRELOAD memcpy and memmove counter using return-address caller buckets through nm, log2 size classes and fresh-process N2 and N4 differencing for steady state, with `perf stat` and record on matched unloaded runs for cycle shares. The chain is one byte each in every cell at every level. Raw to pump chunk uses a 16 KiB `Read` fill inside `read::Encoder`'s pump loop at 2,048 calls per 32 MiB. Chunk to `staged` uses `FrameEncoderCore::write`'s `extend_from_slice`. `staged` to the window tail uses `FrameEncoderCoreSt::encode_block`'s `copy_from_slice` over 128 KiB blocks at 662-1,029 calls by pre-split cuts. On the fast rows the owned window additionally slides in `block_tail` through `win.copy_within(drop.., 0)`, inlined at encode_block plus 0xc7, with a keep equal to the row's stock window: 35 times 768 KiB, or 0.82x per byte, at fastest and 14 times 2 MiB, or 0.875x per byte, at fast. The slide is absent on balanced and best, whose wide windows never fill in a 32 MiB frame, and on far-screen-accepted frames.

Per-cell copy totals read json at L1 4.13 bytes per input byte with 5.6% memmove cycle share and 16.9M copy cycles per iteration, L3 4.10 at 3.7% and 14.0M, L9 3.29 at 0.9% and 17.6M, and L13 3.19 at 0.3% and 19.7M. Text read L1 3.83 at 35.0% and 12.7M, L3 3.89 at 33.1% and 14.6M, L9 3.01 at 11.9% and 17.8M, and L13 3.01 at 9.1% and 18.8M. Skewed read L1 4.41 at 21.1% and 11.6M, L3 5.09 at 3.8% and 31.3M, where 0.69x per byte is 8-32 B literal and sequence staging in the dfast parse at about 930K calls per iteration and not the input chain, L9 3.61 at 20.1% and 15.4M, and L13 3.57 at 0.2% and 34.5M. Copy volume is level-independent at about 13-20M cycles per iteration, and the share collapses on deep rows only because their matcher cost explodes. The wall ceiling ranks text.fast and fastest first, with a third of the wall in chain and compaction copies, then skewed.fastest and balanced at about 20%, text.balanced and best at 9-12%, json at 6% or less, and the deep skewed rows below 1%. Priorities for deleting the chain put the text fast rows first and preferred the ring-window form, which also deletes the compaction slide, over a plain borrowed window that leaves the 0.82-0.875x slide standing. The round's own ceiling falsification in [negative](../negative/mt-stream.md) superseded the deletion lever: stock equals the one-copy gather and is below the zero-copy bulk core, so every owned-window form, the ring included because its deleted slide is cache-rate, is bounded at stock. The cycle shares above stand as attribution.

The far screen's own 4 MiB head staging is copy-neutral, measured the same round. It rides the same 16 KiB pump chunks and 128 KiB block-tail copies, with per-process first-iteration extras of a constant about 430 KB of warmup in every cell and no span-shaped term. The measured 14 times 2 MiB per iteration stream on a fresh-process fast-row cell is `block_tail`'s ring slide at the stock window, and it disappears when the pooled ST core's window capacity is wide. One best-tier stream predecessor zeroes it for subsequent fast frames, with the driver check reading 0 compactions for s13-then-l3 against 56 for l3-only, while a bulk L13 predecessor changes nothing at 56 because the bulk path's direct window never touches the pooled owned window. The carrier is the pooled `win` capacity, which grows by reserve only, and not the screen verdict.

The verdict's own lever is widening. A far-class accept on dll32.raw stream L3, with frame wlog 26 against the stock wlog 21 on text L3, widens to the 64 MiB window and compaction never fires in a 32 MiB frame. Every corpus shape here rejects, and the compaction stream at the stock 768 KiB or 2 MiB keep is itself the evidence, because a widened window never fills in 32 MiB. The healthy solo state therefore pays the slide. Both the chain and the slide sit on the raw to chunk to staged to win path that the copy-tax rework removes, and an in-place or ring window fill deletes 3.81-3.89x per byte on the fast rows at once, from the chain 3.0 plus the slide 0.82-0.88. Nothing about the screen staging requires its own mechanism.

The 16 KiB pump chunk was probed at 64 and 128 KiB on 2026-09-30, with byte-identical output on every probed cell, position-aligned interleaved A/B and perf-stat instruction counts. json fastest, fast and balanced read wall +0.4%, -0.2% and +0.4% with Ir -0.01 to -0.05%, which is flat. Text fastest and fast read wall -2.0 to -3.7% while their Ir improved -0.68 to -0.88% from fewer libc calls, the ineffective-disturbance signature on the layout-sensitive text fast rows and not a regression signal either. No chunk value buys measurable amortization, because the per-call overhead the chain pays is cycle-trivial next to the 3x volume. The lever is deleting the copies, not resizing them.

The one-shot feed shape pays no stream tax, measured on 2026-10-16. `bulk::compress_with` now honors `EncoderOptions::pledged_size` and declares it into every slice-path header with the streaming core's exact shape, single-segment when the row's window covers the input and windowed otherwise, while `compress_slice_mt` keeps its always-declare default. The value is validated against the source with the stream core's `PledgedSizeMismatch` contract, closing a doc-versus-behavior gap because the option was documented as a hard header contract but silently ignored before. The FFI one-shot entries `ZSTD_compress`, `CCtx`, `usingDict`, `usingCDict` and `compress2` ride the slice core, and `pledged_stream_matches_declared_bulk_st` pins byte identity with the pledged stream over both header forms, the checksum and window-log cases, reach-probe donation and dict frames, alongside the C-harness A/B outputs.

The measured verdict from the C harness under A/B/A three times was byte-identical outputs everywhere, with ST one-shot cells on json, text and dll32 at l1, l3 and l9 with 4K to 1M payloads wall-neutral, and json compress2 at w8 and l1 the single reproduced win at +5-8%. A single whole-source `write_all` through the stream core already runs at the slice core's speed, and the earlier copy-chain tax of 3-4 libc copies per byte amortizes to noise at one-shot feed granularity on every measured cell. The pull-shaped stream deficits, such as text.fastest stream at half of bulk, are a property of the chunked feed shape, so the one-shot cells should not be re-measured for stream-tax levers.

`input_shape(len)` is a hint on the MT core too, fixed on 2026-10-22. The core folded the shape length, whether pledge or hint, into every `PledgedSizeMismatch` gate at write, `pump_direct` and finish, and into several schedule sites, so options that streamed cleanly at workers(1) errored at workers(4) once the fed total crossed the hint. Compat's degradation of a pledge into `input_shape(len)` under ContentSizeFlag=false lands exactly there. A dedicated `pledged` field carrying only `options.pledged_size`, the ST core's own split, now keys every hard contract: the mismatch gates, the armed fast-row header's FCS and lattice choice, `reserve_decided`, `post_due`'s final-job hold and the probe re-grids. `resolve_fast_capture` takes the open Capture lattice for unpledged frames instead of pinning a hint-derived Fixed grid and stamping the hint as the header FCS. The shape length keeps sizing the window and overlap and clears the probe's size gate the ST way through `probe_gate`, where a hint of at least `PROBE_MIN_FRAME` arms the probe at `PROBE_SPAN` and a smaller one never does, while the old gate staged every hinted stream. `post_donation`'s arming mirrors `engage_midsize_capture`'s fixed-grid gate, so an open-ended frame's donated job zero arms `Job`, whereas a hinted Growing-grid stream could donate a JobPrefix span against Job jobs and hit the latent pairing-assert divergence. Bytes are unchanged for pledged and hint-less streams, and hinted streams align with the workers(1) contract. Gates were the corpus dump at st and mt4 across all levels and the stream ladder of st, w4-pledged and w4-open at 6 levels, byte-identical against the parent, plus `input_shape_len_is_a_hint_not_a_pledge`, `fast_row_midband_hint_streams_like_open` and the existing pledged-mismatch tests at both worker counts.

The capture schedule's walks are window-bounded, fixed on 2026-10-22. `capture_grid`'s `(1..n_jobs).rfind` and `fast_capture_schedule`'s filter walked `n_jobs = len/16 MiB` candidates with the declared length as `len`, so a fast row carrying a u64::MAX-class pledge, whether a genuine absurd `pledged_size` or compat's ContentSizeFlag=false twin of one as a hint, stalled `write` for minutes after the first staged Head band through pure iteration with no overflow. The predicate that a job start is at most the window is monotone, so the closed form `prefix_last_job = min(window/job_size, n_jobs-1)` replaces all three walks, including `plan_prefix_ldm`'s, and is None on an empty range. The equivalence sweep caught the single-job clamp edge, and the median of the contiguous `1..=last` range is `last/2 + 1`. The two `SPF_MIN_PREFIX` gates are implied on every lattice that reaches them, because the window is at least 2^25 and the fast rows pin 2^26, `job_size` is in `[len/8, 2^24]` and the source clamp requires `len` of at least window/2, so a window-bound tail clears 2^25-2^24 bytes and a job-count-clamped one clears `len-2^24`, above 8 MiB. They are `debug_assert`s now. `reserve_decided`'s pledged reserve saturates, because u64::MAX-class pledges wrapped the want to near zero. The gates were byte-identical outputs on the corpus dump at st and mt4 across all levels and the stream ladder against the parent, plus `prefix_last_job_closed_form_matches_the_scan`, `capture_schedule_survives_absurd_declared_lens` and `absurd_pledge_fast_row_write_returns_bounded`, where the last test hung before the fix.

The capture engagement is one canonical predicate, fixed on 2026-10-22. The pledged stream's mid-size predicate `midsize_capture_window` re-implemented the bulk dispatch's engagement decision of window, head screen and band exclusion as a private copy, and `fast_capture_schedule` hardcoded `LdmMode::Auto` inside its `capture_grid` call, which was safe only through the stream constructor's staging gate. Both paths now read one enum-typed predicate, `mt::capture_band` returning `CaptureBand::{MidSize,Windowed,Fast}`, shared by the bulk planner's `capture_grid` and `plan_prefix_ldm` and the stream core's capture flips. One deliberate shape delta remains documented at the stream wrapper: the bulk grid's prefix-tail gate through `prefix_last_job` inside `capture_grid` stays a grid concern, the pledged re-grid pins `capture_job_size` directly, and the one-job W25 edge keeps the pre-existing pledged-versus-bulk divergence.
