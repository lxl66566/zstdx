# Encoding Path

This page answers where the encoder spends its time outside the matcher and which changes moved the measured cost. It follows the pipeline from the entry API through entropy-table construction, sequence emission, literals coding, block shaping and the dictionary paths. The matcher and level side is on [Matchers and Compression Levels](matchers.md), and the multithreading and streaming pipeline is on [Multithreading and Streaming](mt-stream.md). The x notation is our time divided by the reference time, so values below 1 mean we are faster. The differential dll100 attribution lives in [enc-dll-gap](../bench/enc-dll-gap.md).

## Entry and state management

The slice entry `compress_slice_to_vec` borrows the input as the match window through a raw-pointer window with an unsafe `Send` marker. This removes the read pass, window compaction and per-call window allocation. Output is reserved up front and blocks are written into the final output. Text gained 40% and zeros 60%.

A thread-local state pool holds hash tables, default FSE tables and `BlockScratch`, with `take()`-style reuse across calls and nesting safety. Payloads of 1 KiB gained 3x to 7x, and 4 KiB gained 30% to 80%.

The emit path stopped deep-cloning three FSE tables and a Huffman table per block, which had cost several hundred allocations per block and about 30% of runtime, and now takes and restores entropy tables by value. `start_matching_into` writes into a buffer directly instead of through a callback closure that captured two `Vec`s and matcher state and caused register spills, with one store sampling at 40%. Matcher window reads are unchecked. The items moved 2%, 2% and 11%, cumulatively 24% on json and 39% on text. The lesson is that unchecked-read gains of 11% far outweigh structural rework of 2% twice, and bounds checks on byte-wise paths are the prime target.

Block contents are written in place into the frame output: the 3-byte block header is reserved first and backfilled after compression. Literals, sequence and precomputed-code buffers moved into the `BlockScratch` pool, which cut random by 6.8% of instructions. Blocks with no sequences skip literal staging because the matcher skips the whole-block copy and the encoder reads the window directly, which gained 13% on random from 64K to 1M.

## Entropy table construction

### Arithmetic transition tables

On 2026-09-16 the encoder's FSE tables materialized a full `(max_symbol+1) × table_size` packed-entry matrix, about 54K u32 stores per fresh-table block: LL at 36×512, ML at 53×512 and OF at 32×256, plus the 3×256 row-pointer setups in `encode_sequences`. The tables now carry libzstd's `FSE_CTable` arithmetic form in one pooled buffer of at most 10 KB against 218 KB. `tt[256]` holds u64 entries packing `(delta_nb_bits, delta_find_state)`, and a `table_size`-wide u16 `state_table` holds biased next states as `table_size + index` in ascending-position symbol-major rows. One step is `nb = (state + dnb) >> 16`, `emit = state & mask(nb)` because the run baseline is a multiple of the run width and the biased state's low bits are the old precomputed difference, and `next = state_table[(state >> nb) + dfs]`. The build writes O(table_size) entries and the row-pointer machinery disappears.

Equivalence with the old run walk is structural: same spread, same cumul rows, doubles first in index order. It is pinned by a rewritten `soa_matches_reference` differential over 400 random tables and every symbol and state step against the reference runs, plus `check_tables`. Output is byte-identical everywhere across a 30-file full-ladder dump, the 120-cell ratio sweep and all modes. Gungraun measured json-4K at 7.0% fewer instructions, text-4K at 5.2%, 16K at 2.2% to 2.4% and 64K at 1.2% to 2.0%. The 1 MiB encode slices put the hot-loop price at about 25 extra instructions per sequence on sequence-dense json, fastest and fast at +1.8% and +3.5% instructions, and 0.6% to 1.6% fewer on text. Wall follows the build side rather than the instructions: small json-4K moved x1.79 to 1.62 (-9.4%), text-4K -6.1%, the whole 4K to 1M band -1.6% to -9.3% with json-64K fast crossing to x0.98, matrix json.fastest -2.0% at 581 to 594 MiB/s, json.fast -3.9% at 445 to 469, text fastest and fast -2.4% and -3.3%, dll100 fastest +1.6% to +2.2% at 367 to 373-375, fast +1% to +2% and balanced +1% to +5%. The materialized form's per-sequence packed entry saved ALU but kept a 218 KB L2-resident working set behind every block, and the L1-resident arithmetic tables win on wall at every measured shape even where instructions rise.

A structured SoA layout replaced 256 `Vec`s plus double sorting, with probabilities, starts and transitions in contiguous memory. Per-symbol spread precomputation then flattened each transition entry to `(next<<13)|(nb<<9)|baseline`, giving O(1) state transitions plus an LL and ML code lookup and moving json from 189 to 245 MB/s.

### Table build paths

The CTable build matched libzstd's shape on 2026-09-18. `build_table_from_probabilities_into` cost 13.3K instructions per call over the four builds of a json-4K encode against C's 8.1K in `FSE_buildCTable_wksp`. The old form paid a full 255-entry `owner` fill per table, a separate -1 placement round, a per-occurrence spread walk with a skip check per step, a cumul and cursor u32 pair, and 2 KB of stack traffic.

The -1 placement now rides the row-start pass. `start[s]` doubles as the state-table walk's cursor and transforms into the biased start state inside the `tt` fill, whose running total re-derives each row start, so cumul and its copy are gone. Builds without -1 weights take C's fast path: symbol runs are staged contiguously at eight bytes per unaligned u64 store into a pooled high-water spread buffer, then scattered by a fixed-step two-way unrolled loop of constant trip count. The step is odd for every table_log of 4 or more, so the walk covers each position exactly once, and `spread[k]` is the k-th symbol-major occurrence, which makes the placement provably identical to the classic walk. `owner` and `spread` recycle without per-build clearing, the sum invariant is debug-asserted in the row-start pass, and `soa_matches_reference` grew to 600 cases with every third banning -1 weights. Gungraun measured json-4K 1.34% fewer instructions (x1.224 to 1.208 against libzstd), text-4K 1.06%, and 16K 0.4% to 0.5%, with wall neutral because the cut is loop overhead rather than memory traffic. The full-ladder dump is byte-identical.

`FSETable` was thinned into one pooled buffer. The struct carried `probs: [i32; 256]` and `start: [u16; 256]` inline at about 1.5 KB, so every hop copied it: the build return, the `FseTableMode` enum wraps, `FSEEncoder::new`, `compress_fastest`'s `old_tables` array with three `Option<FSETable>` at one 4.6 KB copy per block, `replace_previous` three times, the `BlockOutcome::Encoded(BlockTables)` return and `reset_slice_state`'s takes. Callgrind put the whole copy class at 8.9K instructions of a 184.8K json-4K call, 4.8%, where C pays near zero because its literals staging is inline wildcopy and its CTables are workspace pointers.

The fat arrays moved into the existing pooled `tab` buffer behind fixed offsets: `[tt: u64×256][probs: i32×256][start: u16×256][state_table: u16×table_size]`, adding 2.5 KB per pooled table. The struct is 32 bytes, all those hops become register moves, and the `probs_full` staging copy disappears. Recycled tails stay stale but unread under an `nsym` bound: `write_table`'s serialization and zero-run scans stop at `nsym`, and `symbol_bit_cost` returns `None` past it. The repeat-coverage check was the one reader that could observe stale bytes, and `pooled_calls_byte_identical` caught it before the bound went in. Output is byte-identical. Gungraun measured json-4K 4.45% fewer instructions (x1.181 to 1.128), text-4K 3.47% (1.148 to 1.109), random-4K 4.9%, 16K 1.0% to 1.2% and 64K 0.3% to 0.4%, with the copy class itself falling from 2.28M to 0.29M instructions on the 256-call json-4K bench. The gotcha is that region offsets must be derived in one unit. The first cut mixed u16-index and byte arithmetic with `START_U16_OFF` 256 slots low, overlapping start into probs and smearing the serialization, and the symptom was a `write_bits` value overflow on the legacy roundtrip path.

### Build scratch

Per-block entropy builds allocated and zeroed their buffers on every build. On a json-4K fastest call that was about 15% of instructions in memsets alone, from the three transition-table `vec![0u32; probs.len()*table_size]` allocations, package-merge level lists, Huffman codes and wire weights, plus about 3% in malloc and free churn. `FSETable` and `HuffmanTable` now return their transition and code buffers to pools in `BlockScratch` through `FseBuildScratch` and `HuffScratch`, and builds write into recycled buffers. A buffer's length is its initialized prefix, so growth and its only zeroing happen once per size, while live rows are fully overwritten and dead rows stay stale but unread because wire codes never exceed the histogram's `max_symbol`, and `bulk::tests::pooled_calls_byte_identical` pins the repeated-call outputs. Recycling sites are table adoption in `compress_fastest`, the raw-block fallback, `reset_slice_state` and the Huffman weight-table temporary inside `write_table_with`.

Measured on the deterministic per-call instruction bench in `zstdx-gungraun/benches/small.rs`: json-4K 13.6% fewer instructions, text-4K 11.1%, json and text 16K 7% to 8% and 64K 7%. Wall moved json-4K x1.96 to 1.83 and text-4K x1.91 to 1.87 at 8% absolute, json and text 8K and 16K -4% to -6%, skewed-4K x0.90 to 0.80, and json and text 1K x2.30 and 2.48 to 1.95 and 2.17. The 64K to 1M band stayed flat on json and gained 11% to 20% on text fastest and fast because per-block builds amortize there too. Output is byte-identical across five shapes and levels 1, 3, 9, 16 and 19 emit-diffed against the pre-change build. The residue was the 12 KB lane zeroing in `choose_tables_fast`, which the arithmetic-form round above removed.

## Sequence emission

### Writer structure

Six bit writes per sequence merged into two u64 spellings on 2026-09-12, one for transition bits of at most 27 and one for add bits of at most 51, for another 4%. Transition bits and add bits then merged into one `hot_push` with a combined safe cap of 56 bits that falls back to a double push beyond it, cutting json instructions 0.4%.

`encode_sequences` reserves `nb_seq*11 + 32` bytes once, covering at most 51 add bits on the last sequence, at most 87 on earlier ones and the 8-byte flush-store overshoot. This replaces a per-push capacity probe with its `Vec` field load and branch per sequence, and the sequence stream and three FSE transition-row tables move to raw pointers, because the slice and `Vec` fields rode the stack through every iteration and were the function's hottest single instructions. Gungraun measured json.fastest a further 1.3% fewer instructions, cumulative 4.0% with the emit split, and json.fast 0.9%.

The loop's invariant half of each row address, `rows + (code << shift)`, is resolved once per block into 256-entry tables of raw pointers, so the hot loop reaches a transition entry with one rsp-addressed stack load plus one indexed load through it. The first generation stored u32 row offsets and OR'd the state in, but that kept a third live base register per channel and all three row bases reloaded from the stack every sequence. Instructions fell 0.7% to 0.8% on fastest and fast json and text, 0.15% to 0.19% on balanced and 0.18% on skewed.fast, whose low tiers stay on RLE one-state tables and miss the path. A SeqWord shrink from 24 to 16 bytes that traded the separate `add_nb` load for a packed width byte was falsified because the packing ALU lands in the hotter matcher emit.

Transition diffs are precomputed in the entries. A flat row is indexed by the state itself, so the emitted value `state - baseline` is a build-time constant per row position, and entries pack `(next << 16) | (nb << 12) | diff` with the run offset written at build time. Every consumer, `encode_sequences`, `FSEEncoder::encode` and `encode_interleaved`, reads the diff straight from the entry. This kills one subtract plus its feeding move per channel per sequence, and the three end-of-loop state copies disappear because entries land in registers that update in place via `shr 16`. Json fastest and fast moved 1.3% and 1.5% fewer instructions, text 0.9% and 1.2%, and skewed.fast 0.3%.

An output cursor replaced the byte-offset position and per-iteration output base reload on 2026-09-16. The loop had kept sixteen live values, reloaded the output base from its stack slot every iteration to form the flush store's address, and spilled the loop counter. It now carries one output cursor pointer advanced by each flush's byte count, derives the exit position once at the end, and terminates on the SeqWord pointer. Instructions fell 0.35% and 0.42% on fastest and fast json and 0.39% on fast text, with wall neutral because the loop is latency-bound and the two-level row-pointer loads and serial accumulator chain set its floor. The concat's variable shifts are at their floor for this entry form because `nb` is per-entry, as a symbol's double states split nb and nb+1 in `fse_encoder.rs`'s baseline walk, so per-code row-pointer tables cannot carry the widths.

### Table modes

Table mode selection ports libzstd's `selectEncodingType` fast branch: RLE single-code, a predefined threshold, and encoded mode with normalizeCount M2 normalization, `optimalTableLog` and a last-sequence count decrement. Json ratio moved 5.22 to 5.37.

Repeat mode is used when the previous block's table covers all active codes and the bit-cost estimate of `Σ c·log2` is at or below the new table's description plus the entropy lower bound. The whole table is then reused with zero description bytes. A predefined or RLE choice invalidates the remembered table, and a three-state `PrevTable::{New,Keep,Clear}` guards against table-state desync with the decoder. Json instructions fell 2.2% and ratio moved 5.99 to 6.00.

The RLE degenerate single-state FSE table sets table_size to 1 and takes exactly the same path as the normal modes, with zero special-case branches.

### Branch work

A mispredict differential study on json.fastest on 2026-09-14 used LBR `perf record -j any` with the `/M` flag on AMD to attribute about 3.1M misses per pass, about 17% of cycles. The single largest mispredicted branch was `encode_sequences`' bit-writer headroom check `nb+bits <= 63` at about 18% of misses, followed by the `scan_fast` rep OR-fold whose short-circuit `a == 0 || b == 0` compiled into three unpredictable branches at about 19%, `extend_match`'s u64-compare loop exits at about 9%, the two hash-match compares at about 9% and `insert_covered`'s mod-2 unroll parity guard at about 4%. Writing the fold as `(a == 0) | (b == 0)` with `a`-disambiguation on the taken path cut branch misses 1% and left wall neutral at json -0.5% within noise.

The headroom branch then disappeared entirely through `hot_push_raw`, which adopts libzstd's `BIT_flushBits` discipline. Every push flushes its whole pending bytes with `k = bits/8 <= 7`, one unaligned u64 store and `acc >>= 8k`, before OR-ing the payload. Every entry therefore has `bits < 8`, and any nb up to 56 fits. The trade paid 0.4% to 0.6% more instructions for eliminating the branch, and wall moved json.fastest 541 to 562 MiB/s (+4%), json.fast 425 to 447 (+5%), skewed.fastest 2758 to 2873 (+4%), text +1% and skewed.fast flat. The full-ladder dump is byte-identical. A branch worth about 18% of misses is worth about five times its instruction-count price in stores, and the reverse trade that folds stores to save under 1% of instructions, recorded in the [entropy negative results](../negative/entropy.md), never paid.

`insert_covered` peels its odd tail once and then inserts in explicit pairs. The old `while p < end` loop auto-unrolled mod 2 behind a per-entry parity guard at about 4% of json.fastest mispredicts. Instructions moved json +0.05% and text flat, and wall stayed neutral. Two alternatives lost: a plain counted `for k in 0..count` stayed rolled at +3.3% instructions, and a hand-unrolled counted loop with per-iteration `start + k` addressing cost +4.3%.

### NCount serialization

`FSETable::write_table` and `normalize_count` sat at about 3.3K and 2.9K instructions per json-4K encode. `write_table` paid a `BitWriter::write_bits` call plus an ilog2 width-ladder recompute per symbol. It now ports libzstd's `FSE_writeNCount_generic` shape onto the writer's hot state: `threshold` and `nb_bits` track ilog2 of the remaining value incrementally, the value and width arithmetic is libzstd's with `max = 2·threshold - 1 - remaining`, a widen at or above the threshold and one bit less below max, zero runs emit 24 zero chunks as one `0xFFFF` pair, and the description accumulates in a register u64 flushed as unaligned 8-byte stores through `hot_state` and `set_hot_state` after one reserve of `4 + 15·nsym` bits. `normalize_count` reslices both slices to the live alphabet once so the per-symbol loop indexes known-length slices without bounds checks. The zip-enumerate form cost more than the checks it removed because zip bookkeeping replaced them.

Per json-4K encode, `write_table` moved 3.29K to 3.01K instructions against C's 3.32K, and `normalize_count` 2.86K to 2.29K against C's 2.22K. Gungraun small measured json-4K 0.51% fewer instructions (x1.110 to 1.105), text-4K 0.44% (x1.082 to 1.077), 16K 0.14% to 0.15% and 64K 0.05%, with random unchanged. Wall moved json-4K 536 to 540 MiB/s and text-4K 367 to 394. Output is byte-identical, 223 tests pass, and the entry-flush bug the tests caught is in the [encoding pitfalls](../pitfalls/encoding.md).

## Huffman coding

### Code construction

Boundary package-merge produced optimal length-limited codes through the Larmore-Hirschberg algorithm, replacing an initial rank-weight ladder that depended on count order and lost heavily on skewed histograms. Every level gained, with json Ultra moving 7.33 to 7.52 and text fastest 196.6 to 206.0. The production path changed on 2026-09-18 to the two-queue build below, and package-merge remains a `cfg(test)` optimality reference.

`build_from_weights` uses a counting sort. Package-merge caps weights at 11, so twelve stack buckets with symbols scattered in ascending order reproduce the comparison sort's weight-ascending and symbol-ascending order exactly. That removes the per-block heap allocation and the O(n log n) sort. Text-4K small-payload encode gained 5% wall, instruction counts dropped on every text-shaped cell, no regression appeared elsewhere, and output is byte-identical. The earlier attrition rounds on the package-merge walk, covering scratch rework, fixed-capacity array stacks and per-level sort removal, are gone with the algorithm swap.

The two-queue tree build replaced package-merge in production. The package-merge walk itself was the residue: `build_from_counts_into` cost 26.7K instructions per call, split into 12K for the merge loop, 5.9K for package formation and 5.6K for backtrack, against libzstd's whole `HUF_buildCTable_wksp` at 10.5K, a 2.5x algorithm-class gap. Package-merge pays max_len levels of 2(n-1)-wide merges of 8-byte entries, while C builds an unlimited-depth Huffman tree in O(n) through a count bucket-sort scatter and a two-queue merge over sorted leaves with a count barrier at slot 0 and 2^30 markers for uncreated nodes. C pays a depth-limit pass through `HUF_setMaxHeight` Kraft repayment only when the unlimited tree exceeds 11 bits. Where unlimited depth fits, both are optimal, and the repayment's excess over package-merge measures at most 0.1% on every non-adversarial histogram and about 2% on a fibonacci-count adversary that C pays identically. `build_lengths_matches_optimal_and_is_kraft_exact` keeps package-merge as the test reference and checks Kraft-exactness plus at most 2% cost over alphabet sizes 2 to 256 and descending, fibonacci and random shapes. `HuffScratch` shrinks with the algorithm: seven package-merge stacks of about 38 KB become one 513-entry `NodeElt` table plus a 192-slot rank-position array of about 5 KB.

Output moves only on fresh json and text tables, by +2 to +92 bytes per file and at most 0.002%, while random, zeros and skewed stay byte-identical. All 30 dump files roundtrip bit-exact through the C decoder, the 120-cell sweep stays at +8.67% geo-mean with the same losing cells at json.fast mt -0.14% and -0.12%, and text.best holds its +0.13% lead. Gungraun measured json-4K 12.1% fewer instructions at x1.394 to 1.224, with the build itself falling from 26.7K to 5.9K per call, 0.57x C's own. Text-4K fell 20.3% at x1.484 to 1.182 because deeper text tables paid more package-merge, 16K fell 3.9% and 7.5%, 64K 1.3% and 2.6%, and random moved 0.1% either way. The 1 MiB encode bench is flat to better everywhere, with text fast -2.7% and fastest -1.7%. Wall moved json-4K x1.563 to 1.442, text-4K 1.716 to 1.419, json and text 16K 1.416 to 1.311 and 1.258 to 1.160, and 64K 1.487 to 1.432 and 1.479 to 1.390. The lesson is to diff the algorithm class against the reference implementation before attrition on a hot loop, because a 2.5x instruction gap in intrinsic per-block work is structural rather than codegen.

The weight-to-code pass now materializes one form per live symbol. It had produced three parallel forms, a `codes: Vec<(u32,u8)>`, a packed u16 and an aligned u64, where C fills one CElt. The struct literal zero-initialized packed and aligned inline, a 2.25 KB memset and 2.6K instructions per build, the largest remaining fixed cost, with a `codes` pool on top. `codes` is gone: every consumer reads the `nb` nibble straight from `packed` in `estimate_compressed_size`, `can_encode` and `write_weights_into`, the alphabet length moves to an `nsym` field, and entries past either alphabet count zero. The pool machinery and the per-build fill disappear with it. `aligned` moves behind a pooled `Box<[u64; 256]>`, dead symbols are never read because the stream encoder only indexes symbols the table covers, so boxes recycle without clearing and every byte stays initialized and clone-safe, and only the 512-byte `packed` keeps a zeroed dead prefix. Uniform-code detection folds into the bucket loop as one non-empty weight bucket, and `build_from_weights` is allocation-free.

`HuffmanTable` shrinks from about 2.6 KB inline to about 0.5 KB plus a box, so per-block adopt and replace copy a fifth of the bytes. The HuffmanTable-shaped memset residue falls from 4.1K to 1.6K instructions per call and `build_from_weights_slice` from 4.4K to 1.9K. Combined with the FSE round, gungraun measured json-4K x1.224 to 1.181 (-3.6% instructions), text-4K 1.182 to 1.148 (-2.8%), 16K -1.0% and -0.9%, and random-4K -1.6%, with all 60 benchmarks in the 1 MiB encode set flat or better. Wall moved json-4K 461 to 484 MiB/s (+4.6%), text-4K 376 to 390 (+3.7%) and json-16K +2.7%. The wall converts at more than the instruction rate because the removed work was memory-bound, from rep-stosb on never-read bytes and 2.6 KB struct moves. This is the counterpart lesson to the FSE half: instruction cuts in the small band only convert to wall where they also cut memory traffic. The full-ladder dump is byte-identical, the 120-cell sweep stays at +8.67%, and `pooled_calls_byte_identical` plus the rewritten packed-based tests, 201 in total, pass.

### Literals stream

The literals stream loop uses a left-aligned dual-accumulator form ported from libzstd's `HUF_CStream` on 2026-09-12. Entries live in a parallel `[u64; 256]` array with the code in the top `nb` bits and `nb` in the low nibble, so one load feeds `c >>= nb`, `c |= e` and `b += nb`. The OR of the raw element dirties the four low bits, and every following shift discards that dirt before it can reach the pending window, so only each round's last add needs a cleaned operand. The pending window grows downward from the container top, and a flush shifts it into the low bytes with one unaligned store and leaves the container untouched. Rounds are five symbols per container with two containers alternating, giving two independent shift and OR chains, and with codes up to 11 bits the margins hold at 7 + 4×11 fast and +11 clean, or 62 or less.

A BMI2 runtime dispatch is load-bearing. On the baseline target the legacy `shr r, cl` costs one move and one AND per symbol, and short-code corpora regress: skewed.fastest measured 4.9% more total instructions without it, recovered to +0.3% with `shrx`. Output is byte-identical, and the differential unit test covers 1 to 11-bit codes, entry alignments and uniform and non-uniform tables. Callgrind on dll32 fast moved the huff0 stream from 88.8M to 50M instructions against libzstd's own 45.7M, closing the 2x per-symbol gap at 14.5 to 10.7 instructions per symbol on dll codes and about 7.2 on skewed's short codes. Dll total fell 3.7%. Gungraun measured json fastest and fast -3.9% and -1.8%, text -2.6% and -1.1%, balanced json and text -1.1% and -0.4%, best, opt and ultra 0.15% or less, and zeros and random +0.1% to +0.4% from the extra 2 KB aligned-array initialization per table build. Wall stayed within noise on the 32 MiB shapes because the loop is about a 5% share of cycles there and latency-tolerant.

The whole-element stream discipline then removed the remaining per-symbol work on 2026-09-20, in the dll100 differential round described in [enc-dll-gap](../bench/enc-dll-gap.md). The dual-accumulator loop still paid an `nb` extract of a move plus `and $0xf`, a `shr %cl` and a separate count add, at 11.1 instructions per literal on dll codes against libzstd's 7.2. Libzstd's `HUF_CElt` is one `size_t` with nb in the low byte and the code left-aligned, and one element operation serves as the shift count because x86 masks it to 6 bits, as the OR payload, and as the whole-element add into the bit counter whose low byte stays exactly the sum of nb because carries only propagate upward. Our table already had that layout because nb sits in the low nibble with bits 4 to 7 zero, so the loop now runs `$c >>= e & 63; c |= e; b = b.wrapping_add(e)`, reading the counter's exact low bits as `& 0xff` for the flush test, `& 0x3f` for the container shift because the round bound keeps the true value at 62 or less, and `& 7` for the carried remainder.

The BMI2 dispatch was also broken in effect: `write_packed_codes_dual_bmi2` delegated to the non-feature implementation, which LLVM compiled once as a tail jump, so no `shrx` body existed. The implementation is now `inline(always)`, so the wrapper carries its own feature-compiled copy, because a delegation is not a feature-compiled copy. Output is byte-identical across the 30-file full-ladder dump and dll100 l1, l3 and l9 xor-parity. Dll100 instructions moved l1 -4.0% at 3,792.5M to 3,639.2M, l3 -2.0% and l9 -0.4%. Gungraun encode at 1 MiB measured json fastest and fast -1.60% and -0.87%, text -1.04% and -0.61%, balanced json -0.47%, best, opt and ultra -0.01% to -0.14%, and skewed, random and zeros 0.001% or less. Wall moved dll100 fastest +0.8% interleaved at 392 to 395 MiB/s, median of three, and fast stayed flat because the loop's serial shift and OR chains bound it.

An AVX-512VBMI kernel packs 4-bit codes for uniform alphabets of 9 to 16 symbols. It processes 64 symbols per batch with two `permutex2var_epi8` operations indexing a 256-entry lookup plus two permutes to un-interleave paired nibbles. Skewed instructions fell 48% and throughput rose 43%.

An AVX2 tier covers dispatches the AVX-512 kernel cannot take, in 32-symbol chunks, and landed on 2026-10-01. A 256-entry byte lookup has no AVX2 permute counterpart, so the table runs as one `vpcmpeqb` and `vblendvb` select per live slot. A flat 4-bit table admits at most sixteen present symbols by Kraft, and the slot list is one scan of `packed`'s nonzero entries. The loaded codes swap lanes once because chunk bytes 16 to 31 feed output bytes 0 to 8 and `vpshufb` is lane-local. Per-lane every-other-byte shuffles and a `vpsllw` by 4 fold the reversed nibble pairs, and one `vpunpcklqdq` stores the 16 output bytes. With the histogram twin below, this is the whole AVX2 tier: skewed.fastest forced to AVX2 against forced scalar measured 18.2% fewer instructions at 7.58G to 6.21G per 20 passes and wall 1493 to 1926 MiB/s (+29%, medians of six interleaved same-binary environment-flip rounds), against an AVX-512 control at 2924. Json and text fast read 1.0014 and 0.9996 of baseline instructions. The dispatch gates on `!wide_ok`, so on wide hardware the two-to-three-group corner under the 64-symbol chunk stays scalar because the fixed 256-entry slot scan loses there. Without the gate, default-path skewed paid +0.19% instructions. Forced-tier dumps are byte-identical across 30 files and both tiers.

### Literals table serialization

The per-block weight FSE description now matches libzstd as of 2026-09-16. ## Block splitting

### Post-parse splitter

libzstd's post-parse block splitter is ported in `blocks/split.rs` as of 2026-09-16. One 128 KiB block's staged sequences are recursively bisected as `ZSTD_deriveBlockSplits` does, a split commits when the halves' estimated sizes beat the whole range's, and each committed range is emitted as its own block. The estimator runs the real table builds and the same selection the emission uses against the block-start entropy state: the Huffman description through `write_table_desc`, the FSE description through `write_table` into a scratch buffer, and stream costs through `estimate_compressed_size` and `repeat_bit_cost`. Estimates therefore track emission rather than bound it. `MIN_SEQUENCES_BLOCK_SPLITTING` is 300, the split cap is 196, and the gate is exactly `ZSTD_resolveBlockSplitterMode`'s auto rule, `Strategy::Opt` and a window of at least 2^17 after shape adjustment, exposed as `Matcher::block_splitting_enabled`.

Emission rolls the remembered tables across the cut, so a later partition repeats the table the previous one wrote and the treeless and repeat savings survive. The net per-stream outcome of New, Keep or Clear against the block start feeds the caller's stock adoption. The speed tax is about `(2·splits+1)·3` estimates per block, each one Huffman build and at most three FSE builds, measured at 0.2% to 1.4% of the cells.

The deviation from C is the incompressible-partition fallback. Libzstd reconciles parser and decoder repcode histories per partition through `ZSTD_seqStore_resolveOffCodes`, so any partition may fall back raw mid-split. Here an incompressible partition restarts the whole block unsplit from the block-start entropy state and recycles the abandoned tables. This is correct by construction because no partition was emitted and the decoder's repcode history never diverged, and it is unreachable in practice because every partition carries at least 150 sequences whose encoded form cannot lose to its raw bytes. The rep-rewrite machinery stays unported until a corpus fires the restart, traceable through a counter if ever needed.

Results: json.opt bulk single-thread moved 4,484,059 to 4,481,374 (-0.060%), closing the recorded json.opt single-thread residue, with the sweep reading 4,481,370 against the reference's 4,481,382. Json.opt MT and stream cells moved +0.07%, json.ultra -1,167 bytes single-thread and +0.02% to +0.11% across cells, and text opt and ultra -4 and -10 bytes because its 128 KiB blocks rarely split and the reference only splits 2 to 5 blocks there. ### Pre-splitter

libzstd's pre-splitter is ported in `encoding/pre_split.rs` as of 2026-09-23, as an exact integer port of `ZSTD_splitBlock`, differential-pinned against a C harness over every 128 KiB block of dll100, dll32, json, text, skewed, zeros and random at all five levels, with a committed fixture and an environment-gated whole-file harness test. Before parsing, each full 128 KiB block's content shape decides its own boundary. Level 0, `fromBorders`, compares 512-byte border byte-histograms and cuts at 32, 64 or 96 KiB around a middle histogram. Levels 1 to 4, `byChunks`, fingerprint every 8 KiB chunk with 2-byte Knuth hashes at a sampling rate of 43, 11, 5 or 1 and a table of 2^8 to 2^10, then cut at the first chunk whose fingerprint deviates from the merged history beyond `p50·(14+penalty)/16`. The penalty relaxes from 3 to 0 per agreeing chunk, and cuts land on 8 KiB multiples, the smallest block the splitter emits.

The gate is libzstd's `ZSTD_optimalBlockSize`. It runs only on windows of 128 KiB or more, and a forced or downsized window below that keeps the fixed grid. It runs only while the frame's verified savings stay at or above 3, where savings are input minus output accumulated per block and header bytes are included, tracked as `FrameSavings` in `CompressState`. The first block of a frame or MT job never splits, incompressible data never re-chunks, and the raw-block level is exempt because its grid is byte-identity-pinned. The reach probe's donated span and the streaming cores clamp the last span block at the 2 MiB boundary so every path makes identical decisions, and `pre_split_paths_stay_identical` pins the slice, Read-stream and staged-stream identity on a dll-shaped heterogeneous input for both detector families. The probe's own shrink-side cost parse keeps the fixed 128 KiB grid because its cost model merges literal histograms globally and the grid only enters through second-order parse feedback, a documented approximation. MT jobs accumulate savings per job from zero, mirroring libzstd's per-context counters.

Two deviations from the C gate are measured, while the strategy mapping otherwise follows libzstd's `splitLevels[]` row over our `LevelParams` family, with greedy and lazy at rate 11 and lazy2 at rate 5. Every non-lazy row is exempt. The single-probe rows pay 4% to 6% of their throughput for noise-level deltas, with dll100 fastest at -4.3% wall for -0.24% size. The tree-fill rows restart their per-block tree state on every cut, with dll100 best at -10% wall for +0.06% size at btlazy2. The opt rows additionally carry the post-parse splitter, which content-cut inputs degrade: dll100 opt and ultra grow +1.06% and +0.96% in size with the cuts on, against C's own +0.12% and +0.06% gains, and our unsplit opt rows already sit about 10% denser than C's, so the headroom is negative.

## Histograms and incompressibility

The pre-match incompressibility gate skips the match search on a block that is near-certainly raw. It runs one strided content-tag probe pass with slot equal to the high 15 hash bits of a sampled 8-byte window, entry equal to the low 32 bits, and about 2000 samples per 128 KiB block. Any hit keeps the matcher running, so duplicated data with uniform entropy still parses. Only a clean probe triggers an exact 4-lane byte histogram, gated at Miller-Madow-corrected 7.97 bits per byte or more.

The design is ratio-safe. The opt tree's fill is lazy from `next_update`, so a skipped block stays available as later match history and `update_tree` clamps its catch-up fill to the live window. The table strategies of fast, dfast and chain track a `gap_start` watermark opened at the gated block's start, and the next scanning block's `catch_up_insertions` dense-fills `[gap_start, block_start)` window-clamped and oldest to newest before its first probe, exactly the state a full scan's miss-path inserts would have left. The watermark is written from the gate side only, so the three scan-loop bodies stay byte-identical to the ungated build, because any growth there re-rolls the fast tiers' code layout, the placement lottery recorded in the [entropy negative results](../negative/entropy.md).

Two sampled distinct micro-screens keep never-gating corpora out of the full pass: reject below 32 distinct after 64 samples and below 96 after 512, where uniform bytes expect 57 and 221. Sixteen-letter skewed and json exit at 64 samples, and text-like 76-symbol alphabets exit at 512, so the gate costs 0.1% to 0.5% of instructions on compressible shapes. The entropy decision must be exact because a 2016-sample Miller-Madow estimate of maximum-entropy input has a standard deviation of about 0.11 bits. The correction fixes the mean and not the spread, so the 7.97 bar would coin-flip per block, and a wrongly kept block forces a full scan plus catch-up fill at hundreds of times the histogram cost.

Random.best moved 12 to 1574 MiB/s against libzstd-12's 615, random.opt 1573 against 14 (112x) and random.ultra 1542 against 6 (257x). Extending to the table levels, random.fast moved 1469 to 1678 (x1.39 to 1.25 against libzstd-3), random.balanced 1405 to 1688 (x1.29 to 1.09 against libzstd-6) and random.fastest 1652 to 1681 (x1.33 to 1.32). The full-corpus full-ladder dump is byte-identical and the 120-cell ratio sweep stays flat.

A sticky `gate_hold` landed on 2026-09-12. Once a block clears the exact pass, later blocks gate on the probe alone until any rejection clears the hold, so the histogram runs once per incompressible run instead of once per block. The histogram sits at the x86 memory-op floor of 3 memory ops per byte, a byte load plus an RMW load and store, at 3 memory ops per cycle for about 1.04 cycles per byte measured through perf annotate, so the pass could only be skipped and never cheapened, and u64-load extracts or unrolling only trade non-bound ALU. The risk model is that a mis-held block needs at least 208 distinct sampled bytes, zero repeated 8-grams in the strided probe and exact entropy below 7.97 directly after a gated block, and the loss is only the literals Huffman saving, which at 208 or more distinct symbols cannot beat integer code lengths plus the table description. The result is byte-identical on the full-ladder dump, on a random-to-text transition file at level 1, and on a crafted flat-232-symbol adversary with a 16 MiB random prefill to arm the hold, at levels 1 and 19. Random tiers moved 1.68 to 2.4-2.5 GB/s and run ahead of libzstd at all six levels: fastest 2464 against 2157, fast 2569 against 2044, balanced 2505 against 1525, best 2452 against 218, opt 2427 against 9 and ultra 2253 against 7. Solo `prof enc` level 1 instructions fell 61% from 4.58G to 1.79G, and the gate symbol is gone from the profile after being 37.9% of cycles.

The probe is the job's own history rather than its residue, as fixed on 2026-09-17. `prefill_window` and `adopt_strip_snapshot` replay the per-block sampling over the strip with block-sized segments and per-segment stride and phase, mirroring what a from-scratch scan of the strip would plant, and `reset` clears the filter so pooled matchers gate like fresh ones. Before that, the probe accumulated across jobs un-cleared and un-seeded, so MT verdicts and bytes depended on which worker ran which job, and a block whose only twins live in the matchable strip gated away, which killed the far class for window strips the armed LDM serves and lost the near class for reach strips. The replay must copy the scanner's sampling geometry exactly, because a single whole-strip stride is a modular lottery. Gated blocks also leave `fed` behind `block_start`, which the head-phase fill then tripped on a stale rolling hash, and `LdmState::fill` now re-arms at its base, as the prologue `generate` always had.

The literals histogram uses 4-way sub-tables split by position mod 4 to avoid store-forwarding serialization on the same counters. Skewed cycles fell 7% and throughput rose 10%. An AVX-512 exact histogram for alphabets of 16 symbols or fewer uses a 16-slot `cmpeq_epi8_mask` plus popcount accumulation, and the popcount sum doubles as 17th-symbol detection, with a scalar fallback only when uncontaminated. Skewed instructions fell 18% and throughput rose 28%.

The AVX2 tier of that histogram landed on 2026-10-01 and shrinks the per-slot compares to two 32-byte vectors per 64-byte step, with `vpcmpeqb`, `vpmovmskb` and popcnt per slot. It commits only when the counts sum to exactly 64, because a shortfall means a novel byte and the scalar absorb recounts the chunk, so committing the vector counts too would double them. The AVX-512 kernel's permute-LUT novel-byte test is replaced by the compare sum itself. It shares the uniform-4 pack's forced-tier numbers.

A separate Miller-Madow sampling rejection gate uses about 1024 strided points, bias correction, a distinct-above-208 pre-screen and a sticky `gate_hold`, and rejects only at 8 bits per byte or more. Random instructions fell 43% and throughput rose 43% from 1450 to 2060 MiB/s, overtaking libzstd level 1. A literals entropy lower-bound pre-check uses an exact histogram, a Shannon bound and an 8% margin to skip the Huffman attempt when it cannot beat raw, with the histogram and table build sharing one scan. The fused pass splits lanes so each channel has four sub-histograms of 12 by 1 KB, which breaks store-forwarding serialization on repeated-code runs. It gates at `nb_seq >= 128` because the 12 KB zero and merge does not pay on tiny blocks. `choose_tables_fast` cycles fell from 2.83% to 2.00% on json.fast, and gungraun measured -0.07%.

Sequence-code histograms are sized to the 64-entry code space through `SEQ_CODE_SPACE`, whose wire codes are LL at most 35, ML at most 52 and OF at most 31. Instructions fell 0.4% and 0.5% on json and text fastest and fast.

Lane histograms are pooled with a prefix-only clear. The 12 KB lane zeroing in `choose_tables_fast` was the largest per-block memset left on the fast path: callgrind on a json-4K fastest call put it at 12.3K instructions of 240K, 5.1%, with `__memset_avx2` running rep-stosb at about one instruction per byte, and it engaged at `nb_seq >= 128` so every block in the 4K-64K band paid it. ## Checksum

The in-house XXH64 uses eight accumulation chains and two 32-byte reads per iteration, with eight chains in flight giving 8% over four. It is shared with the decoder, `twox-hash` is demoted to a development dependency, and there are no external runtime dependencies. An AVX-512 `vpmullq` vector core was a negative result because it serializes the dependency chain, and eight interleaved scalar chains are the right answer.

Three passes fuse with the checksum. The RLE uniformity scan runs XXH64 rounds while comparing, so uniform-block hashing completes in the single pass and a mismatch returns a 32-byte-aligned resume offset to continue. Raw-block copies absorb the hash while copying. The zero-sequence-block gate rejects with an early exit. Zeros gained 22% and random 10%.

A sidecar thread offloads hashing on std plus hash builds with inputs of at least 256 KiB and at least two cores. A thread-local SPSC ring of 512 slots posts block bytes to a dedicated worker, and a `BlockChecksum` trait unifies the inline and offload paths. Random gained 13%, text 26%, skewed 14%, zeros 2% and json 4%.

The worker parks on a condition variable after a bounded spin of about 300 microseconds. A budget of 80 microseconds was not enough because the raw-block pipeline paces at worker saturation, so wakeup latency lands directly on the critical path. The head flip is published under the lock to prevent lost wakeups. Saturated runs see about one voluntary context switch per frame and zero ticks while idle. On the MT path the checksum is absorbed in parallel and inline in the calling thread, and during streaming bursts the main thread hashes.

## Bit writing and miscellaneous

The `BitWriter` 64-bit flush uses a single unaligned u64 store instead of an 8-byte memcpy, gaining 1% to 2% on all corpora. Sequence codes are computed once and shared, so `choose_table` and `encode_sequences` consume the same code arrays and the META lookup is split from add bits, with identical output bits. A single-pass precomputation packs codes into a u32 stream and pre-merges add bits into u64, gaining 3% on json. The matcher pushes the packed sequence stream directly through `Matcher::start_matching_codes`, cutting json instructions 3.5%, with details on the [matchers page](matchers.md).

RLE detection is chunked by u64 because the original closure's byte-wise indexing could not be vectorized. An RLE block's `skip_matching` inserts only the first position because all 5-byte windows of a uniform run land in the same slot, which gained 7.3x on zeros. After match emission, a short literal overrun-overwrite copy handles runs of 8 bytes or less with `reserve(8)`, an unaligned read and write, and `set_len`. The read runs into the following match, and the write overflow of at most 7 bytes is overwritten by subsequent pushes.

Uniform block detection uses four batched u64 compares and is deliberately `#[inline(never)]`. Inlining it into large callers made the loop lose its data pointer and reload it from the stack every 32 bytes, halving throughput.

## Frame shaping for small payloads

A CLI-to-CLI comparison of zstdx `-l -c` against zstd 1.5.7 `-q -l -c` on corpus slices at offsets 0, 1M and 16M measured text at +7.5% to +13.7% output, or +68 to +137 per mille, at both level 1 and level 9 over 1-64 KiB, and json 4K at +4% at level 1. The 16K-64K band shrank, and json already won at levels 3 and 9. Frame-parser section diffs from rebuilt tooling covering FHD, block, literals and sequence decode plus `FSE_readNCount` emulation ranked four mechanisms. Frame overhead cost +4 bytes on every frame, from a trailing empty raw block on grid-exact inputs because the streaming reader cannot mark a just-filled block last until a read returns EOF, and from a window descriptor where libzstd writes the single-segment form, 13 per mille of a 300-byte frame. cParams row selection was missing because libzstd switches `ZSTD_defaultCParameters` tables at 256, 128 and 16 KiB, with searchLength 4 through the lazy family and its 16 KiB table swapping levels 9 and 10 from lazy2 to btlazy2 at searchLog 5, while our rows never keyed on size. Parser shape at level 1 used large-input bars, with the miss ramp `1 + miss>>2` against libzstd's near-unstepped small-row scan and the `ml >= 6` accept bar. Literal Huffman descriptions cost +6 to +10 bytes because we always built at tableLog 11 where libzstd caps by `highbit(srcSize-1)-1`. The entropy-side suspects were quantified out: sequence sections sat at parity, both CLIs default the checksum on, and reject-floor margins moved 10 bytes or less. The gap was frame shape, row policy and parse bars, with entropy a tail.

Frame shape closed next. The slice path marks its final real block last and emits no empty tail block.
