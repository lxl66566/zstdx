# Research map

Ranked map of the structural levers that stay unfalsified at the R97 state of the tree, for the next optimization rounds. Shortlist rows 1, 2 and 5 executed on 2026-11-04 and are marked; rows 6 and 7 are the R98 additions. The R98 round map below records the wall inventory, the arithmetic that killed the R97 structural seeds, and the zstd 1.5.7 C-source mining that found nothing new to port. No new measurement backs this page: every wall, share and cell value cites its page, and every expected gain is arithmetic on recorded numbers. Each proposal names the falsifications it must dodge, by entry, so a build round can check them before starting. The x notation is our time divided by the reference time, so below 1 means faster. Machine, corpus and pass facts are those of [snapshot](bench/snapshot.md), and the exactness classes follow the repo's gate discipline: byte-identical by construction, decode- or schedule-internal with output bytes unable to move, gated with the dump ladders and the 120-cell sweep; gated with size bars, bytes moving by design and named bars pricing them; and ratio trades, a size axis spent to buy speed.

MT decode is this crate's exclusive territory, since libzstd exposes no MT decode API, and none of the sub-structures below has an external reference implementation, so every cost model here is built from the recorded walls alone.

### Seed evaluations from the R97 structural pass

The structural seeds of the round were evaluated by arithmetic on recorded numbers. All four died; the entries below record the kills so no round re-derives them.

Decoder-side content-adaptive piece depth. The idea was to derive per-job piece geometry D from the staged records' own crossing-read statistics, with no encoder cooperation, and engage `mt_pieces` when the derived D meets the recorded pricing condition. The engagement condition fails on every measured shape: `mt_pieces` prices a grid only when every piece keeps a top at least 3/8 of the job size below its start, and the piece-watermarks data measured the binding depth at the shallowest crossing read, offsets of 100 to 400 B co-occurring in every early prefix, so pieces on json.zst3 64 MiB solo completed in a serial chain about 8.5 ms apart, mt4 at 1.23x against the serial-B design's 1.47x, skewed.zst9 at 0.90x, and the derived D on every recorded frame is about 400 B, pricing a pipeline at J/(J-400), about 1.0. Cut placement cannot rescue it, because deepest-read-early selection is falsified on the same data and non-uniform lattices inherit the shallow-first-read law: nearest-match offsets and repcodes open every output region. The derivation itself has no cheap form. Stage A keeps wire offsets, so a repcode's resolved depth is unknown to any staged-record scan; the exact form is a serial repcode-history pass over the whole frame's records, 6.09M on skewed.zst9 and a double-digit-milliseconds share of the 19.5 ms wall it would serve, and the conservative form, treating every repcode sequence as a shallow read, rejects every cut on rep-bearing shapes, where dll100 fastest runs 33.8 percent repcode sequences and json about 11. ### C-source mining, zstd 1.5.7

The reference at /root/programs/fork/zstd, lib/ at d9c0c7e2, was walked for structural techniques this tree has not ported and for format-legal degrees of freedom the C implementation does not exercise. Nothing new prices; the findings follow so no round re-derives them.

- `ZSTD_seqToCodes`' longOffsets and `ZSTD_maxShortOffset` are 32-bit-only armoring, `MEM_32bits() && ofCode >= STREAM_ACCUMULATOR_MIN`: on 64-bit the bitstream accumulator covers the whole window and the long-offset decode arm never selects. Nothing to port.
- `ZSTD_decompressSequencesLong`, the decode-ahead prefetch decoder, selects in C when `usePrefetchDecoder = dctx->ddictIsCold`. Its premise, source-load latency a decoder can hide behind decode-ahead, is the falsified prefetch and ring-pipeline entry in [negative decoding](negative/decoding.md): Zen4's hardware prefetchers and memory-level parallelism hide the latency below the price of the decode-ahead ring.
- Dedicated dictionary search, the bucket-sorted ddss tables of `ZSTD_dedicatedDictSearch_lazy_*` with `ZSTD_LAZY_DDSS_BUCKET_LOG` and extra attempts, buys dictionary candidate coverage. This tree's dictionary rows measured coverage complete after the width-4 fill, so the bucket layout has no row to buy; the residue is the fragmentation and tie classes of todos 6 and 19.
- `ZSTDMT_serialState`, the frame-continuous LDM feed: landed for the opt rows in R93; the btlazy feed port needs the adopted window, which is the falsified geometry, so C's form stays closed here.
- `ZSTD_compressLiterals`' stream rules, singleStream below 256 literals and the repeat-valid single-stream arm below 1 KiB, are ported both. The format's freedom to run one literal stream at any size is priced by the small-band seed evaluation above: under every land bar on the recorded cells.
- `ZSTD_c_rsyncable`, content-defined job grids, is a CLI compat feature whose content-defined machinery is measured and falsified here through the CDC index entry in [negative MT](negative/mt-stream.md).
- Superblock splitting stays falsified on every measured shape and the pre and post splitters are ported.
- The one format-legal channel the C encoder never uses is skippable frames as an encoder-side metadata carrier for decoder hints. Every measurable hint dies by recorded arithmetic: the pre-scan's table fold already reads 35 to 42 µs per 256 blocks, so a hint map saves nothing; piece-cut depth hints cannot manufacture deep crossing reads on parses whose minimum cut depth is 100 to 400 B, the piece-watermarks data; and a checksum shortcut is impossible by construction. Dead on arrival.

## Restart-poor MT decode, todo 1

### The stage-A staging-rate wall, re-derived

skewed.zst9 carries 33.6 MB of output, 6.09M sequences and a staged stream of 73.1 MB of sequences plus 2.0 MB of literals, behind two restart points at 16 MiB; the traffic table is in [decoding](perf/decoding.md). Current mt8 and mt16 cells read about 1040-1066 MiB/s, walls of 31.5 to 32.3 ms, against a 982 MiB/s single-thread control. Stage B's execute after the R90 port runs 7.8 ms per 16 MiB segment, 15.6 ms for the frame, so the first segment's staging holds about 16.6 ms of the 32.2 ms wall. The single-thread path decodes the same frame in 34.2 ms, of which about 18.6 ms is the entropy-decode share, 9.3 ms per segment, so one staging worker runs a 16 MiB segment at about 1.0 GB/s of output against the single-thread path's about 1.8 GB/s: the staging body keeps wire offsets, so repcodes stay unresolved until stage B, and the segment writes 36.5 MB of staged sequences. With two segments the wall is one 16 MiB staging plus two executes, and worker count changes neither term. The restart points belong to the frame, the per-worker staging rate is the falsified body-widening territory, and every representation lever on the staged set is measured and closed, so restart-poor frames hold about x1.06 single-thread.

The wall has one unpriced degree of freedom left: staging starts are confined to restart points, and a restart point is only where every table re-describes. A restart point is the sufficient condition for a cold start, which the scan enforces because a cold worker needs no carried state. It is a stronger condition than staging requires, and the gap between the two is proposal 1.

### Proposal 1: mid-epoch stage-A segmentation

Mechanism. Between two restart points, the only state stage A carries across block boundaries is the entropy tables. Literal Huffman streams, FSE descriptions and sequence bitstreams are per block, repcode history never touches stage A, and the pre-scan already parses every block's literals and sequence headers through `probe_restart`, so every block boundary is a known input offset with known section modes. Define the table epoch of a block as the fold of table transitions since the last full restart: a Compressed-literals block re-establishes the Huffman table while its FSE modes may stay Repeat, so Huffman and FSE state must fold separately. A sub-segment may start at any block boundary whose table state the worker holds. One snapshot per table state, a few KiB of FSE scratch plus Huffman table, is built once from the descriptions and cloned per staging lease, so the priming cost per sub-segment is a clone, and the description replay runs at header-parse rate.

The planner keeps its current target multiples and snaps a segment start to the first restart point past the target when one exists within 1.5x of it, and to the nearest block boundary otherwise. Restart-dense frames keep today's segmentation exactly, and only restart-poor epochs split. Segment bookkeeping, the staged-literal zero tail, the per-segment wild cap and the inline checksum absorb all work per segment unchanged, so stage B sees the same machinery at a finer granularity.

Target cells. skewed.zst9 mt8 and mt16 at about 1040-1066 MiB/s, about x1.06 of the 982 MiB/s single-thread control; skewed.zst19 mt8 and mt16 at about 2920 against a single-thread bulk cell at 2629-2855, the other restart-poor frame, whose two 16 MiB segments each stage about 14 MiB of literals. json.zst3, dll100.zst3 and text.zst3 mt cells are the no-regression controls, at about 1.5, 1.8 and 0.98 times single-thread respectively.

Exactness: byte-identical by construction. Decode output is untouched, staging is internal, and stage B stays segment-serial.

Expected gain with arithmetic. Splitting each 16 MiB epoch into eight 2 MiB block-aligned sub-segments puts the staging latency at two waves of about 2.1 ms per worker at mt8, about 4.5 ms with lease handoff, against today's 16.6 ms. The wall becomes about 4.5 plus 15.6 ms of serial execute, about 20 ms, so about 1680 MiB/s, x1.7 of single-thread; at mt16 one wave lands the wall near 18 ms, about 1850 MiB/s, and the ceiling is the 15.6 ms execute plus handoff, about x1.85 to 1.9 single-thread. At mt4, four rounds of staging keep the wall near 24 ms, about 1400 MiB/s, a third over the cell. The aggregate staging traffic, 73 MB of staged writes inside about 4 ms, about 18 GB/s beside stage B's round trip, must not collapse under contention; the R94 padding probe put skewed's bandwidth-bound stage-B wall share at only 0.12 to 0.15, so the headroom exists on paper and the A/B must confirm it.

Gate recipe. Extend the `probe_restart` unit tests with the fold classes: a Compressed-literals block mid-epoch with all-Repeat FSE, partial FSE rebuilds with a single explicit mode, Treeless chains across Raw and RLE literals blocks, and zero-sequence blocks. Add a debug-mode equivalence test that stages every candidate sub-segmentation and compares against whole-segment staging on the corpus plus crafted frames. Then the dump ladders `dump --all-levels` and `dump --stream --all-levels` diff-empty, mtcheck over corpus, big and corrupt, the 45k-case parity sweep with MT arms 2, 4 and 8, and the paranoid, debug, release and no-default-features suites. The perf gate is a locked position-balanced interleaved A/B, five or more rounds per side, on skewed.zst9 and skewed.zst19 at mt4, mt8 and mt16, with json.zst3, dll100.zst3 and text.zst3 mt8 and every single-thread control flat within 1 to 2 percent.

Kill conditions. Any control cell regresses more than 2 percent; the equivalence test finds a fold divergence; mt16 loses to today's build, which would mean the staging write burst contends with the execute beyond the R94 shares.

Falsifications to dodge. The stage-A body widening, the direct literal placement, the in-dst zone staging, the packed staged-record family including the SIMD-expansion reopen, and the tail prefetch in [negative decoding](negative/decoding.md) all stay dodged because stage A's decode body, the staged representation and the executor are untouched. The piece-watermarks falsification in the same file, whose data serializes stage B, stays dodged by keeping stage B segment-serial. Restart-position grid discovery in [negative MT](negative/mt-stream.md) falsified the restart lattice as a *job-grid signal* for the piece executor; proposal 1 uses block boundaries as staging starts and inherits none of that premise. The in-dst-zones reopen condition, cache-clean staging warmth, is untouched because the staging buffers stay pooled scratch.

Landed R96 (2026-11-04). The locked interleaved A/B measured skewed.zst9 mt8 at 1061 to 1725 MiB/s and mt16 at 1062 to 1746, skewed.zst19 mt8 at 2914 to 6374 and mt16 at 2914 to 6500, with the json, dll100 and text controls flat and the single-thread controls inside 0.2 percent. The probe splits the skewed.zst9 mt8 wall at 13.7 ms of serial execute against the predicted 15.6, plus a 3 to 4.5 ms first-batch tail, so the ceiling landed at x1.78 of single-thread, at the low end of the predicted 1.85 to 1.9 band, and mt16 sits on mt8 because the 256 KiB input floor prices the grid at 15 segments either way. ### Proposal 6: head-ramp segmentation for the first-batch staging tail

Mechanism. The R96 planner cuts at target multiples, `input/(workers*2)` floored at 256 KiB of input, and on skewed.zst9 the first lease stages about 2 MiB of output, so the consumer waits 1.9 to 4.5 ms behind its staging plus handoff before executing its first byte. Nothing prices the first cut at the target: the tail is a pure cold-start latency term, and the per-segment machinery, bookkeeping, zero tails, wild caps, priming and inline absorb, is the same at any size. A head ramp cuts the first few segments at a smaller floor, for example a head floor of 128 KiB of input for the first cut doubling per cut up to the target, with every cut on a block boundary so the mid-epoch priming handles each start. The rest of the grid, the restart snapping and the staged representation stay untouched, stage B stays segment-serial, and the total staging work is unchanged: the consumer starts executing after about 0.6 to 0.8 MiB of output staging, about 0.7 ms at the recorded per-worker rate of about 1.0 GB/s plus handoff, instead of 1.9 to 4.5 ms. Restart-dense frames keep their restart-snapped grids wherever the snap lands within 1.5x of the ramped target, so their segmentation moves at most in the first two cuts.

Target cells. skewed.zst9 mt8 at 1725 and mt16 at 1746 MiB/s, skewed.zst19 mt8 at 6374 and mt16 at 6500, with the serial execute of 13.7 ms and 2.3 ms respectively as the floors below the tail. json.zst3, dll100.zst3 and text.zst3 mt8 are the controls.

### Proposal 7: the skewed.zst19 per-call serial residue split

Mechanism. The R96 probe leaves about 2 ms of skewed.zst19's 5.3 ms mt8 wall unattributed between the inline checksum absorb and the staged-buffer teardown, against an execute sum of only 2.3 ms, so the residue is 38 percent of the cell. The absorb has a price model: the concurrent-hash falsification recorded the L3-warm pass rate at about 1.6 ms per 32 MiB, so 33.6 MB prices at about 1.7 to 1.8 ms and the teardown share is about 0.2 to 0.3 ms. A throwaway probe splits the two on the skewed.zst19 mt cells, timing the absorb and the pool return path per call. If the teardown share reads at or above 0.5 ms, the lever moves the return off the consumer's serial span: the staging pool is a global mutex-protected freelist, the consumer's per-segment drops return buffers under the same lock the staging workers allocate under, and the workers can return their own buffers at lease completion while the pool already recycles across calls. If the split reads absorb-dominated, the row closes with the residue attributed.

Target cells. skewed.zst19 mt8 at 6374 and mt16 at 6500 MiB/s; skewed.zst9 mt cells read a small carry, their 33.6 MB absorb hides inside the 13.7 ms execute.

Exactness: byte-identical by construction. The probe measures; a lever keeps the staged set, the executor and the absorb unchanged and moves only the buffer-return scheduling.

Expected gain with arithmetic. Bounded by the teardown share of the about 2 ms residue: at the 0.5 ms opening bound the zst19 wall reads about 4.8 ms, about +10 percent to about 7000 MiB/s; the absorb price model reads the likely outcome as zero, with the residue attributed to intrinsic serial xxh64 over 33.6 MB.

Gate recipe. Probe-only first, no output gates. A lever build takes the dump ladders, mtcheck over corpus, big and corrupt, the 45k-case parity sweep with MT arms 2, 4 and 8, and a locked interleaved A/B, eight rounds per side, on skewed.zst9 and skewed.zst19 at mt4, mt8 and mt16 with the json, dll100 and text mt8 controls and every single-thread control flat within 1 to 2 percent.

Kill conditions. The split reads the absorb at or above 1.5 ms of the 2 ms residue; any deferred return regresses the staging rate or any control cell by more than 2 percent.

Falsifications to dodge. The parallel chunked xxh64 merge is impossible by construction, the concurrent-hasher placement is falsified in [negative decoding](negative/decoding.md) with the inline absorb the landed optimum, and the staged-representation falsifications stay dodged, the set untouched.

## dll100 streaming-MT fastest encode, todo 17

### The pump wall, restated

The R95 decomposition in [mt-stream](perf/mt-stream.md) reads dll100 fastest stream-mt8 at a 109 ms span: the makespan job covering 52-68 MiB claims at t+15 ms, the moment the pump has fed its 68 MiB end at about 4 GB/s under the eight workers' memory traffic, then runs 84 ms; the caller's pump is 25.9 ms, of which 24.7 ms is the read itself, 100 pulls of 1 MiB, so the pump is about 95% memcpy at the loaded rate and its recoverable serialization is 1.1% of wall. The landed stepped finish drain moved the cell to 958-967 MiB/s against a 1059-1075 bulk-mt8 ceiling, so the remaining gap, about 15 ms, matches the feed latency of the makespan job's span. The wall is the copy rate itself, and the side-thread falsification in [negative MT](negative/mt-stream.md) names the reopen condition: the copy must get cheaper, and the borrowed-window forms are falsified below it.

The 2.12x against libzstd-mt8 on this cell is a non-iso-ratio comparison: zstd's 1971 MiB/s buys 48 MB of output against our 24 MB, recorded in [matrix](bench/matrix.md), so the row's own-ceiling distance is the honest target and the four levers below price it.

### The four feed levers, cost-modeled

The accumulate buffer's hugepage wiring is landed: `MADV_HUGEPAGE` scoped to the stream-mt buffer since 2026-09-12, saving about 3 ms per 32 MiB of first-touch faults, per [negative decoding](negative/decoding.md), and the same advice on the opt tables is falsified in [negative MT](negative/mt-stream.md). No hugepage lever remains on the pump path.

Non-temporal stores in the copy. ## Wildcards

Encoder-emitted restart density. Dead on arrival. Proposal 1 landed in R96 and buys the same staging parallelism at zero size cost without touching the parse, so the fallback condition that kept this knob probe-worthy is gone.

Same-CCD staging affinity. The in-dst-zones falsification in [negative decoding](negative/decoding.md) lost through cross-core ownership transfers and names cache-clean warmth as a reopen condition. An adjacent unmeasured form places the staging workers on the executor's own CCD, where Zen4 shares one L3 among eight cores: json.zst3's staged set, 26.7 MB, fits a 32 MiB per-CCD L3, and the bandwidth-bound stage-B share of 0.21 to 0.40 prices the read-side saving at up to a quarter of the json wall if the lines survive the concurrent staging writes. dll100's 93 MB staged set does not fit, so the lever is json-class only. A codec library should not pin threads, so the shape needs an opt-in placement knob and the pool is shared with the encode side, which doubles the policy cost. Probe-worthy at low priority, behind an explicit knob.

Multi-frame parallel decode. Concatenated-frame streams decode with independent state per frame, so frame-granular parallelism is sound by construction and composes with the per-frame two-stage machine. No bench payload measures concatenated frames today, so the lever has no cell: the payload comes first, a concatenated corpus stream plus a matrix cell, then the wall is the largest frame's decode plus assembly. Bench-first, probe-worthy only with that payload.

NT-store staged writes above L3 scale. The staged set is write-once, read-once, a pure streaming pattern, so NT stores on stage A's staged writes delete the RFO half of their traffic when the set exceeds L3: skewed.zst9's 73.1 MB and dll100.zst3's 93.3 MB qualify, json.zst3's 26.7 MB fits the L3 and must keep temporal stores so the executor reads stay cached. The R96 landing shrank the exposure from the staging-owned wall to the first-batch tail and the staging-ahead rate, and with proposal 6's head ramp the first lease is about 0.7 ms, so the rider shaves at most the RFO third of its staged-write traffic, about -1 to -2 percent of the skewed.zst9 mt wall; dll100's set stays hidden behind its restart-dense grid. Carry as a rider probe on proposal 6's build.

Decode-side hugepages. Falsified by data: the page-dispersity probe in [negative decoding](negative/decoding.md) shows decode insensitive to PFN layout at steady dTLB miss rates, and the MADV entry records the accumulate buffer as the only load-bearing scope. Dead on arrival.

Structure-of-arrays staged sequences. Splitting the 12 B record into three streams reads the same bytes with more loads and loses the single-record locality; the R94 traffic model prices width alone, and the pack and expansion forms are measured losses. Dead on arrival.
