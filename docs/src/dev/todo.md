## Item notes

### 1. MT decode scaling

Per-job content-adaptive D is the remaining structural idea. Pieces fully unchain only at D at or above the window, where crossing reads drop to zero. Between that point and the piece size, floor(D/J) pieces chain back through overlap. Below one piece the job is serial-chain. Ratio demands a D under the shape's dominant match period, and text-9 has a period of 800 KiB against a 4 MiB window, so no D satisfies it. Unpledged streams stay excluded because the growing grid's tail split depends on the encoder's worker count.

The landed walls cover the json mt8 verdict and execute stages, and they are memory-bound floors. No wall lever remains short of overlapping execute with the verdict, which is a falsified class. A beyond-noise regression on the piece-parallel path was traced to one decoder commit that fused bit reads, and that commit was reverted. The fused body's six-widths-six-bases live set fits only the flat executor's loop, and the staging pass spilled it. The per-callsite `const FUSED` monomorphization later restored the ST stream cells. Details are in [mt-stream](perf/mt-stream.md) and [negative](negative/mt-stream.md).

### 3. Fastest json scan

The structural levers are exhausted. The u32 idx-space resolve and the register-removal class in both cursor forms are falsified. The table geometry lever landed in the sanctioned form: the dense body scans a 2^13 half behind the covered-symbol bar, buying json fastest +9 to 10% wall on bulk ST and mt8 at -0.3% size with every other shape byte-identical, see [matchers](perf/matchers.md). A former defect past 2^32 plus max_window of a stream, where the u64 resolve rejected all hash candidates, is closed. ### 18. compat knobs without an `EncoderOptions` field

`include_dictid(bool)` landed as `EncoderOptions::include_dictid`, which suppresses the header field while the dictionary stays in effect. The options-based decode entries, `DecoderOptions::dictionary`, `DecoderOptions::parsed_dictionary` and the compat constructors, register the dictionary under the null id too, so a suppressed frame decodes when the dictionary is handed over. Libzstd applies a loaded dictionary unconditionally. The bulk MT dictionary path cannot express the suppression and demotes to the single-threaded dictionary entry, which is the same kind of MT engagement demotion as the small-input one. The FFI `ZSTD_c_dictIDFlag` is wired.

The remaining knobs are in place. `long_distance_matching(bool)` maps onto the `EncoderOptions::long_distance_matching(LdmMode)` tri-state, because the upstream bool expresses enable and auto only. `Enable` raises the window to 27 when none is forced, and `Disable` stands the arming down. The setting is wired through bulk and stream ST, the compat setters and FFI `ZSTD_c_enableLongDistanceMatching`. Both MT dispatches carry the tri-state into every job: `Disable` stands the capture lattice down, including dict frames and the stream far-dead screens, and `Enable` forces every job with the fast-row capture off, while `st_fallback` and the ST stream core keep the mode. `with_ref_prefix` on both stream sides is the raw-dictionary path under libzstd's prefix name. `ZSTD_c_refPrefix` loads raw content, which is exactly the engine's raw-dict load: no entropy, null id and format-default repcodes. Decode-side prefix registration rides the null-id dictionary work. The fidelity notes in the [user guide](../user.md) compat deviations still hold: an explicit enable below the 64 MiB full window leaves raw-block prefixes unindexed through the split pass's stock exemption, and the tagged rows at levels 5-8 and 10-12 have no LDM feed in their parse.

`set_target_cblock_size` stays unimplemented by verdict. The splitting machinery loses ratio on every measured shape and serves only fixed-size transport chunks, which this engine does not model. See [negative](negative/entropy.md).

Dictionary re-parse residues are closed. The compat `bulk::Compressor` raw attachment resolves its parse lazily at the first compression and caches the outcome until any re-set. Error timing stays: an invalid dictionary errors at the first compress call and at every later one, replayed from the cached raw bytes. `FrameCompressor::set_parsed_dictionary` shares one parse across frames, while the raw `set_dictionary` keeps its per-frame parse and panic contract. The other byte-dictionary resolvers, `bulk::compress_with`, the stream cores and the compat stream encoders' single materialization, are audited to one parse per use.

Before the cache, the re-parse cost 12.4, 14.7 and 13.6 µs per call at levels 1, 3 and 9 on 4 KiB payloads with a 110 KiB formatted dictionary. Of that, 9.0 µs was the parse and the rest was the per-call `options.dictionary` byte copy, and the cost was invisible at 19. The per-call dictionary cost is now a cache hit. Pooled ST dictionary states restore a captured post-seed image of tables, filled trees and entropy, keyed on the dictionary identity, the level, the LDM mode and the shape's seeding projection. The per-call dictionary tax is 5.8 µs at level 1 and 16.5 µs at level 3, and the seeding itself takes 3.5 to 16 µs at every level. The residuals are the dictionary-row parse at 9 and the lazy tree fill at 13 and 19. See [encoding](perf/encoding.md).

The cache key's exact-shape slot is replaced by the seeding's own decision projection, `dict_seed_key`, which covers the resolved params, `dict_row` and `LitStreams`. Payload sizes inside one projection class share the image, so a repeated-dictionary workload with varying record sizes stays on the hit path. A loop over varying sizes in [4K, 5K) reads 20, 32, 218, 904 and 959 µs at levels 1, 3, 9, 13 and 19, with every output byte identical to the uncached path.

### 19. Dictionary-frame parse residuals

The seed-image cache eagerly fills the tree families over the dictionary content. Opt rows use `update_tree` with the ultra rows' frame-start statistics parse replayed first, and the replay shapes `opt_state` so the frame skips its own `initStats`. Btlazy rows use the DUBT `fill_range` with the deferred heads clear run eagerly. The tables and the opt coordinate origin are canonicalized to zero beforehand, so the image is a pure function of the cache key and captures the filled tree. A restore is one sequential table copy instead of the first block's random-access fill. The per-call dictionary tax on 512 calls of 4 KiB over the 52 KiB fixture dictionary is 0.58 ms at level 13, 0.57 ms at 15 and -0.15 ms at 19, where the dictionary frame outruns the plain one. The numbers and the fill and copy cost model are in [encoding](perf/encoding.md).

The fill stays byte-neutral by construction. Three exceptions are deliberate. Opt rows with dictionary content past the stock search domain keep the lazy fill, because the frame's LDM alphabet gate parks LDM at the first block and moves the fill floor. Btlazy rows on small-source spans keep it, because the tree hash width is screened over the dictionary plus the block. An ultra dictionary frame whose first block is 8 bytes or less parses differently, because the lazy flow skips its seed parse there while the eager replay has already shaped the prices. That frame still has a legal parse.

The mid-band dictionary row closed the opt rows' 32-64 KiB tail on 2026-10-30: levels 16-17 follow libzstd's srcSize columns onto the btultra parser knobs on a known payload inside the armed span, see [encoding](perf/encoding.md). Level 9 keeps a tax of about 54 µs per call from the dictionary-row x8 chain walk through the dictionary's dense twin field. The cost is candidate density, against libzstd's 40 µs, and it is likely not tractable. The 13 and 15 residue in the 4 KiB loop is now parse-side: the swapped small-dictionary row's dense parse plus the 4096-position tail fill. MT job zero keeps the plain per-call seeding by verdict, because dict-MT payloads are multi-job inputs where the fill is noise. A 32 MiB payload at 19 amortizes the 6 ms fill to under 0.3%, while the cross-thread `DICT_KIT` would carry four stock-row images, about 320 MiB at 19, for that gain.

The projection key lands the varying-size half. Size changes inside a projection class no longer re-seed, and the cache-carving and threshold ladder is gate-pinned by `dict_seed_key_mirror_sweep`, so a new length read in the seeding fails the sweep instead of corrupting restores.

### 23. Fuzz campaign for window-adoption edges

`smoke.sh` keeps the libFuzzer default `max_len` of 4096. The long-payload campaign uses `-max_len=300000 -len_control=20`. Its first pass ran 2026-10-24 as two 25-minute runs with eight ASAN workers over encode and encode_stream, and found no crashes. The corpora grew 2x to 6x and were re-merged. The remaining limit is that evolved inputs carry no beyond-window redundancy. Pre-campaign seeds are all 4096 B or smaller, and coverage-guided mutation rarely builds multi-KiB-period structure, so the window-adoption edges that a dict-seeded corpus would reach stay cold. ### Item 28

The reach probe's donated prefix encodes the first PROBE_SPAN bytes, so job zero must end at or past the span for the continuation to own its own bytes. ### Item 29

The R86 consolidation belt caught one degenerate stream dump: the pre-round build emitted `text.raw.stream.l9.zst` at 4,662,041 bytes where every neighboring run emits 87,238, a ratio collapse to 7.2 on the balanced tier that no round change touches. The frame itself is legal and decodes to the exact input. Three reruns on the same binary, the same corpus and the same flags, plus two on the post-round build, all reproduce the 87,238 B frame, so the degenerate emission is a rare concurrent-path event inside the parallel dump pool, either a race in the stream core's shared state or a cross-cell parameter leak between pool slots. The dump differential is the round gate for byte-exactness, so a one-in-many degenerate cell can silently flip a verdict; until the cause lands, a differing single stream cell is re-emitted before it is read as a regression.

### Item 8

Every decode dispatch site is BMI2-gated: the fused flat executor, the two-pass sequence decode and the MT segment decode. AVX2 entered silicon together with BMI2 at Haswell in 2013 and Zen in 2017, and the two have never been separated since, while AVX2 has no scalar variable-shift form, so the vector batching class is falsified. Every AVX2-capable CPU already runs the top decode tier, and an intermediate AVX2 tier has no selectable CPU, because the scalar fallback's population of pre-Haswell and pre-Zen machines has no AVX2 either. The BMI2 tier is forced through `ZSTDX_DEC_SIMD_TIER=scalar|bmi2`, where unset means detect. The scalar fallback pays json.zst3 and json.zst19 -6.7% and -7.3%, dll100.zst3 and dll100.zst9 -5.6% and -6.3%, and skewed.zst3 -3.9% stream wall. json.zst3 shows +18.0% Ir at IPC 3.83 against 3.49, which is pure variable-shift volume, while text.zst3, text.zst19 and skewed.zst19 stay within -1.9% and -0.4%. The tier's codegen is shlx, shrx and bzhi only, with no pdep or pext, so the Zen1 and Zen2 microcoded-BMI2 hazard does not apply. Explicit SSE2 tiers stay out of scope because SSE2 is baseline and the ring copy's 16 B chunks are compile-time `u128`.

On the encode side, AVX2 kernels landed for the uniform-4 pack and the 16-symbol-or-smaller alphabet histogram. skewed.fastest forced to AVX2 reads -18.2% Ir and +29% wall against forced scalar, forced through `ZSTDX_SIMD_FORCE=avx2|scalar`, where unset means detect. Every other cell measured no scalar gap, so no further AVX2 kernels are planned, and the tiers exist for AVX2-only machines. The default AVX-512 path was re-measured flat after landing, at ±0.1% Ir and flat wall. The nested `#[target_feature]` plus non-always `#[inline]` pattern is verified, and the remaining risk is codegen interference between the N instantiations.
