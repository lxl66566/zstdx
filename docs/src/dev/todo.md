## Item notes

### 1. MT decode scaling

Per-job content-adaptive D is the remaining structural idea. Pieces fully unchain only at D at or above the window, where crossing reads drop to zero. Between that point and the piece size, floor(D/J) pieces chain back through overlap. Below one piece the job is serial-chain. Ratio demands a D under the shape's dominant match period, and text-9 has a period of 800 KiB against a 4 MiB window, so no D satisfies it. Unpledged streams stay excluded because the growing grid's tail split depends on the encoder's worker count.

The landed walls cover the json mt8 verdict and execute stages, and they are memory-bound floors. No wall lever remains short of overlapping execute with the verdict, which is a falsified class. Details are in [mt-stream](perf/mt-stream.md) and [negative](negative/mt-stream.md).

The 2026-11-02 libzstd-shaped ST loop moved the bar: on that build json.zst3 read 1983 MiB/s single-thread against 1989, 2175 and 2231 at mt4, mt8 and mt16, skewed.zst9 read 962 against about 770 at every worker count, and dll100.zst3 read 1604 against 2292 at mt16. A temporary stage-timing probe put the wall in serial stage-B execution on every shape, which ran at about the single-thread decode rate, so R90 (2026-11-02) ported the ST loop's structure into the staged executor: repcodes resolve in locals off the wire value, both wildcopy bounds fold into one end compare, one combined check sends every edge case to an out-of-line exact path, and literals copy unconditionally in 16-byte chunks behind a 16-byte zero tail appended to the staged literals. Execute time per segment dropped to 43 percent on skewed.zst9, 65 on json.zst3 and 70 on dll100.zst3, and one locked before/after pair moved json.zst3 mt16 from 2197 to 3222 MiB/s, dll100.zst3 from 2291 to 3086 and skewed.zst9 from 776 to 1043, the last above its 982 single-thread control for the first time.

After the port, json and dll100 split their walls between the memory-bound stage-B execute, which reads every staged literal and sequence while writing the whole output, and stage-A production behind it. Skewed.zst9 carries only the two 16 MiB restart points its encoder emitted, so the first segment's staging latency dominates its wall and stage A's per-worker rate holds every restart-poor frame near single-thread speed; with the body-widening falsified, the decoder-side lever for such frames is spent.

R93 (2026-11-03) falsified the literals half of that split and re-pointed the wall. Stage-A direct literal placement lost 33 to 44 percent on the very cells it targeted, because the executor's unconditional literal copy doubles as the wildcopy repair of the previous match's overshoot and placement breaks that pairing. MT in-dst zone staging proved memory-safe through header-bound anchors yet lost 2 to 3 percent: stage-A stores leave dirty lines in the workers' private caches, so stage B pays cross-core ownership transfers instead of fills, and json's wall reads sequence-dominated at 0.63 literals per sequence. The json and dll100 walls are sequence-read plus aggregate-bandwidth bound, and the staged Sequence vector is the open lever. Both records are in [negative](negative/decoding.md).

R94 (2026-11-04) validated that premise with a padding probe. A throwaway build padding the staged element to 24 B per sequence, byte-identical under mtcheck, cost every mt cell 7.7 to 14.4 percent wall in two locked interleaved rounds against base 91191a7d, with the single-thread controls flat on json and dll100: the staged Sequence vector is causal on the mt walls. The same pass built the traffic model, which puts the sequence stream at 42, 37, 67 and 36 percent of stage B's round-trip on json.zst3, json.zst9, skewed.zst9 and dll100.zst3, and the measured losses imply the bandwidth-bound stage-B share of the mt wall at 0.21 to 0.40 on json and dll100 and 0.12 to 0.15 on restart-poor skewed. The packing lever that pricing pointed at was measured and closed: an 8 B record cannot hold the staged triple, because the staged offset needs the whole u32 under position-bounded offset validity, and the 9 B record that fits lost 2.4 to 6.2 percent on every mt cell, since the per-sequence unpack on the serial executor costs more than the byte cut buys and the pack taxes stage A's staging rate, which owns skewed's wall. The representation side of the staged vector is closed, and the wall attribution now carries the quantified stage-B bandwidth share and the executor's ALU floor. The 2026-11-04 reopen of the packing lever as a batched SIMD expansion, the exact form the falsification named, lost 9 to 15 percent on every mt cell across three shapes at +3.8 percent instructions, so the bandwidth share never pays for the pack-and-expand cycle efficiency. Tail prefetch of the next segment's staging measured flat and is falsified in [negative](negative/decoding.md). The traffic tables are in [decoding](perf/decoding.md), and both packing records are in [negative](negative/decoding.md).

#### The R89 map

Two accepted proposals, one rejection and one falsification, each grounded in the R88 decomposition. The accepted ones move bytes by design, so none may claim the dump gate; each carries its own ratio and wall gates.

Sorted strip fill for the btlazy rows, falsified in R91. Premise: the whale jobs' whole excess over the non-whale floor (jobs 3-5 at 2.1-2.6 s against job 2's 1.18 s) is the first-search batch sort of the O(1) threaded strip at 771 B per call, while the opt rows already fill sorted in-tree at 8 MiB/s with 10 B compares. Mechanism: run the btlazy strip prefill through the finder's sorted insert (the `insert_bt1` descent with its commonLength carry and its >384 B match skip) instead of the O(1) chain fill, so no unsorted backlog ever reaches a search. Expected gain: the fill costs about 0.4-0.5 s per 4 MiB strip on non-twin content but collapses on twins through the skip, and the whales lose the 1.2-1.4 s batch-sort tail, for a dll100 best wall bound near 1.5-1.9 s against today's 2.56 s. Risk: the sorted tree's orphan set differs from the budgeted batch sort's, so bytes move on both sides of the ledger. Gates: dll100/dll32 best dumps for size within +0.3%, the 120-cell sweep, and a locked wall A/B that must show the whale spread narrowing. Falsified if the deterministic fill cost on json-class strips eats the whale saving or any dll cell loses more than 0.3%. It fired on 2026-11-02: the sizes moved the right way (dll100 best bulk-mt8 20,515,629 to 20,506,118 B, dll32 4,242,823 to 4,242,440, corpus best-tier mt cells inside +146 B) while every wall collapsed. The interleaved clean A/B read dll100 best bulk-mt8 1895-1935 to 3898-5251 ms, its pledged stream cell 1932-1963 to 3896-3949 ms, dll32 best bulk-mt8 367-465 to 717-729 ms, and the json clause fired at 1140 to 2280 ms with the fill at 44-49% of job time. The eager insert pays the descent budget at every strip position against the nearest twin the head chain offers, about a kilobyte compared per filled byte on dll100, where the batch sort pays per search, and the fill serializes ahead of every parse from t=0, widening the whale timeline from 0.97-2.51 to 2.59-5.55 s per job. The A/B and the mechanism are in [negative](negative/mt-stream.md).

### Item 8

Every decode dispatch site is BMI2-gated: the fused flat executor, the two-pass sequence decode and the MT segment decode.
